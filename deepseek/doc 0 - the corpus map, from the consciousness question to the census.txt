## Part 1. Act I — the question, and what the audits settled (docs 1–8)

The corpus began as a critique. The source document (S) was an LLM's philosophical essay arguing that meta-theoretical constraints — elegance, parsimony, explanatory scope — could deductively refute reductive materialism about consciousness; the registrar asked for it to be found, fetched, and audited. Doc 1 delivered the audit: nine internal inconsistencies, eleven logical flaws, twelve gaps, and the two findings that structured everything after — Principle B (∃x Exp(x)), the essay's ownload-bearing existential claim, directly contradicts its advertised success criterion ("no consciousness premise"), and the "deductive refutation" is vacuous, refuting a stipulated zombie-definition no one holds. Doc 2 adjudicated the audit against a second, independent audit (D): ten points of convergence settled the diagnosis (S overclaims), all seven genuine contradictions between the audits resolved in favor of the first — largely on D's own terms, with D's proposed repairs contradicting D's own diagnoses in six places — and one concession filed: an abductive case for panexperientialism could in principle be made, though neither S nor D's reconstruction makes it.

Docs 3–4 then asked what could be salvaged and what could be proven. The repaired argument (doc 3) survives both audits only as a debt-carrying conditional research program — it changes no minds, it clarifies what believers bought — and it tied, on those terms, with neutral monism. The necessity analysis (doc 4) closed the proof question in both directions: proto-consciousness necessity is provable only from premises that presuppose it (or from a necessary divine mind), the "at least some" weakening is an illusion (the Collapse Lemma), the negation is equally unprovable, and the whole question is the mind-body problem in modal costume. Doc 5 steelmanned the strongest remaining route — the theistic necessary mind — and found it the shortest contested path to R1 (why-anything, math-knowledge, contingency of finite minds, no combination problem for matter), not a proof, its fate bound to Platonism-versus-conceptualism and emergentism-versus-panprotopsychism, in decisive rivalry with the computational route.

Doc 6 is the exchange's hinge: the Chu bridge question — can physics and computation be proven one self-dual reality (the DeepSeek program quoted in this session's opening)? The verdict was a layered no: T1 (theorem-deep entanglement) is real and the strongest yes available; T2 (the full unity) is undecided under real counter-theorems; T3 (the schema) is the best schema, not a theorem; the "only" in "the only structural language" is refuted; and the bridge premise carries the span. The corpus named the missing span — the phenomenality of the inner pole — and minted the exchange's final theorem: **theorems do not introduce what their premises do not contain.** Doc 7 took the decisive battleground the bridge verdict left open — can syntax carry semantics? — and found it theorem-undecidable (the Reciprocity Fork is the Bridge Constraint's instance; Kripkenstein symmetry blocks the cheap humanist victory) but, for the first time in the exchange, empirically live: the asymmetry of tractability, the verification squeeze, and Othello-GPT as the first clean originality witness. Content and phenomenality were kept rigorously separate — sequential wars. Doc 8 evaluated the registrar's own provocation (original content by dumb luck, meaningful only to an outside reader with aboutness) against another AI's response: the wedge judged the right instrument, the response substantially correct with two genuine advances (selection redemption; D3′ — original content from organization) and six instructive defects, the deepest a symmetry violation.

**What Act I settled, in one paragraph.** No proof exists on any side of the original question; the proofs that appear to exist fail by smuggling (the Collapse Lemma and the Bridge Constraint are the same discipline stated twice); the theistic and panexperientialist routes are priced, conditional, and rivaled; and exactly one question in the entire exchange is decidable by evidence rather than argument — whether organization alone can produce original content — and it is decidable only by building instruments and running them.

## Part 7. The methodology the corpus invented

The corpus's durable product is not any single verdict; it is the discipline under which the verdicts were produced. Six practices, minted in use:

**Pre-registration before compute.** Every battery's forks are fixed in the script's header before the run (BT1–BT6 in doc 82 is the current exemplar), and the forks' limbs are named with their identity readings before any world runs. Nothing was ever run first and interpreted after.

One more reflexive fact deserves filing. What the corpus contains, after eighty-two documents, is a conditional scaffold where the arguments stood, a discipline under which verdicts were produced, and one measured world with its laws and its named open cells. What it does not contain is the answer to the question it was asked.
