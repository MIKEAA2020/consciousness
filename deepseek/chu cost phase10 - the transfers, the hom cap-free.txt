## Part 1. Lemma 69 — the hom is cap-free

**Standing notation (docs 24, 27, 66).** ChuCost^[0,K] is the full subcategory of ChuCost on the [0, K]-valued pairings; the capped tensor ⊗_⊕ carries doc 24's Def 5 carriers with the pairing u ⊕ r := min(u + r, K); the difference hom ⊸_⊖ carries doc 27's Def 15 carriers with the pairing s ⊖ r := max(s − r, 0); Proposition 67 proved the adjunction, the unit (1, 1, 0), the dualizer (1, 1, K), and the de Morgan identity in this presentation. **Lemma 69 (the hom is cap-free) [Derived here].** For [0, K]-valued A and B, the object A ⊸_⊖ B of ChuCost^[0,K] and the object A ⊸ B of ChuCost are the same triple: the same carriers (X^Y × B^A, A × Y — Def 15 in both constructions) and the same pairing, since s ∸ r and max(s − r, 0) are the same function. Consequently: (i) the hom-sets coincide — ChuCost^[0,K](A, B) = ChuCost(A, B), because the (L) condition reads only A's and B's pairings; (ii) every statement about internal homs, hom-sets, or hom-dimensions that the corpus proved additively, restricted to [0, K]-valued objects, is a statement about the capped home unchanged — in particular Definition 62(iii)'s probe-dimensionality (affdim ChuCost(C, X) = f(δC, δX)) reads the same numbers in both presentations; and (iii) the equality core between [0, K]-valued objects (the morphisms satisfying (L) with equality) is presentation-independent. *Proof.* The pairing identity is doc 27's own definition read at [0, K]-valued arguments; the carriers are shared by construction (doc 66's Prop 67 took Def 15's carriers verbatim); (i)–(iii) are immediate. ∎

The lemma is one line, and it is load-bearing twice. First, it is the transfer engine for Theorem Y and Corollary Y.1: the capped home's closed structure differs from the additive one at exactly one point — the tensor's pairing — so any proof whose only tensor-dependence is the adjunction's condition equivalence (which Proposition 67 rebuilt for the capped pairing) transfers, and any proof that never touches the tensor transfers for free. ## Part 3. The Born world at K = 1 — well-posed, and unclosed

**Lemma 70 (the Born world is not ⊗_⊕-closed) [Derived here; the Born pairing is doc 42's Def 48].** Let F_phys(p, A) = (D(A), E(A), r_A) with r_A(ρ, e) = 1 − tr(eρ) be the Born pairings, and let B₁, B₂ be matrix algebras with d_{B_i} ≥ 2. Then F_phys(B₁) ⊗_⊕ F_phys(B₂) is not isomorphic to F_phys(B) for any matrix algebra B. *Proof.* The capped tensor's system carrier is E(B₂)^{D(B₁)} × E(B₁)^{D(B₂)}. For a matrix algebra of dimension d, the effect space E(B) is a closed convex subset of a d²-dimensional real vector space, so |E(B)| = 𝔠; likewise |D(B)| = 𝔠. Hence |E(B₂)^{D(B₁)}| = 𝔠^𝔠 = 2^𝔠 > 𝔠 = |E(B)| for every B (Cantor), and isomorphisms in ChuCost are carrier bijections preserving pairings exactly. ∎

The re-reading this buys is the cell's content. Additively, the [0, 1] world's tensor-closure question never reached the mathematics: the additive tensor of two Born pairings takes values up to 2 — doc 27's Lemma 21 overflow, quantified on the quantum side as doc 42's double billing — so the tensor left the [0, 1]-valued world by value overflow before any closure question could be posed, and the fragment questions lived only in the unbounded scale where the Born objects sit among strangers. Under the cap, the values stay: the tensor of two Born pairings is an honest object of ChuCost^[0,1], and the closure question is well-posed for the first time. The answer is no — and the no is a different no: not the overflow's (the values escape) but the exponential's (the carriers escape). The system carrier a function space from densities to effects is not any matrix algebra's effect space, by the most elementary of cardinalities, and the cap — a repair on values — was never going to touch it. The Born world's tensor-closure failure is thus seen to be robust across presentations for a structural reason: the Dialectica tensor populates its observer side by exponentiation, and doc 42's sentence ("the Dialectica tensor's observer carrier is a function space, and no physical product populates one") acquires its converse-clause: no capped arithmetic populates one either.

**Proposition 71 (the Born pairings' capped readings) [Derived here, except where labeled].** (i) At K = 1, the capped tensor composes Born costs by c₁ ⊕ c₂ = min(c₁ + c₂, 1); on probabilities (p_i = 1 − c_i) this is 1 − min(c₁ + c₂, 1) = max(p₁ + p₂ − 1, 0), the Łukasiewicz t-norm. (ii) Doc 42's lax monoidal structure — the product-state forward map, the conditioned-effect backward — survives the cap: its condition reads 1 − p ≤ min(2(1 − p), 1), true since 1 − p is bounded by both arguments; the slack is min(2(1 − p), 1) − (1 − p) = min(1 − p, p). (iii) The equality core between Born pairings, the completely positive slice, and the transpose witness (Prop 51) are unchanged in the capped home, by Lemma 69(iii): the morphism-sets are the same, so the first morphism the capped duality meets that no channel induces is still the transpose. (iv) The capped dualizer's blindness stands as doc 66 reallocated it (Lemma 68): (1, 1, 1) sees every density matrix — its probe set at a Born pairing is the whole system carrier — and reads nothing of the couplings. *Proof.* (i) is the displayed arithmetic; (ii) is the two bounds; (iii) and (iv) are Lemma 69 and doc 66's Lemma 68 restated at K = 1. ∎ **[Interpretation]** The Fréchet reading, labeled: max(p₁ + p₂ − 1, 0) is the lower Fréchet bound on the joint probability of two events — the probability they have under maximal anticorrelation. At K = 1, the capped tensor's cost bookkeeping composes the way anticorrelated events compose: each side's provision counted against the other's demand with nothing left over, ever. Doc 62's ledger already noted the MV convergence (the closure-equivalence's home is the Lindley currency the instruments have been counting since the deadband); Proposition 71(i) adds the tensor-side sentence: the capped composition of Born costs is the Łukasiewicz arithmetic of the Born probabilities themselves.
