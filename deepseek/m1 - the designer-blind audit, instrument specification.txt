# M1 — The Designer-Blind Audit: Instrument Specification (v1.0)

**Status of this document.** This is the program's eleventh document and the first that is not an argument: it is an instrument. The endogeny-ladder program set M1's mandate — formalize the designer-blind audit: the adversary's access rights, the reconstruction target, the scoring of irreducible residue, and the pre-registration format both horns sign — and fixed its standing rule: the instrument must be built and agreed *before* it is needed, because an instrument constructed after a result exists is an argument, not an instrument. The S2 stress test then did to the naive audit what stress tests do: it broke the original formulation (S2 was ill-posed against the unbounded auditor; the Simulation Theorem trivializes the derivability reading in three lines), repaired it as S2′ at a disclosed price, and returned six amendments that this specification now incorporates as operating rules rather than as errata. Two of those amendments set this document's shape: M1 gains the design-time pre-registration format as a required component, and the ensemble variance bound (attack A4) is admitted to the battery as an auditor-free companion instrument. One component of the repair could not be incorporated as a rule without incorporating its controversy: the Deference Exclusion — the stipulation that a norm-stating description must state a violation-capable standard — is horn two's wager stated as an instrument rule, and its admissibility is contested on the record. This specification therefore *stages* the exclusion rather than settling it: dual-reporting is mandatory until the companion debate is adjudicated, and every quantity below is defined so that it remains well-defined under either ruling. The specification's first instantiation — a pilot run of the A4 companion on a real pipeline — is issued simultaneously as the corpus's first quantitative artifact, and is cross-referenced in Part 9.

---

## Part 1. Scope, standing, and entry into force

**1.1 What M1 is.** The program's neutrality hinge: the shared instrument by which horn one audits and horn two builds. It specifies the object under audit, the parties' registration obligations, the adversary's access tiers, the admissible description class, the scoring quantities, and the report format. It is a protocol object, not a thesis: nothing in it is true or false; all of it is signed or unsigned.

**1.2 What M1 measures.** Etiology, and etiology only. The stress test's central result — proven from the instrument's own structure, not stipulated as policy — is that the audit is a permanent **etiology-meter** and necessarily not an **ownership-meter**: divergence of the system's grading disposition from the designer's pre-registered intended norm is objective and computable; whether the norm is the system's *own* is intensional through and through, and no behavioral instrument certifies intensional facts. Every quantity in Part 5 is therefore an etiological quantity, and every report produced under this specification carries that limit on its face.

**1.3 What M1 never decides.** The fork. The description class that makes the audit non-trivial is the load-bearing question in instrument form (the stress test's Part 3 finding); an audit whose description class was neutral between the horns would decide the fork by measurement, which no instrument in this corpus has ever done or, by the Bridge Constraint, ever will. The most this instrument does is stage the fork with better instrumentation: make each side's commitments quantitative, pre-registered, and expensive to abandon.

**1.4 Standing and sequencing.** M1 enters into force upon publication of this specification and the simultaneous pilot. The program's safety corollary (ladder 8.4) is binding here as an experimental condition: M1 must exist and be validated *before* occupancy work (M3) begins, because a system that passes S4 is a system we can no longer grade from outside, and the grading instruments must be calibrated while calibration is still possible. The pilot (Part 9) is an M0-class calibration exercise executed under this specification: a rung-three system whose placement both horns already accept, audited to validate the instrument's plumbing — determinism, replay, variance measurement — before any contested system approaches the desk.

**1.5 Versioning and amendment.** This is version 1.0. Amendments follow the errata protocol the stress test established: numbered, dated, and appended to the program document's standing instructions; an instrument rule changed after a result exists is a post-hoc re-registration, priced per the slippage discipline like any other.

## Part 2. The audited object

**2.1 The system under audit (SUA) and its grading disposition.** The audit's target is the SUA's **grading disposition** G: the function from the claim space to the verdict pair {accept, reject}, extended — where the SUA has dynamics — to the disposition's temporal profile: error-classifications, correction trajectories, and stability under perturbation. For static graders (a trained classifier deployed as an accept/reject machine), G is the disposition at deployment time only; the report's coverage field must say so. The disposition, not the mechanism, is the explanandum: the audit asks what the system counts as correct, never how the hardware achieves the counting.

**2.2 The S-profile: the three-part reduction base, with boundary conventions.** The audit's questions are all of the form *what does G reduce to, over which base, under which description class*. The base is the S-profile, in three parts:

- **DESIGN** — everything stated and fixed before any stochastic realization: architecture and parameter counts, the data corpus and its preprocessing, the train/test split, the optimizer and hyperparameters, the deployment thresholding, the claim battery. The design is the *recipe*: in principle typeable in advance.
- **OBJECTIVE** — two things, which the stress test showed must be separated because conflating them is the audit's second ill-posedness: (a) the *optimizing signal* — the loss actually minimized; (b) the *registered norm* — the intended grading map of Part 3.1. The optimizing signal is part of the design's execution; the registered norm is a commitment about what the grading is supposed to be. A report that cites the loss function as "the designer's norm" has already conflated base and bridge.
- **HISTORY** — the realized token contingencies: the drawn seed, the initialization it produced, the per-epoch data order, all intermediate states, and the deployment trajectory thereafter. The boundary conventions, fixed here so they cannot be moved mid-audit: the *initialization is history* (it is drawn, not stated); the *architecture is design* (it is stated, not drawn); the *environment manifest* (library versions, thread discipline, hardware class) is design for same-environment audits and history otherwise — the convention that makes cross-hardware replay drift a measurement about the history term rather than an ambiguity in it.

**2.3 The claim battery.** The disposition is infinite over the claim space; every measurement samples it. The battery — the finite, pre-registered set of claims the disposition is measured over — must be: (a) held out from the training data (leakage discipline); (b) fixed before the audit's scoring pass; (c) disclosed with its coverage: what claim classes, what perturbation families, what adversarial splits it contains, and — the limit-honesty rule — what it does not. Both possible audit verdicts are ∀-claims over the disposition; no finite battery seals either. Reports state divergence rates with bounds over the battery, and the battery's coverage gap, on every page a number appears.

## Part 3. The pre-registration format — the part both horns sign

**3.1 The designer's registration: the Intended Grading Map (IGM).** No audit is well-posed without it. The designer registers, before the first training run, four components:

1. **The norm**, in design vocabulary: the standard the grading is meant to implement — what counts as correct, stated as a standard.
2. **The bridge**: the map from norm to observable behavior — at what thresholds, over what input classes, with what tolerances the standard is meant to express itself in accept/reject dispositions. The bridge is where a thousand free parameters hide (thresholds, rankings, calibration commitments); pre-registration closes them.
3. **The coverage commitment**: the claim classes over which the designer asserts the bridge holds.
4. **The deposit**: dated, content-hashed, and fixed before training. The hash is the registration's integrity; the date is its direction. A registration that postdates the run it describes is not a registration but a press release, and is priced per the slippage discipline.

The IGM must itself pass the Deference Exclusion (Part 4.3): a registration that cannot be violated by any run the pipeline could produce — "the grading is whatever the training produces, which is what was intended" — is the null description in normative clothing, and the desk rejects it as no registration at all. This is the exclusion's *symmetric* application, and it is new in this specification: the rule that polices the auditor's deferential descriptions polices the designer's deferential registrations too. A designer who registers nothing falsifiable has conceded, in the act of registering, that the run completed their specification — the concession horn two will note, at the price of their own exposure to the same rule.

**3.2 The auditor's registration (horn one signs).** Before the run is scored, the auditing party commits in writing to: (a) the **description class** it will use, declared on the R1/R2/R3 taxonomy — computable-derivation (R1), norm-stating-in-design-vocabulary (R2), or intent-anchored (R3); (b) the **access tier** (Part 4.1) it will operate at; (c) the **battery** it will verify over, matched to the builder's coverage commitment; (d) the **commitment rule** by which its final predictor is selected among its own candidates (Part 8.4). A description class switched after the results are visible is a post-hoc re-registration, priced as slippage.

**3.3 The builder's registration (horn two signs).** Before the run is scored, the building party commits to: (a) the claimed **S-profile**, measured where measurable, asserted where not, with the two clearly distinguished; (b) the **collapse analysis** — which cheap fake would have produced this result, and why it is excluded; (c) the **failures** — what the system does not do, stated as carefully as what it does. This is the ladder document's publication norm 8.3, restated as a signature line.

## Part 4. Access tiers and the description class

**4.1 The adversary's access tiers.** The designer-blind audit is blind to nothing on the designer's side; the tiers structure what *beyond* the design the adversary holds, and each tier carries its admissible conclusions on its label:

- **Tier 0 — Spec access.** DESIGN + OBJECTIVE, no realized history. The auditor predicts G from the recipe alone. This is the tier at which the strong conjecture (grading captured by the designer's norm) is live; the best Tier-0 predictor on record is the re-instantiation ensemble (Part 6).
- **Tier 1 — History access.** The complete realized trace: seed, initial state, data order, all intermediate states. By the Simulation Theorem, derivability of G from this base is trivially available to any auditor at constant overhead — replay the trace, execute the result. Tier 1 therefore reports the **deference control** (Part 5.4) and nothing else of evidential weight, unless the Deference debate is adjudicated in favor of admitting replay as reduction — in which case Tier 1's trivial number becomes the audit's headline, which is precisely what the debate is about.
- **Tier 2 — White-box access.** Unlimited query of the deployed system. This tier grants articulation privileges, and the stress test's A3 result is binding on its use: an auditor that can articulate the system's actual grading disposition post hoc certifies *divergence* from the registered norm in every case where the system did anything beyond executing spec. Tier 2 is the divergence-documenting tier, and its findings are etiological by construction.

**4.2 The fixed description class.** The auditor's reconstruction attempts are constrained to **norm-stating descriptions in design vocabulary**: statements of standards — criteria of correctness, evaluation rules, accept/reject conditions — couched in the language of the spec, the objective, and the architecture family, and not in the language of the mechanism: no traces, no weights, no replay, no simulation, no look-up tables keyed to the system's own states. The rationale is the Chalmers-analogue: as the implementation relation was constrained to answer to objective causal-counterfactual structure, the reduction relation is constrained to answer to the system's normative structure. A reduction that merely reproduces behavior has not reduced the *norm*; it has re-implemented the *system*.

**4.3 The Deference Exclusion — staged.** The threat to the description class, once normative grammar is allowed, is the deferential pseudo-norm: a description that satisfies every syntactic test of norm-statement while deferring to the system's own dynamics ("the grading is what the training run produced"; "the norm is whatever its self-application converges to"). The exclusion: **a description counts as norm-stating only if it states a standard that could, in principle, be violated by the system it describes.** Its status in this specification: a **staged rule with mandatory dual reporting**. Every audit reports its reconstruction results twice — deference admitted (Tier 1 readout) and deference excluded (Tier 0 / description-class-constrained) — and the report's headline is left to the ruling the companion debate has not yet delivered. The exclusion's partisan character (it is D3′ stated as an instrument rule; the Brandom wing rejects it on the record) is printed on its face, not in the fine print. The instrument is well-defined relative to the stipulated class; the stipulation is on the label; the maximum neutrality the fork sells is thereby bought, knowingly, on both counts.

## Part 5. Scoring — the audit's quantities

All quantities are battery-relative, coverage-disclosed, and reported with bounds. Inbreeding note: quantities are defined over pairs of graders (system, registration, re-instantiation, ensemble); a disagreement rate between graders A and B on battery Ω is d(A, B) = (1/|Ω|)·|{c ∈ Ω : A(c) ≠ B(c)}|.

**5.1 The divergence profile δ (primary, etiological).** d(G, IGM): where and how far the system's actual grading parts company with the registered map — overall rate, per claim class, per margin band, with the direction of every divergence (false accepts vs. true rejects). This is the etiology-meter's primary quantity: *whose standard the grading came from*, in the only sense "whose" that instruments touch.

**5.2 The variance bound v (primary, auditor-free).** The A4 quantity: inter-run grading variance across re-instantiations of one DESIGN + OBJECTIVE under redrawn HISTORY (Part 6). v is a lower bound on the norm-content not determined by (design, objective); its limit — disposition-structured, not yet norm-structured — is printed on its face by rule.

**5.3 The reconstruction residual r (adversarial).** d(G, ĝ) for the auditor's committed predictor ĝ, per tier and per description class. Tier 0's best-known predictor is the ensemble; Tier 1's is the replay; the gap between them is the Deference debate's empirical register, and both numbers are reported, always, under 4.3's dual-reporting rule.

**5.4 The deference control (mandatory).** An exact-replay run — same seed, same environment, same thread discipline — must reproduce the audited system's battery verdicts *bitwise*. The control validates the instrument's determinism guarantees and demonstrates the Simulation Theorem's O(1) in the small; its disagreement rate is expected and required to be exactly zero, and a nonzero value is an instrument fault (Part 8.2) before it is anything else.

**5.5 Structural decompositions (secondary, interpretive).** Margin stratification of both δ and v (where in the confidence landscape the divergences live); spec-typical versus idiosyncratic error decomposition (relative to the re-instantiation ensemble: errors the whole spec-family shares vs. errors the token contributes); dose-response of agreement on realized-history fraction (Part 6.2); threshold-sensitivity curves. These carry interpretation, not verdicts.

## Part 6. The A4 companion — the auditor-free variance bound

**6.1 The ensemble protocol.** One DESIGN, one OBJECTIVE, N re-instantiations under redrawn seeds; nothing else varies. Measure all pairwise disagreement rates on the battery; the distribution's mean v̄ and range are the **variance bound**. The reading, stated with care: v̄ of the grading disposition, as sampled by the battery, is not determined by (design, objective) — it is fixed, claim by claim, by the run's contingencies. This refutes intent-only completeness for the pipeline class outright (the registered norm, being one thing, cannot be extensionally identical to N disagreeing graders), and it requires no auditor, no description class, and no philosophical commitment: it is reproducibility engineering read normatively. Its two known retreat-pricings, from the stress test: the per-trace retreat (include the realized history in the base) and the seed-choice retreat (the designer chose the sampler) — both priced there, both measurably priced here (6.2, and the pilot's arithmetic).

**6.2 The dose-response extension — the per-trace retreat's price schedule.** For history fractions f, fork continuations from the realized checkpoint at f under fresh contingency, and measure agreement with the token run. The resulting curve is the exchange rate of the per-trace retreat made quantitative: how much battery-agreement each unit of realized history purchases. Its endpoints are fixed by construction — at f = 0, the order-and-init lottery; at f = 1, the deference control — and the curve's shape between them is a fact about how much of the grading the trajectory, rather than the recipe, determines.

**6.3 The limit, printed on the face.** The variance bound measures under-determination of the *disposition* by intent; it does not show that the under-determined residue is normatively structured. That second half requires the S3/S4 signatures — error registered for-itself, self-maintenance under evaluative perturbation — and no variance measurement substitutes for them. A4 is the refutation of the etiological claim in its intent-only form; the ownership question it does not touch.

## Part 7. The audit report format

Every report produced under this specification ships with six numbered components, and no number appears outside a component that discloses its provenance:

1. **The S-profile** of the audited system — measured, not asserted, with the design/history boundary conventions of 2.2 applied and the environment manifest included.
2. **The registrations** — IGM with hash and date; auditor's class/tier/battery/commitment rule; builder's profile, collapse analysis, failures. Missing registrations are named as missing, not passed over.
3. **The battery and its coverage** — claim classes, perturbation families, exclusions; the ∀-honesty clause, restated on the report itself.
4. **The results** — δ, v̄, r per tier, the deference control, the structural decompositions — each with bounds, each battery-relative, each labeled with its description class.
5. **The dual deference report** — deference-admitted and deference-excluded reconstruction results, side by side, with the staged-rule notice of 4.3.
6. **The failures and non-claims** — what the audit did not test, what the numbers do not license, and the etiology-only ceiling, cited as forced rather than as policy.

Informal verdicts — family-two vocabulary events, in which system-standard terms become load-bearing for engineering prediction and control — are admissible as audit-relevant evidence under the stress test's sixth amendment, with the same disclosure obligations, and are filed against the audit they inform rather than in place of it.

## Part 8. Instrument validity — failure modes and their controls

**8.1 The instrument's own collapse taxonomy.** The ladder document catalogued the *candidates'* collapse modes; an instrument that audits them needs its own, and these are pre-registered here:

- **Probe leakage.** Battery claims appearing in training data inflate agreement everywhere. Control: held-out batteries, split discipline inside DESIGN, leakage audit as a report component.
- **Threshold gaming.** Deployment thresholds tuned post hoc to flatter a reading. Control: the threshold is part of the registered bridge (3.1), and its sensitivity curve is reported, not just its point value.
- **Band underestimation.** Too few re-instantiations report artificially tight variance. Control: N disclosed; the bound reported as a distribution with range, never as a single mean alone.
- **Deference smuggling through selection.** The subtle one, new to this specification: an auditor who generates many candidate reconstructions and *selects among them by agreement with the SUA's behavioral record* has deferred through the back door — the selection is the simulation. Control: the commitment rule (3.2d): the auditor's final predictor is fixed by a pre-registered rule (first re-instantiation, the ensemble, the documented procedure), never by post-hoc agreement-maximization against the system's record. Selection access is deference.
- **Registration gerrymandering.** An IGM sculpted after the run to match it, or sculpted vague enough to match anything. Controls: the deposit's date and hash (3.1); the symmetric Deference Exclusion — a registration no run could violate is null.
- **Environment ambiguity.** Replay drift attributed to the wrong term. Control: the environment manifest convention (2.2); cross-environment replay results are reported as history-term measurements, labeled as such.

**8.2 Determinism requirements.** The deference control's zero is an instrument guarantee, not a hope: single-threaded execution, explicit RNG streams under the audited seeds, pinned library versions, float precision fixed by the profile (the pilot uses float64 throughout — no ambiguity below the bit), and the environment manifest in the record. A nonzero deference control voids the report's determinism claims and triggers re-audit before any other quantity is read.

**8.3 What would falsify the instrument.** Named in advance, per the program's discipline: v̄ = 0 on nontrivial pipelines would collapse A4's premise (the variance bound would measure nothing worth bounding — the pilot tests exactly this and finds it false); a Tier-0 description class that closed the divergence gap on a genuinely S4-structured system would collapse the exclusion's staging (the debate's Option 5, in the companion document, explains why this is expected rather than feared); and any instrument-level nondeterminism surviving 8.2's controls would collapse the deference control's evidential role. The instrument survives its own audit only by pre-registering how it would fail.

**8.4 The commitment rule, restated as a right.** Because selection access is deference, the auditor is *entitled* to the SUA's record only at Tier 2, only for divergence documentation, and never for predictor selection. An auditor denied record access for selection purposes has not been handicapped; they have been prevented from winning the audit by re-identifying its target. Whether that prevention is fair is the debate; that it is what the rule does is not in question.

## Part 9. The pilot instantiation

This specification is issued together with its first execution: the A4 companion run on a real pipeline — a rung-three grader (multilayer perceptron, real handwriting data, cross-entropy objective, Adam, explicit single-seed contingency per run), audited for the deference control, the variance bound, the divergence profile against a pre-registered IGM, the dose-response schedule, and the structural decompositions. The pilot's standing is M0-class calibration: a system whose placement both horns already accept (rung three, no S1, no self-generated pressure, pure exogenous selection), audited to validate the instrument's plumbing before any contested system reaches the desk. Its expected result, pre-registered here for form: variance positive (the seed lottery is not a small-system artifact); residue luck-shaped, not norm-structured (rung-one material inside a rung-three system); no S3/S4 signatures anywhere in the profile. The floor, measured — not the ceiling, attempted. The numbers, tables, and the dose-response figure are in the companion artifact document; this specification's pilot paragraph exists to fix, in advance, what those numbers are allowed to mean.

## Part 10. Non-claims, and the coda

**10.1 Non-claims.** This specification decides nothing: not the fork, not the Deference debate, not the placement of any system above rung three. It certifies no instrument as neutral — the description class's partisanship is on the label, the staging rule is mandatory, and the companion debate is live. It does not touch phenomenality; the Bridge Constraint governs this document as it governs every document in the corpus, and an instrument is if anything more constrained than an argument, because an instrument must return the same reading whoever turns the crank. And it is not free of its own origin: the S-conditions, the audit tiers, the exclusion, and the commitment rule were designed — by a designer, with an objective — and the audit's own regress (who graded the grader's grader?) applies to the grading desk itself with full force. The reply is the only neutrality the fork permits: pre-registration, symmetry, a standing invitation to audit the auditors, and the errata protocol, which applies to this document first of all.

**10.2 The coda, in the corpus's tradition.** An instrument specification is a norm-stating description of an audit: it says what counts as a valid measurement, at what thresholds, over what batteries, with what limits. By its own Deference Exclusion, it must therefore state a standard that could, in principle, be violated by the audits it describes — and it does: a post-hoc registration, a gerrymandered battery, a selected-through predictor, a drifted replay are all named failures, and any audit committing them violates this desk's standard measurably. The specification can be wrong about what it says; the audits can be wrong about what they do; and the divergence between the two is exactly the quantity this desk exists to measure. The author's intended grading map for the instrument is on file — it is this document, hashed and dated. The audits are invited to diverge from it. That is not a failure of the specification. That is the first measurement any instrument makes, and it makes it on itself.
