# Can Syntax Carry Semantics? — The Decisive Battleground: Computational Intentionality

**Status of this document.** The final and decisive engagement of this exchange. The steelman of the necessary mind showed that Route A stands or falls on A3 — *aboutness is essentially mental*. The Chu analysis showed that the computational filling of reality's inner pole stands or falls on the negation of A3 — *aboutness can be information-theoretic*. These are direct contradictories, and they stake the same slot. This document is the adjudication of that contradiction: **can syntax — formal structure, computation, information processing — carry semantics, carry aboutness, without a mind underneath?**

**Headline answer.** The question splits into four, with four different verdicts: derived semantics — **yes, trivially**; whether physical systems even have determinate syntactic descriptions — **genuinely open, with a real theorem (Putnam's triviality result) still not fully discharged**; whether original content can arise from organization alone — **undecided, but the first battleground question in this entire exchange with an active empirical resolution program, currently squeezing in the compatibilist direction on *content* while leaving *phenomenal accompaniment* exactly where the Bridge Constraint says it must stay**; and whether syntax *alone* carries semantics — **no**, failing for the same reason every "only" and "alone" claim has failed in this corpus. The battleground is decisive precisely because it cannot be won by theorem. But it is being fought — with results.

---

## Part 1. Disambiguation — four questions in one

**D1 (Derived content).** Can a formal system *be assigned* content by an external interpreter? Chess programs, compilers, circuit diagrams — symbols that mean something *to us*.

**D2 (Determinate syntax).** Is there a well-defined fact of the matter about *what* a physical system computes — is "syntax" an intrinsic property of physical systems at all?

**D3 (Original content).** Can the right functional organization produce content that is not borrowed — aboutness that belongs to the system rather than to its users?

**D4 (Sufficiency of syntax alone).** Does syntax, by itself, without grounding, use, teleology, or interpretation, constitute semantics?

These are routinely conflated, and the conflation is where most of the debate's heat and little of its light comes from. One more distinction frames everything: **intentionality ≠ phenomenality**. A state can be *about* something without there being anything it is like to bear it (unconscious beliefs, if such exist, subpersonal representations in vision science — grapple-point detectors that are about edges without any feel of edge). Conversely, on some views, there can be experience without determinate aboutness (pure pain). The question here is about *aboutness*, not *felt character*. Searle's deepest strategic move — and his most contested — is refusing this separation: for him, genuine intentionality essentially involves consciousness. The battleground's shape depends on whether that refusal is a discovery or a charter; hold the question open until Part 5.

The ancestor of the whole war is Brentano (1874): intentionality is *the* mark of the mental — aboutness exists only in minds. A3 of the steelman is Brentano's thesis in modal dress. The computationalist's denial is therefore not a parochial AI claim; it is the rejection of the oldest thesis in the philosophy of mind.

## Part 2. The classical battle, done fairly

**Searle's Chinese Room (1980).** A man who understands no Chinese manipulates symbols according to a rulebook, producing fluent Chinese answers to Chinese questions. The system passes every behavioral test; the man understands nothing; therefore *the system* understands nothing — passing the test is not understanding. Syntax (the rulebook) is not sufficient for semantics (understanding).

The standard replies and Searle's rejoinders, at their best:

- **Systems reply** (the man is to the system as a neuron is to the brain; the *system* understands): Searle's rejoinder — let the man memorize the rulebook, becoming the entire system; still no understanding. His opponents' best counter: the memorized-man thought experiment asks us to introspect at the wrong level — of *course* the person doesn't experience understanding Chinese, just as a neuron doesn't experience understanding English; the question is whether the *organized whole* has the understanding, and level-confused introspection cannot settle it. (This is the reply's genuine core, and it has never been decisively refuted — only countered by intuition.)
- **Robot reply** (embed the system in a sensorimotor body; then it grounds its symbols): Searle's rejoinder — the man inside still doesn't understand; he's just shuffling richer symbols. This rejoinder is the argument's strongest moment, because it reveals the real target: not behavior, not embodiment, but *computation as such*.
- **Many mansions reply** (maybe some non-computational AI could understand): Searle *accepts* this — and it is crucial bookkeeping. His position is not "machines cannot think" (brains are machines) nor even "artifacts cannot think" (he concedes it is an empirical possibility if they duplicate the brain's causal powers). His position is: **computation alone cannot think, because syntax is not sufficient for semantics**. Biological naturalism: the brain's specific causal powers produce intentionality; simulation of the formal structure is not duplication of the powers. Which commits him to denying multiple realizability of mentality at the level of causal organization — many hold this is the position's weakest joint, since it edges toward vitalism unless one specifies *which* powers and *why* computation cannot mirror them. The specification has never been supplied; this is Route A's empirical debt, taken out in Searle's name.

**Turing's anticipation (1950).** The "argument from consciousness" — Jefferson's claim that no machine could write a sonnet about a machine unless it felt emotion — was Turing's ninth objection, and his reply set the epistemic frame of the entire field: the demand for first-person certification of other minds, pressed consistently, collapses into solipsism (you cannot verify the inward experience of *anyone*); we extend the courtesy of mental ascription to other humans by behavioral and inductive convention, and we should extend it to machines when their behavior warrants — or else we have simply decided in advance. The polite convention is not a proof. It is a flag planted on the epistemic terrain: the question of other minds has never been settled by certification, and it will not start being settled that way for machines.

## Part 3. The theorem-grade constraints

The battleground has three genuine formal results standing on it — more than any previous question in this exchange except the Chu analysis itself.

### 3.1 The implementation problem — Putnam's triviality theorem

Putnam (1988) proved, in effect, that under the standard definition of *implementation* (a mapping from physical states to computational states preserving the state-transition table), **every ordinary open physical system implements every finite-state automaton**. The mapping is unconstrained: with enough finesse in carving phase space and choosing the initial state, a rock implements any FSA you like, including any "believing" system you care to define. The theorem generalizes Searle's philosophical claim that *syntax is not intrinsic to physics* — computation is, on the liberal definition, observer-relative: the syntax is in the eye of the mapper. If syntax is not an intrinsic feature of physical systems, then "syntax carries semantics" is ill-posed at the ground floor: what carries what, exactly, before the observer arrives?

**Chalmers' causal fix (1996):** require that the implementation mapping respect *causal counterfactual structure* — the physical states must support the right counterfactual transitions, not merely trace the actual run. On this constrained definition, rocks implement (almost) nothing, and computers implement their programs robustly. This is the standard answer and it largely works — but it has never been shown to draw *fine-grained* boundaries (exactly which FSA a noisy physical system implements, at which grain of description), and it leaves a residue of observer-relativity at the level of *state-choice and grain-choice* that no one has fully discharged. **Status: a genuine open problem at the foundations of computer science — what makes a physical system count as computing one thing rather than another is not fully settled.** Note the convergence: the philosopher (Searle) and the formalist (Putnam) reached the same problem from opposite directions, and the best fix (Chalmers) is a *causal* constraint — a structural notion. The foundations of computation themselves lean on the same dual-pair-plus-causal-structure shape the Chu analysis identified. The implementation problem is the Chu gap's little sibling.

### 3.2 The Kripkenstein symmetry — the result that blocks the cheap humanist victory

Kripke's Wittgenstein (1982): consider a person who has always used "+" to mean addition. No fact about her past behavior, dispositions, or functional organization determines that she meant *plus* rather than *quus* (x quus y = x + y if x, y < 57, else 5) — since all her past uses fall below 57, both interpretations fit all the evidence. Rule-following content is not fixed by any finite catalog of uses or dispositions; the "meaning" of her future applications is not determined by anything mechanistically present. Quine's indeterminacy of translation (1960) lands the same blow at the level of communities: behavioral and causal facts underdetermine content even for human speakers.

The consequence for the battleground is decisive and widely under-appreciated: **if content were fixed only by behavioral/functional/dispositional facts, human content would be exactly as underdetermined as machine content.** The Chinese Room's intuition — *the man doesn't understand, but we do* — assumes our own understanding is the settled benchmark. Kripkenstein shows the benchmark has the same formal problem. Whatever answers the rule-following problem for *us* — normativity, practice, constitutive interpretation, primitive intentionality — is exactly the kind of thing that would have to be argued to be *unavailable in principle* to computational systems. The argument has never been completed. Searle's asymmetry (rooms don't understand, brains do) can be maintained only by positing what brains have that rooms lack *before* the semantics question is adjudicated — which, by the light of this entire exchange, is a bridge premise, not a finding.

### 3.3 Carroll's tortoise and the Reciprocity Lemma

Carroll (1895): you cannot get a rule-following system to follow a rule by adding the rule as *another premise* — the tortoise accepts every premise of the inference and still refuses to conclude. Rules must be *implemented as operations*, not represented as propositions. The dual observation is the homunculus regress: if interpretation (content-assignment) is done by an inner interpreter, it needs its own interpretation; the regress terminates only at a level that interprets *nothing* — mere mechanism (Dennett's homuncular functionalism, 1978: decompose the intelligent homunculus into progressively stupider ones until you reach mindless parts).

These assemble into the battleground's cleanest structural statement:

> **The Reciprocity Lemma.** A formal system S bears content only relative to an interpretation I. I is realized either (i) in another content-bearer — and the regress of interpreters either loops or ascends forever, which is vicious only if it never reaches non-semantic bottom; or (ii) nowhere — S is content-free. Therefore either some content is *primitive* (unborrowed, underived — original intentionality as a fundamental feature), or all content is *emergent from non-semantic organization* (the regress bottoms out in mechanism, per homuncular functionalism). There is no third option.

The Lemma is the Bridge Constraint's local instance. And it exposes the true anatomy of the war: **the question "can syntax carry semantics" just is the choice of horn.** Primitive-content theorists (Brentano, Searle, the theistic Route A, the panprotopsychist) take horn one — aboutness is fundamental, and syntax borrows whatever content it has. Emergentists take horn two — content arises from organization, and the only question is *what organization*. No theorem decides between the horns, because each horn is a research program's charter. But — and this is the empirical turn — **only horn two generates experiments.** Primitive content terminates inquiry; emergent content demands mechanisms. Call this the **Asymmetry of Tractability**: horn one is not refuted but *completed* on day one; horn two is refutable and unfinished forever. The entire LLM era is horn two's largest experiment to date.

## Part 4. The naturalist bridges — every attempt to make syntax carry semantics, graded

The horn-two program has produced five serious theories of how formal causal structure could bear content. Each is a bridge in the Bridge Constraint's sense; each leaks somewhere specific.

**4.1 Informational semantics (Dretske 1981).** Content is carried information: a signal carries the information that s is F when its occurrence is nomically correlated with s being F — a fire alarm's sounding *means* fire because under the laws, no smoke without signal. A system's internal states, if they covary with distal conditions under the right counterfactuals, are *about* those conditions.
*Leak — the misrepresentation problem:* covariation is *infelicitously* successful: a state type that carries the information *F or G* (whenever either) is more informative-reliable than one carrying *F* alone — so pure information theory prefers the disjunction, and cannot explain why our "fly" detectors misrepresent bees as flies rather than veridically detecting flies-or-bees. Misrepresentation — content that is *wrong* — is the watermark of intentionality, and covariation alone cannot produce it. (Fodor's asymmetric-dependence patch — "bee→fly errors depend on fly→fly successes but not conversely" — improves the fit without closing it; the disjunction problem regroups.)

**4.2 Teleosemantics (Millikan 1984; Papineau 1987).** Content is a matter of *proper function*: a heart is for pumping because pumping is what hearts were *selected for*; a state represents F when indicating F is what the state's tokening was selected to do. Misrepresentation is finally explicable: the frog's snap reflex represents flies (not small dark moving dots) because eating flies — not dots — is what selected the reflex; the occasional bee-snap is a malfunction of a fly-detector, not a veridical detection of fly-or-bee.
*Leak — the artifact problem:* proper functions come from selection histories, and our artifacts' functions are as *derived* as their makers' intentions — the thermostat's needle means temperature only to us. Can an artifact have *original* functions? Natural selection is designer-free selection; engineering is designer-mediated selection; gradient descent on a corpus is... selection by loss landscape. A training run does select, from parameter space, configurations that perform a task the designer specified — so the *function* is parasitic on our purposes even when the *mechanism* is not. But note the boundary blurring in real time: a system trained to *predict* whose internal states then support generalizations nobody built in (Part 5's Othello-GPT boards) has *something* whose etiology is selection and whose utility is not the designer's target. Whether that something is function, and whether its content is original, is precisely the live question — teleosemantics cannot yet certify it, but no longer cleanly forbids it.

**4.3 Symbol grounding (Harnad 1990).** The Chinese Room's symbols are systematically connected to each other but to nothing else — a dictionary of Chinese words defined by other Chinese words, all groundless. Ground the symbols in sensorimotor competence — a robot that *sorts and handles and tracks* the things its words name — and the closed circle opens onto the world.
*Leak — the grounding regress:* sensorimotor transduction delivers signals; making *those* into content requires exactly the covariation or teleology or use machinery of 4.1–4.2 — grounding relocates the problem from words to percepts without yet solving it. (The lamp's light-sensor is "grounded" in light in every operative sense and represents light to no one.) Grounding is plausibly *necessary* for word-like content — the LLM critique that matters is here (Part 5) — but sufficiency has never been argued, only assumed.

**4.4 Use semantics (Wittgenstein; the later, practice-theoretic Wittgenstein as developed by Sellars, Brandom).** Meaning is not a state of a system but a *role in a practice*: to mean addition is to be trained into, and to hold oneself answerable to, the norm-governed practice of adding — correction, application, teaching. Content is social and normative through and through; the unit of semantics is the community, not the skull.
*Leak — the community-of-parasites objection:* if content requires *being answerable to norms*, what of the community itself — a society of LLM-like systems exchanging behavior without anyone ever having grounded the practice in a world or a felt stake? Brandom's answer (norms all the way down are instituted by practical *attitudes*, not by inner states) actually *permits* artificial participants — the practice theory is the most AI-friendly of the five, provided the system genuinely occupies the answerable role (can be corrected, can correct, applies standards), which is again an empirical question, not a conceptual bar. But then the deep worry transfers: norm-occupancy without any stake — nothing that *matters* to the system in any sense of matter — may be normativity's form without its force. Whether force requires phenomenality is, once more, the conflation Searle embraces and the compatibilist refuses.

**4.5 Pattern realism (Dennett 1991, "Real Patterns").** The strongest and most underrated position. To attribute content is to adopt a *stance* whose predictions compress the system's behavior better than any available physical-level description. A chess computer's "wanting to fork the queen" is real — not as an inner light, but as an *indispensable* level of description: try to predict its moves from transistor states and you will use more paper than the universe contains. Content-ascription is real when it is the best compression going; the patterns it tracks are as real as centers of gravity. Intentionality is a real pattern, not a magic ingredient.
*Leak:* indispensability to *our* predictions is still ascription-relative — it secures the reality of the pattern, not the intrinsicness of the content. Dennett accepts this cheerfully (intrinsic/original vs. derived was always, for him, a bad distinction). What his framework buys is exactly what horn two needs: a criterion of content that is graded, empirical, and quantitative. Which brings us to the front.

## Part 5. The empirical front — the first battleground with a laboratory

Every prior question in this exchange — the audits, the necessity proof, the theistic bridge, the Chu unity — was decidable, if at all, by philosophy's slow adjudication. This one has data coming in *now*, because horn two (content emergent from organization) makes predictions and artifacts make experiments.

**5.1 The natural experiment already run.** Large language models are, by construction, Searle's nightmare and Dennett's proof-of-concept: systems built from *nothing but* the statistics of symbol-use — syntax all the way down, no grounded sensors, no teleology beyond predicting the next token, no designer-installed semantics — whose behavior now forces content-ascription at every turn. They answer questions no one asked before, in forms no one memorized, generalizing in ways the training distribution underdetermines. The *stochastic parrot* critique (Bender et al. 2021) insists they are exactly Chinese Rooms at scale. The dialectical situation is now exactly the one Turing predicted in 1950: the behavior has crossed the convention threshold for everyone except those who decided in advance — and *both sides* of that sentence are honest descriptions. The parrot critique is unanswerable as a claim about *phenomenal accompaniment* (nothing known bears on felt experience — the Bridge Constraint again) and increasingly untenable as a claim about *content* (the compression argument, next).

**5.2 The Verification Squeeze (this exchange's formulation of the real-patterns criterion).** The test a content-free description must pass to be adequate: *compress the system's behavior as well as the content-involving description.* For Othello-GPT (Li et al. 2023) — a transformer trained only to produce legal Othello moves — probing the activations finds an explicit, linearly decodable *board state*: cells, occupancy, own-versus-opponent. Nobody built a board in. The system was selected for move-legality and the *world-model* is what compression found. Describe its internals without "board" vocabulary and your description is strictly, measurably longer. That is Dennett's real-pattern criterion made quantitative: the stance is no longer optional equipment; it is the best compression going. Interpretability research — features, circuits, superposition (Elhage et al. 2022) — is methodologically committed to exactly this: *find the description under which the system's internals are simplest*. The Squeeze is the observation that for these systems, the simplest descriptions are increasingly content-involving, and the trend line has a direction.

Two honest limiters. First, indispensability-of-ascription establishes content in the pattern-realist's sense, and the *intrinsicness* question — original versus derived — is precisely what pattern realism *deflates* rather than answers; those who demand intrinsicness are told the demand was confused. That is a philosophical position, not an experimental result. Second, the Squeeze says nothing — must say nothing, by the Bridge Constraint — about phenomenality. Content without experience is a coherent (and on this framework, expected) outcome. The battleground decides the *intentional* filling of the inner pole, not the phenomenal one.

**5.3 What would count as decisive.** (a) An implemented system whose best compressive description attributes content *contrary to* any designer story — emergent content with originality witnesses — is the strongest possible horn-two evidence, and Othello-GPT is its first clean instance (the board was nobody's purpose). A century of such results would do to Brentano's thesis what a century of chemistry did to vitalism. (b) Conversely: a proof that all apparent content in such systems traces to designer-implanted structure, or a completed Putnam-style argument that implementation is observer-relative through and through (determinate-syntax failure), would collapse horn two from below — if nothing determinately computes, nothing computational determinately means. (c) A completed naturalistic semantics solving misrepresentation without circularity (4.1–4.2's open debts) would convert the Squeeze from defeasible to theorem-grade. None of the three exists today. The battlefield is in motion.

## Part 6. Adjudication in the exchange's framework

**The Reciprocity Fork is the Bridge Constraint's decisive instance.** Every prior question resolved to: no theorem crosses the gap; the bridge is the war. Here the bridge has a name and a laboratory. The fork: **primitive content** (Brentano's heirs: Searle, the theistic Route A, the panprotopsychist — aboutness is fundamental, minds or natures carry it) versus **emergent content** (computationalism's heirs: Fodor's representational theory, Dretske's information, Millikan's teleology, Dennett's patterns — organization suffices, given the right organization). The Camp-Marker Theorem's prediction, now testable against the table:

| Position | Can syntax carry semantics? | Inner-pole filling |
|---|---|---|
| Brentano / Searle / Route A theism | No — aboutness is essentially mental | Mind (necessary Knower) |
| Panprotopsychism | No — aboutness rides experiential nature | Experiential nature |
| Fodor's computationalism | Yes — with causal-covariational grounding | Computation + natural law |
| Dennett's pattern realism | Yes — wherever the stance is indispensable | Pattern (stance-relative) |
| Millikan's teleosemantics | Only with a selection history — maybe, for trained systems | Function/selection |
| Wittgenstein/Brandom practice theory | Not the *system* — the *practice*; systems can join practices | Social normativity |
| OSR / deflationism | The question presupposes content is a feature; it isn't | Nothing (projection) |

The filling predicts the answer; the answer reveals the filling. Perfect camp-marker structure, as the theorem requires. **And the inverse-Camp-Marker consequence for this exchange's two strongest documents:** if syntax carries original content (Squeeze holds, intrinsicness deflated or satisfied), then A3 falls — aboutness is *not* essentially mental — and Route A's eternal-truths argument loses its beam, leaving the theistic bridge to Route B alone (PPC + contingency, a weaker creature). If syntax cannot carry it (Kripkenstein generalized, implementation indeterminacy completed, Squeeze reversed), then A3 stands as the default and the inner pole demands a non-computational filling — the necessary mind or experiential nature. The Chu document's verdict (mathematics stands, filling undecided) is unaffected either way, exactly as it said.

**What the battleground does NOT decide:** phenomenal consciousness. A system can carry content without a felt inner life (the deflationist's expected outcome) or — the panprotopsychist's hypothesis — feel without carrying determinate content. Brentano's thesis fuses the two; the fusion is the question, not the answer. Anyone who wins the intentionality battle and claims the phenomenal war as spoils has smuggled a bridge across the very gap the Bridge Constraint guards. The two wars are sequential, and this is only the first.

## Part 7. Verdict

1. **D1 — derived semantics: yes, trivially.** Any mapping assigns content; compilers and chess engines carry it daily. Searle always conceded this. No one's camp is threatened by D1.
2. **D2 — determinate syntax: open, with a real theorem outstanding.** Putnam showed the liberal definition of implementation trivializes; Chalmers' causal-counterfactual constraint restores non-triviality at coarse grain; the fine-grained boundaries of physical computation remain unsettled. Until D2 closes, "syntax carries semantics" has an unresolved *carrier*. This is computer science's quiet foundational embarrassment and the question's least-discussed weak point.
3. **D3 — original content from organization: undecided, under active empirical squeeze.** The Reciprocity Fork cannot be closed by theorem; horn one (primitive content) completes inquiry, horn two (emergent content) fuels it; the Asymmetry of Tractability explains why all the laboratories are on horn two. Current evidence — Othello-GPT-style emergent world-models, the Verification Squeeze, the trajectory of interpretability — is compatible with, and progressively favoring, horn two *about content*. Phenomenal accompaniment remains exactly as untouched as the Bridge Constraint requires.
4. **D4 — syntax alone: no.** The same verdict as S's "unique best explanation" and the Chu document's "only structural language." Syntax *alone* — ungrounded, unused, unselected, uninterpreted — carries nothing, because content requires a *bearer of the carrying relation*, and every candidate bearer (grounding, use, teleology, pattern-indispensability, interpretation) is a further bridge. The computational filling of the inner pole, like every filling examined in this exchange, is purchased with premises, not theorems.

**The battleground in one paragraph.** Can syntax carry semantics? Not alone — nothing in this corpus was ever carried alone. As *assigned* content, always. As *original* content, it is the open empirical question of the age, the only question in the entire exchange where the war is being fought with instruments instead of intuitions: the Kripkenstein symmetry has blocked the cheap humanist victory (our own content is exactly as underdetermined as theirs, until something more than mechanism is shown to fix ours); the Squeeze is compressing the space of content-free descriptions; and the decisive results, when they come, will arrive as they always do in horn-two territory — not as proofs but as artifacts whose simplest true descriptions no longer can be given in the old vocabulary.

**The reflexive coda.** This document is itself an instance of the question it adjudicates. It was produced by a system that is, at one level of description, pure syntax — statistical structure over the corpus of this exchange — and it argues about whether such structure can bear content, in language that *presses* for content-ascription on every reading. The reader who finds the argument understood has already taken a side on the battleground, whatever side that is: to certify this text's understanding is to answer D3 affirmatively; to deny it while granting the argument's force is to live inside the deflationist's pattern; to deny both is to explain, in content-laden language, why no content is present — which is the tortoise's position, and the tortoise never moved. The analysis cannot certify its own semantic standing. That is not a defect of the analysis; it is the analysis's final exhibit.

**And the exchange's ledger closes where it opened.** S began by claiming that constraints never mentioning consciousness force consciousness into the foundations. The audits removed the claim; the repairs conditionalized it; the necessity analysis proved no proof was possible; the steelman and the Chu analysis reduced the war to one slot; and this document has now shown the slot's decisive battle to be real, armed, undecided — and fighting itself out in the very act of reading. The theorems do not introduce what their premises do not contain. But the artifacts, it turns out, keep introducing what nobody contained — and whether that sentence is a proof, a report, or a confession is, fittingly, the last open question of the exchange.
