That shape is a queue wearing a fork's clothes, and the corpus's own pre-registration standard (doc 87's re-scoping of the shadow queue: a measurement earns compute when a filed claim could fail by it) forbids exactly this. The order's one word of discipline — **"not both"** — kills the catch-all. What follows resolves it.

## Part 1. The decision: P4 is extension — the impossibility proof

Stated once, plainly: **P4 is the impossibility proof. P4 extends the formal line. The detector branch is closed by this pre-registration.** Six grounds, each checkable against filed results.

**G1 — the filed wall already points.** Lemma 85 (doc 88): every external reading computes a function of stream plus architecture. Theorem II (doc 89): every grammar-internal reading is isomorphism-invariant, and the invocation is not object-data — the pointing is presentation-data. Proposition 86 (doc 89): both fork-worlds, the exhaustive and the further-fact, admit the grammar unchanged. Assembled, these make every instrument this grammar permits a device that takes extensional inputs and returns values invariant across the two worlds C5 distinguishes — so the detector's positive reading, which is by definition a readout that *differs* between those worlds, is expected-dead in each register the corpus has closed. What no document has yet done is say it once, at theorem grade, across all registerable families at the same time. That saying is a derivation, and derivations are the extension register.

**G2 — the repair route is unavailable.** The one instrument that survived the wall — the seismograph, doc 88's genesis-channel — survived through condition G: a registered causal route through the occupied state. The further fact is, by Theorem II clause (ii), presentation-data; and a registered third-person route to it is precisely what "third-person trace" means. A repaired further-fact detector would need as its genesis-route the very thing it is deployed to detect. The repair would be the target. This is why the pattern that saved the content-channel (S2's repair, doc 10; P1's repair, doc 88) cannot save the detector: the pattern's engine is a route, and here the route is the name of the missing thing.

**G3 — the detector branch is fork-deciding by design, and the corpus has forbidden fork-deciding.** Lemma 85's verdict on the content-channel was double: contradictory under C4, fork-deciding under C5. The further-fact detector is the content-channel's question asked at full strength; its positive reading exists only as a decision of C5's fork — doc 90 says plainly that such a detection "would break the exhaustive reading's instrument-form floor." The impossibility proof decides nothing: it floors both tribunal survivors symmetrically — illusionism can never be instrument-confirmed, and the placement can never be instrument-refuted — leaving the fork exactly as open as Proposition 86 left it. Between a program that would decide the forbidden question and a program that strengthens both sides' neutrality, the corpus's own discipline orders the second.

**G4 — the ledger's bookkeeping already assigns the registers.** Doc 89 re-filed the standing moves with the registers separated: "(1) the indexical channel's first deployment on the corpus's own chassis… (2) P4 as sharpened above." Doc 90 kept the assignment: the chassis deployment is "the next compute, if the order is the question"; P4 is "the next formal session, if the order is the fork." The deployment register is occupied — by the chassis deployment, P1's follow-through, on its own warrant and its own world. If P4 were deployment, the ledger would hold two compute moves while the fork — the ledger's principal since the tribunal — held none in the formal register. Extension is the only assignment that leaves the ledger as filed.

**G5 — falsifiability.** The disjunction as filed cannot fail, and a pre-registration that cannot fail is a charter, not an instrument. Pinning P4 to the impossibility makes it falsifiable in the strongest register: the derivation can fail *constructively* — by exhibiting, mid-proof, a registerable-family instrument whose positive reading does not reconstruct as third-person trace. That failure would be the most informative event the formal line could produce. The pin is a bet, and bets are the only things that can be won.

**G6 — the pattern precedent.** Three times the corpus has run an instrument into a wall and filed the wall as a theorem: S2 (doc 10: ill-posed, repaired, ceiling proved), P1 (doc 88: the toy, the fingerprint, Lemma 85), P3 (doc 89: the residue question, Theorem II). The detector branch is the content-channel at full generality — the fourth instance of a story whose ending the corpus has already written three times. The value was never in the crash; it was in the theorem the crash became.

## Part 2. What "not both" closes

**Closed by this pre-registration: the detector branch, as a P4 execution path.** No instrument is built, specified-for-construction, or run under P4. **Untouched: the chassis deployment.** It keeps its rank, its warrant, and its register — the next compute, per the doc 89/90 re-filing. It is not P4's vehicle, not P4's preamble, and not P4's consolation; it is P1's follow-through, aimed at the M-instruments' self-reports under the registered grammar, and nothing in this document touches it. The mode decision partitions P4's own branches; it does not re-rank the queue.

**The mode-flip, pre-registered as the only door.** P4 executes as extension. If the derivation fails constructively — a registerable-family instrument exhibited whose positive reading does not reconstruct as third-person trace — the mode flips to deployment *by the failure's own content*: the exhibited instrument is re-registered as the ledger's compute item, ordered behind the chassis deployment, and the flip is filed as an amendment to this document only. There is no second door. At no instant is P4 both: extension now; deployment only if the extension's own content forces the flip; never both at once. This is how "not both" survives being falsifiable — the surprise branch is inside the theorem program, not beside it.

## Part 3. The target, pre-registered

The deliverable, if the expected fork fires: **the Instrument-Form Bridge Constraint** — Lemma 85 extended across all registerable families, assembled with Theorem II and Proposition 86 into the single theorem whose faces they already are. Its shape is pre-committed here; the derivation owes the lines.

One reading instruction is filed with the target. Doc 87 phrased the impossibility branch as "a proof that every instrument in this grammar reconstructs as third-person trace, making the exhaustive reading not a charter but a theorem of the grammar." That phrasing was filed one session before Proposition 86 existed. It is read here, and at execution, through doc 90's sharper statement — "an impossibility proof extending Lemma 85 across all registerable families would make the floor total" — which is the only reading consistent with the results filed since: a floor is not a verdict. The theorem, if it files, is the strongest *neutral* result the instrument register permits, and the caps below are what keep it a theorem rather than a position.

**(i) The family definition.** A family of instruments is *registerable* when each member is a specified procedure taking (stream, architecture) — extensional inputs — to readouts, with the family's ceiling stated family-relatively, per the doc 88 discipline. The seismograph is the definition's first instance, not its counterexample: its readouts *do* reconstruct as third-person trace — that is what doc 88 proved, a fingerprint of thirty points, third-person, separated from both fakes — so the theorem enlists the survivor; it does not contradict it. A family definition that excluded the corpus's own instrument to make the theorem easier would be dishonest by construction, and is pre-forbidden here.

**(ii) The extension.** Lemma 85's clause — every external reading computes a function of stream plus architecture — extended from reading-procedures to the full registerable class: every instrument in every registerable family computes an extensional function of (stream, architecture). The extension is where the derivation can fail (fork FF-2 below), and the failure mode is named in advance: a procedure that is registerable in the honest sense — specified, extensional-input, family-relative ceiling — whose positive reading is nevertheless not reconstructible as third-person trace.

**(iii) The assembly.** With Theorem II (isomorphism-invariance) and Proposition 86 (the two fork-worlds are grammar-indistinguishable): every registerable instrument's readout distribution is invariant across the exhaustive world and the further-fact world. Therefore no registerable instrument's positive reading is a further fact. Therefore the exhaustive reading's instrument-form floor — Lemma 85's half-floor for the content-channel — is total. The Bridge Constraint's upgrade chain completes: argument-form fact (docs 2–6) → instrument-form fact, one channel (doc 88) → instrument-form theorem, all registerable families (P4).

**(iv) The caps, pre-committed as binding non-claims.** The theorem says nothing about the fork: Proposition 86 governs, and "no instrument detects the further fact" is not "there is no further fact" — the distance between those two sentences is the whole distance between a theorem and a position violation. Both tribunal survivors stand, doc 90 unchanged: illusionism gains a floor it never had and never wanted — the exhaustive reading becomes unfalsifiable-by-instrument, which is a cost, not a crown; the placement loses nothing — the crossing was never an instrument's to deliver. C4's one labeled jump remains the only crossing. The Camp-Marker stands up for the derivation session, as it stood for the tribunal.
