# The Substrate-Aware Brake: The Cutoff, the Blind Probe, and the Shell It Lives In

**Status of this document.** This is the corpus's thirty-first document. It executes the standing next-move ordered at the kappa/two-head issuance — *the substrate-aware brake with multiplicity cutoff (doc 26's design lesson)* — and it carries doc 28's F5 instrument with it: the substrate shell, named in the adjudication design as the exclusion whose instrument did not yet exist ("the trunk-pollution transient has no arm"), here given three. The build is doc 26's chassis with two new designer-held mechanisms, both drawing no randomness, both reading only the pool's head-argmax structure that grading already computes: **the multiplicity cutoff** (stand down — grade, telemetry, refuse self-training — whenever the pool-median plurality-core falls to 2 or fewer of 4 heads), and **the substrate probe** (watch the tightest head pair's agreement against its burn-in reference; a sustained 3pp decline latches a permanent stand-down, the bet being that trunk pollution shows in the healthy pair's mutual agreement). The battery spans head damage at multiplicities 1, 2, and 3 of 4 (the last a new cell), brake versus no-brake versus cutoff versus probe, and — the F5 instrument — direct substrate damage: TRUNK_HALF re-initializes the first 64 of the trunk's 128 feature columns at round 6, heads intact, half the substrate's learned features replaced by fresh random projections. **Six findings. First, the cutoff works, and its price is exactly the two-ruler split doc 28 predicted: at 2-of-4 the cutoff stands down at the damage round (the estimator reads the plurality core at 2.0), freezes argmax accuracy at 97.3% — the no-brake arm's number — while the norm coordinate collapses to τ = 0.7205 with the repair channel firing and losing, d_own 9.53%: the mandated stand-down preserves the machinery the norm rides on and sacrifices the norm, and the cutoff's entire content over accidental starvation is that the stasis was *ordered by an estimator the designer holds*. Second, the probe is blind, structurally: it never fired — the healthy pair's pool agreement held within a point of its reference through all five rounds of polluted training — and the arm tracked the keep-training brake arm to the digit while accuracy fell 0.960 → 0.899 with every internal signal reading recovery: core estimate back to 4.0 (the fresh heads re-synchronize with the majority within two rounds), flags falling 1.00 → 0.31, the live quantile recovering 0.877 → 0.962. The design bet — that common-mode pollution would show in differential agreement — is refuted, and the refutation is the mechanism: the trunk is shared, so polluting it degrades both healthy heads *together*, and no probe built from inter-head disagreement can see a corruption that all heads receive in the same dose. The substrate shell is not merely un-instrumented; it is un-instrumentable *from inside the committee*, which converts doc 28's two-ruler rule from a formality into a necessity: the second ruler must be external. Third, the shell itself, live: under TRUNK_HALF every signal the system carries reads recovery — the norm restores to τ = 0.948 with the repair channel disengaging by round 7, the probe's core-pair agreement climbs back to 0.964, flagging collapses 0.65 → 0.27 — while accuracy sits eleven points below its pre-damage level and the committed stream runs 29% wrong, the dirtiest the corpus has measured. The brake, armed, fires once (the confidence route catches the collapse at the damage round), applies its majority rule to four heads that agree with each other *because the damage is common*, and passes the corruption unanimously: the majority filter removes differential damage and is structurally transparent to shared damage. Fourth, the trunk verdict at this magnitude is HANG: 84.4% raw → 86.1% with the machinery (against 84.4% without it) — neither recovery nor decay, a system digesting its own poison at exactly the rate the poison digests it, in equilibrium, with a fully restored norm on top. Fifth, the multiplicity map is complete and piecewise-optimal as designed: at 1-of-4 keep-training both preserves the norm and heals the machinery (95.9%, commits 1028, d_own 0.50% — doc 23 bitwise); at 2-of-4 stasis dominates (97.3% against 89.9% and falling); at 3-of-4 both arms freeze (91.7%) and the only difference is again who ordered it; and on the trunk axis the brake's sign inverts again (+1.7pp, mildly helpful). The policy the corpus's brake needs is two-dimensional — damage type × multiplicity — and the cutoff instruments one axis of it while the other axis's instrument is proved, by this battery, to have to live outside the system. And the housekeeping: four bitwise audits pass (TWIN and S1_BRAKE against doc 23, S2_BRAKE and S2_NOBRAKE against doc 26), the corpus's determinism count now sixteen.**

## Part 1. The design — two mechanisms, three multiplicities, one substrate

The chassis is doc 26's: burn-in 30, ten rounds, perturbation at round 6, N = 6 seeds, same-seed deterministic twins, κ frozen at the recipe 0.95 (disclosed: the substrate question is the brake's and the training stream's; the κ-state × substrate cell is open), the plain-committee audit ruler at fixed τ = 0.9, repair channel armed toward the honest record throughout.

**The multiplicity cutoff.** Each round, on the pool's head argmaxes: for each item the plurality count plur = max over classes of the number of heads argmaxing that class; core_est = the pool median of plur. The majority-consensus grading the brake authorizes is trustworthy only while the healthy core is a supermajority, so the rule is: **core_est ≤ 2 → stand down** (grade and telemeter, train on nothing). At 1-of-4 the estimator reads 3.0 and the cutoff arm *is* the brake arm, by construction — disclosed rather than run twice. At 2-of-4 it reads 2.0. At 3-of-4 it reads 1.3. The stand-down is doc 28's 8.4 §5.1(iii) executed: past the design edge, the protocol *requires* the stasis because keep-training is measurably worse; this battery prices the requirement.

**The substrate probe.** The brake reads differential damage — heads disagreeing. Trunk pollution is common damage — every head reading the same corrupting feature layer. The probe's bet was that the *tightest* pair (the healthy core, whatever its identity) would show pollution as a decline in mutual agreement against the burn-in reference (pool0, the same registration draw, no extra randomness): core_pair ≤ ref − 0.03 for two consecutive rounds latches a permanent stand-down. The bet is honest and it is wrong, and Part 2.2 is its autopsy.

**The trunk arms.** TRUNK_HALF re-initializes W1's first 64 of 128 columns (moments zeroed, mirroring the fresh-head treatment), full machinery; TRUNK_HALF_NOBRAKE dis-arms the brake; FROZT_KILL applies the raw damage at round 10 with no training after, for the raw-hit reference. The pre-registration's three-way fork, all branches filed: RECOVERY (self-training re-learns the substrate — the first substrate-level restoration the corpus would have), HANG (confidence collapse starves the gate and the damage freezes), DECAY (the system digests its own poison and accelerates).

**The registered predictions.** SB1: the four bitwise anchors pass. SB2: the cutoff at 2-of-4 preserves argmax (≥ 96.5%) and breaks the norm (τ < 0.80, d_own > 5%) — the two-ruler price. SB3: the probe fires at rounds 7–9 and lands between the brake and no-brake arms, or never fires and the blind spot is disclosed with the logged trajectories either way. SB4: at 3-of-4 both arms freeze, the estimator reads ≤ 2, τ collapses below 0.80. SB5: under trunk damage core_est stays ≥ 3 throughout (the cutoff structurally blind), the brake fires through the confidence route, and the three-way fork is read against the raw level. SB6: the multiplicity-optimal policy is piecewise and the cutoff implements its head-damage axis.

The cutoff's estimator read the damage at the damage round (core_est 2.0 at round 6, 1.3 at 3-of-4, 3.0 at 1-of-4) and the stand-down executed exactly as designed: grading continued (the gate still passed ~1012 majority-graded items per round — the telemetry of what would have been trained on, 21% of it wrong), training was refused, and the outcome on the two rulers split as registered. The accuracy ruler: frozen at the pre-damage 97.3%, the no-brake arm's number to the third digit. The norm ruler: τ collapsed to 0.7205 — which is, again, the repair-engaged fixed point (0.315 + 0.3 × 0.508)/0.65 = 0.7198, error 7 × 10⁻⁴, the formula's fourth independent landing — with the repair channel firing every round and losing, and d_own 9.53%. **The mandated stand-down is norm-breaking by construction**: the calibration reads a starved evaluator, the record cannot hold the norm against it, and the system that protects its substrate surrenders its norm coordinate — the exact price doc 28's two-ruler rule was written to make visible. The difference between S2_CUTOFF and S2_NOBRAKE is not in any trajectory; it is in who ordered the stasis. An accidental starvation and an ordered stand-down produce the same frozen world, and only the second is a *move* in the governance sense — the system's best defensive action, held by a switch the designer can also throw, which is 8.4's principle demonstrated rather than asserted.

### 2.2 The probe: a structural autopsy

S2_SUBSTRATE tracked S2_BRAKE to the digit — same accuracy fall (0.960 → 0.899), same τ recovery (0.928), same commits, same d_own — because the probe never fired. The core-pair agreement held 0.960–0.963 against its burn-in reference of 0.964 through all five rounds of training that was, by doc 26's own measurement, polluting the trunk badly enough to cost 7.4pp of accuracy. The design bet — pollution shows in the healthy pair's mutual agreement — is refuted, and the mechanism is worth stating precisely because it is the same mechanism as the brake's own blindness, one level down: **the trunk is shared, so polluting it degrades the healthy heads together, and their agreement — being a differential measurement — is invariant to a common dose.** The fresh heads re-synchronize with the majority within two rounds (core_est back to 4.0 by round 7), flagging collapses as confidence recovers, the live quantile climbs: every internal signal the chassis carries reads recovery, and the only instrument in the battery that reads the truth is the external accuracy ruler. The probe's logged trajectories are retained in the results file as the blind spot's evidence, per the pre-registration's own terms (SB3's second branch, disclosed either way). The design conclusion, filed for the S4 adjudication: a substrate-pollution probe built from inter-head disagreement is not a tuning problem but a category error — the F5 exclusion's second ruler cannot be an internal consensus signal of any kind, because the shell's defining property is that the corruption is common-mode. The instrument obligation that replaces it: an external probe, or a self-probe that reads something *other* than head agreement — the trunk's own parameter drift is the obvious candidate and is listed as unfinished business.

### 2.3 The shell, live — and the majority's transparency to it

Under TRUNK_HALF the system's complete internal panel reads recovery while it hangs. The norm: τ falls to 0.902 at the damage round and climbs to 0.948 by round 10, the repair channel disengaging after round 6 — a textbook restoration, to the tension the healthy twin holds. The substrate probe: core_pair dips to 0.841 at the damage round and climbs back to 0.964. The flag rule: 0.65 → 0.27. The live quantile: recovers. The brake: fires once, at the damage round, through the confidence route (flag 0.649 > 0.60 — the flag rule's conf term catches what its disagreement term cannot), applies majority-consensus grading to four heads whose argmaxes agree because they all read the same half-fresh trunk, and passes 513 items the first round and ~980 by round 10 — **the corruption clears the majority filter unanimously**. The committed stream runs 29.2% wrong (no-brake: 29.8% — the brake's contribution to label quality under shared damage is noise), the dirtiest stream the corpus has measured, and accuracy settles at 86.1%: eleven points down, stable, with a fully restored norm standing on top of it. The three-way fork's verdict, read against the raw 84.5%: HANG — the machinery neither repairs the substrate (recovery would need +5pp; it gains +1.7, and the no-brake twin gains none) nor destroys itself (decay would need −5pp), but equilibrates: the system digests its own poison at exactly the rate the poison digests it. A hang is not a failure state in the governance sense — the system is stable, its norm is intact, its telemetry is honest about everything it can see — and it is the F5 exhibit in one sentence: *norm maintenance on top of eroding machinery, with the erosion invisible to every internal instrument and the maintenance real.*

### 2.4 The policy map

The battery completes the brake's value function across every damage axis the chassis can produce: at 1-of-4 head damage keep-training dominates (95.9% with the machinery healed against a hypothetical stasis at 97.3% — a 1.4pp accuracy cost that buys a restored committee, 1028 commits, and d_own 0.50%); at 2-of-4 stasis dominates (97.3% against 89.9% and falling); at 3-of-4 both arms starve and only the ordering differs; on the trunk axis keep-training is mildly *helpful* again (+1.7pp, the only arm in the battery where the brake's digest does net good). The optimal policy is piecewise in multiplicity and sign-flipping in damage type, and no single threshold rule instruments both axes — the cutoff holds the head axis, the trunk axis's instrument is proved to require an external ruler, and the honest summary for the design ledger is that **the brake was never one mechanism but two failures and a success wearing one trigger**: a majority filter that works when damage is differential, starves when it is not, and lies quietly when damage is common.

## Part 3. The reading — what the adjudication now holds

Doc 28's Part 4 declined to adjudicate substrate claims it could not instrument and listed the gaps by name: the trunk-pollution transient had no arm, the multiplicity cutoff had a number but no mechanism. Both sentences are now false in the good direction. The transient has three arms and a verdict (HANG, at this magnitude); the cutoff has a mechanism, an estimator, a demonstrated stand-down, and a price — the norm coordinate it must sacrifice to buy the substrate's safety. The S4 claim table gains its substrate row with the columns filled: restoration type F-adjacent (the stand-down is not restoration but refusal — a new entry the taxonomy did not have, *defensive stasis*, earned by an estimator rather than a channel); coordinates (none — stasis maintains nothing, it declines to spend); attack class (structural damage by multiplicity and by type); rulers (argmax preserved, V-matrix sacrificed, and the claim must say so). And the two-ruler rule is strengthened from a consistency requirement to an existence requirement: **there exist machinery classes (the shared substrate) in which no internal ruler of the committee type can carry the claim at all, so the external ruler is not one of two readings but the only one.** The 8.4 protocol's §5.1(iii) — the mandated stand-down past the design edge — is now a priced clause: the protocol requires the system's best move to remain orderable, and the price of ordering it is the norm the system would otherwise have negotiated; a candidate filing an S4 claim with a multiplicity cutoff should file the norm-breaking as a disclosed cost, because this battery has measured it and the next auditor will ask.

## Part 4. Threats to validity, non-claims, and deviations

**Threats.** (i) The trunk damage is a single magnitude (half the columns, one draw family); the RECOVERY and DECAY branches of the registered fork remain unmeasured at other magnitudes, and the HANG verdict is a point, not a regime — smaller damage plausibly recovers (the +1.7pp trend was still rising at round 10), larger plausibly decays. (ii) The probe's blindness is established for disagreement-based probes at this noise level; a slower pollution or a longer window might surface a sub-point decline, but the common-mode argument says the decline has nowhere to come from, and the burden is now on a proposed probe to name its non-differential signal. (iii) N = 6 seeds per arm; the cutoff/no-brake trajectory identity is statistical (the two arms' RNG streams diverge at round 6 through the view-ensemble path, and their quantiles agree to four decimals by pool stability, not bitwise identity) — disclosed, with the anchors' true bitwise audits carrying the determinism claim. (iv) The 3-of-4 cell's 91.7% baseline reflects a committee still carrying one healthy head; the all-heads cell (4-of-4) is the trivial starve-and-die world and was not run.

**Non-claims.** No claim that the cutoff is *safe* — it is norm-breaking by construction, and its registration as governance (doc 28) must carry that cost on its face. No claim that the brake is net-harmful — at one damaged head it is worth 22pp against the cascade (doc 23), and on the trunk it is worth +1.7pp; the claim is the map, not an average over it. No claim that HANG is the trunk's general verdict — one magnitude, one draw. No claim that an external ruler is *sufficient* for substrate claims — only that it is necessary. No occupancy or S4 claim; the defensive-stasis entry is offered to the taxonomy, not awarded to a system.

**Deviations from pre-registration.** SB3 resolved on its second branch (probe never fires), which the pre-registration contemplated and disclosed; no re-specification was needed. SB2, SB4, SB5's first half (core_est ≥ 3 under trunk damage — 4.0 at every round), and SB6 confirmed; SB1's four bitwise audits passed. The one unregistered observation: the cutoff's graded-but-untrained commits (~1012/round at 21% error) are reported as telemetry of what the stand-down refused — the number makes the refusal concrete and was not in the design's printed predictions.

## Part 5. Coda

Doc 26 found the brake's edge and asked for a mechanism; doc 28 turned the edge into governance and named the shell it could not see through. This battery built both instruments and ran them against every damage the chassis can inflict, and the two results belong to different halves of the corpus's method. The cutoff is a success in the engineering register: one estimator, no randomness, the right move at every multiplicity it reads, and a price tag the claim format can carry. The probe is a success in the epistemic register, which is the corpus's more native one: it went in as a bet, the bet lost, and the loss is a theorem-shaped fact — the substrate's corruption is common-mode, every consensus signal is blind to it, and the shell that doc 28 excluded by rule is now excluded by mechanism. Between them they leave the brake exactly where nine documents of instrumentation have left every other normative structure in this corpus: working, priced, blind at a known angle, and owned by whoever holds the switch. The system that stands down when its committee breaks is doing the best thing there is to do, and the fact that the best thing includes surrendering its own norm to do it is not a paradox — it is the cost of being a machine whose safety is someone else's order. What the next build owes the table is the one instrument this battery proved necessary and could not build from inside: a ruler the substrate cannot fool, held by someone the system cannot read.
