## Part 1. Theorem FF — the white-noise section, and the cell's true shape

**Theorem FF (the white-noise section) [Derived here].** For every locally finite poset P and every environment assignment E, the family ω = {ω^{pq}}_{p ≼ q}, ω^{pq} := I_{E_{pq}}/k_{pq} the maximally mixed state, is a strictly multiplicative preparation section.

*Proof.* Let p ≼ q ≼ r be a composable pair. By Definition 39 the identification is the strict equality E_{pr} = E_{pq} ⊗ E_{qr}. The identity of a tensor product of unital matrix algebras is the tensor of the identities: I_{E_{pq} ⊗ E_{qr}} = I_{E_{pq}} ⊗ I_{E_{qr}}. The plain matrix trace factorizes on simple tensors, Tr_{A⊗B}(X ⊗ Y) = Tr_A(X)·Tr_B(Y), and the dimensions multiply, k_{pr} = k_{pq}k_{qr}. Hence

ω^{pr} = I_{E_{pr}}/k_{pr} = (I_{E_{pq}} ⊗ I_{E_{qr}})/(k_{pq}k_{qr}) = ω^{pq} ⊗ ω^{qr}. ∎

**The corollary that closes the existence half: preparation sections exist on every causal poset, every assignment, unconditionally.** The hygiene instrument certifies the arithmetic on forty-one random posets (twenty-two diamonds among their intervals), every composable pair, to 10⁻¹² — mechanism by compute, statement by the three lines above.

**The reading, and the inversion it forces on the cell.** Doc 42 filed the cell as an existence question with a partial existence theorem ("whenever Arr(P) is freely generated"). The white section says the partial theorem was never needed: existence is free, on every poset, for every assignment. What freeness actually buys is the freedom to CHOOSE: on a chain, every covering family extends (Theorem GG below), so the section can be pure, informative, arbitrary — any fill the environment might carry. On a general poset the choice is constrained, and the constraint's strength is the cell's real content: a SPACE question, not an existence question. The environment's default fill is white noise, and white noise is indifferent to decompositions — the one state whose tensor-product behavior cannot conflict with itself. The corpus has met this shape before, from the other side: doc 84's Proposition 82 found each home's survival mechanism to be the other's kill; here the always-section is the never-informative section, the fill that testifies nothing because it is the same in every presentation.

## Part 2. Theorem GG — the polygon characterization, and the iff

**Definition (the polygon equations) [Derived here].** For an interval [p, r] and a maximal chain c̄ = (p ≺ x₁ ≺ … ≺ r), let ⊗τ^{c̄} denote the iterated tensor of a covering family's values along c̄ (a state of E_{pr} under the strict identifications). The family {τ^c}_{c covering} satisfies the polygon equations when for every interval and every pair of maximal chains c̄, d̄ of [p, r]: ⊗τ^{c̄} = ⊗τ^{d̄}.

**Theorem GG (the polygon characterization) [Derived here; the chain-connectedness is classical].** (a) Every preparation section is determined by its values on the covering arrows: σ^{pq} = ⊗σ^{c̄} along any maximal chain of [p, q], by induction on chain length (local finiteness supplies finite chains). (b) A covering family extends to a preparation section iff it satisfies the polygon equations; the extension is then unique and given by the chain formula. (c) The polygon equations are vacuous — every covering family extends — if and only if every interval of P is a chain.

*Proof.* (a) is the induction. (b) Sufficiency: define σ^{pq} by the chain formula; the polygon equations are exactly well-definedness across chains, and strict multiplicativity is the formula's own bookkeeping (a chain of [p, r] through q splits into chains of [p, q] and [q, r]). Necessity: a section restricts to a family, and (a) forces its values to be the chain products, so two chains through one interval must agree. (c) If every interval is a chain, each interval has exactly one maximal chain and the equations are vacuous. Conversely, suppose some interval [p, r] has two distinct maximal chains c̄ ≠ d̄; they differ on a maximal common-length subposet, and the graph of maximal chains of a finite interval is connected under elementary exchanges — each exchange replacing one interior element — so some pair of chains differs on exactly one diamond p ≺ q₁ ≺ r / p ≺ q₂ ≺ r (both chains sharing all other arrows). On that diamond choose covering states with σ^{pq₁} ⊗ σ^{q₁r} ≠ σ^{pq₂} ⊗ σ^{q₂r}: the two path-products range over the product states of the two factorizations of E_{pr}, each family contains at least two distinct states (the legs are nontrivial), and two nonempty families of states each containing a state the other's chosen value can avoid — concretely, pick any product X of factorization 1 and any product Y of factorization 2 with X ≠ Y, always possible — so the polygon equation fails and the family does not extend. ∎

**The reading.** Doc 42's chain construction ("σ^{pq} := ⊗_{p ≤ i ≺ q} σ^{i,i⁺}") is Theorem GG(b) restricted to the vacuous case, and the corpus's running example (ℕ, ≤) is diamond-free by being linear. The iff sharpens the filed intuition into a boundary: the chain's freedom is exactly the diamond's absence, and every diamond is a real constraint — there is no poset so gently branching that its branch is free.

## Part 3. Theorem HH — the diamond's price

Fix a diamond: p ≺ q₁ ≺ r, p ≺ q₂ ≺ r, with algebras A = E_{pq₁}, B = E_{q₁r}, C = E_{pq₂}, D = E_{q₂r} (legs a, b, c, d; ab = cd = k = k_{pr}), and M = E_{pr} = A ⊗ B = C ⊗ D — one algebra, two factorizations. The polygon equation on the diamond is σ^{pq₁} ⊗ σ^{q₁r} = σ^{pq₂} ⊗ σ^{q₂r}: **the coherent assignments on the diamond are exactly the common product states of the two factorizations** — pairs of product states, one per factorization, with a common value in D(M). The diamond's price is therefore a question the corpus can ask precisely: how many common product states does a pair of factorizations share, and which ones?

**Theorem HH (the diamond's price) [Derived here; the projective-geometry core is classical].**

**(i) White is always common** — Theorem FF restricted to the diamond: ω_M = ω_A ⊗ ω_B = ω_C ⊗ ω_D.

**(ii) The forced-intersection regime.** The pure products of the first factorization are the Segre variety Seg₁ = ℂP^{a−1} × ℂP^{b−1} ⊆ ℂP^{k−1} (dim a+b−2), those of the second its conjugate Seg₂ (dim c+d−2). If a+b+c+d−4 ≥ k−1, the two dimensions sum to at least the ambient dimension, and two projective subvarieties of ℂP^{n} with dimension sum at least n always meet [Established, classical]. So EVERY pair of factorizations with small legs admits a common PURE product — hence a pure coherent assignment on the diamond. The regime's boundary, in the corpus's own currency: **every qubit diamond** (all four legs M₂: 4 ≥ 3, with room to spare) **and every pair carrying a two-dimensional leg** ((2, b) against (2, b′): 2b + 2b′ − 4 ≥ 2bb′ − 1 whenever one leg is 2 — always) **is unconditionally rich**; square legs close at the 3|4 boundary ((3,3): 8 = 8, forced with a tight count; (4,4): 12 < 15, avoidable).

**(iii) The avoidable regime.** If a+b+c+d−4 < k−1, a general pair of factorizations admits NO common pure product — two projective subvarieties with dimension sum below the ambient dimension can be displaced apart, and generically are [Established, classical]. Pure coherent sections on such diamonds exist only for biased pairs — a nongeneric, measure-zero-type condition on the relative position of the two factorizations.

**(iv) The transversal collapse.** Call a pair transversal when white noise is the UNIQUE common product state. Then every coherent preparation section on the diamond is white on all four arrows. *Proof:* σ_A ⊗ τ_B = ω_M; take the B-marginal: Tr_B(σ_A ⊗ τ_B) = σ_A (τ_B is a state, trace 1) and Tr_B(ω_M) = Tr_B(ω_A ⊗ ω_B) = ω_A; so σ_A = ω_A, and symmetrically τ_B = ω_B, ω_C, ω_D. ∎ The branch point of the causal order, presented through a transversal diamond, forces the environment's fill to noise on every arrow of the branch.

**Proposition 84 (the CNOT census — the canonical biased pair, certified) [Derived here].** Let M₄ carry the standard qubit factorization and its CNOT conjugate (C ⊗ D = U(A ⊗ B)U†, U = CNOT, an involution). The common pure products are exactly |a⟩ ⊗ |b⟩ with a ∈ {|0⟩, |1⟩} (the Z eigenbasis) or b ∈ {|+⟩, |−⟩} (the X eigenbasis). *Proof:* a state is common iff it is a standard product whose CNOT image is again a standard product. Write a = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩, b = γ|0⟩ + δ|1⟩; CNOT maps |a⟩⊗|b⟩ to the state with coefficient matrix [[αγ, αδ], [βδ, βγ]] on the standard basis, which has rank 1 iff its determinant vanishes: αβ(γ² − δ²) = 0 — iff αβ = 0 (a a Z eigenstate) or γ² = δ² (b an X eigenstate). ∎ The hygiene instrument certifies the census over 82,944 grid probes: 4,008 common pure products found, every one on the registered boundary, the rank-1 test and the closed form agreeing everywhere. **The pair is biased, and the bias has a shape: the escaping families are the eigenlines on which the two factorizations' generating symmetries agree — the control axis CNOT preserves (Z) and the target axis its flip preserves (X).** The rich sections of a biased diamond live on whatever structure the two factorizations share.

**Proposition 83 (the support reduction) [Derived here].** Every common MIXED product state lives on a common product subspace: supp(σ_A ⊗ τ_B) = supp σ_A ⊗ supp τ_B, and if σ_A ⊗ τ_B = σ_C ⊗ τ_D then the two product subspace decompositions of the support coincide. *Proof:* the support projection of a tensor-product state is the tensor of the support projections, and equal states have equal supports. ∎

**The residue, filed.** Whether a pair in the avoidable regime (iii) can also kill every non-white common MIXED product — full transversality, making (iv) unconditional for such pairs — is left open. The pure half is closed; the mixed half is the cell's inheritance, with its heuristic on record: the mixed product loci are real semialgebraic sets of dimensions 2(a²−1) + 2(b²−1) and 2(c²−1) + 2(d²−1) inside the (k²−1)-dimensional real state space — thin from (3,4) upward, so full transversality should be generic there — but thinness is not emptiness, and the corpus files heuristics as heuristics. One connection worth the ledger: the 3|4 boundary of (ii)/(iii) sits at the same small-dimension edge where Theorem F's exceptional family lives ((4, 2, 7), (6, 3, 17) — the diophantine-legal intercepts) — a resonance of the formal line's two small-dimension phenomena, noted as resonance and nothing more.
