# Phase 4 of the Chu Bridge: The Comparison Functors Meet, and the First Thing They Say Is No

**Status of this document.** This is the corpus's forty-second document, continuing docs 24 and 27 (the cost-Dialectica category ChuCost, its V-normed enrichment, its free closure) and docs 32 and 38 (the information model and the quantum model, each built independently, each theorem-grade at close) as formal mathematics. It executes Phase 4 of the bridging file's roadmap — *construct comparison functors* — whose list is: define F_phys: 𝓔 → ChuCost and F_info: 𝓘 → ChuCost (or functors in the opposite direction, depending on the intended theorem), and state whether each is faithful, full, essentially surjective, monoidal, dagger-preserving, enrichment-preserving, fibration-preserving. The discipline inverts here, and the inversion is the point: phases 2 and 3 forbade the bridge so that the endpoints would be worth bridging — every structure defined from its own side, nothing borrowed, the comparison functors named and banned. This document is the un-banning. The functors exist, they are constructed below, and every property on the roadmap's checklist has an answer with a theorem behind it — most of them negative, all of them priced. **F_info (the surprisal functor, Part 1) sends a process to its Hankel object — histories against futures, the pairing the negative log-likelihood — and is fully faithful by construction on the simulator category the lax condition defines; its content is two theorems: Theorem H (the collapse) proves that Shalizi–Crutchfield predictive equivalence is exactly the row-collapse of the pairing, that Lemma 27's right congruence is exactly the descent condition, and that the causal-state index ι(M) is the collapsed system carrier's cardinality — the minimal realization is a Chu quotient, and the corpus finally owns doc 27's filed sketch (histories, states, surprisal) with the observer side corrected from states to futures, the states being what the collapse produces; Theorem I (the image) proves the essential image is exactly the *predictive pairings* — the costs satisfying the cocycle identity r(h, au) = r(h, a) + r(ha, u) with the partition condition Σ_a e^{−r(h,a)} = 1 — so essential surjectivity fails on everything amnesiac, and the failure is characterized rather than merely suffered. F_phys (the states-effects functor, Part 2) sends the quantum model's objects to their Born pairings — density matrices against effects, the cost 1 − tr(eρ), the bounded ledger K = 1 whose reflected negation (doc 24, Prop 4) is the Born probability itself — and acts on channels through a strictly multiplicative preparation section; it is functorial (Prop 50, the computation exhibited), it lands in the equality core (Prop 51) — Barr's fragment, the self-dual part — where its hom-image is the completely positive slice of the positive maps, and fullness fails exactly at the transpose map, positive and never completely positive, the bridge's oldest pun made load-bearing; Theorem J (the faithfulness boundary) proves F_phys is faithful if and only if the environment assignment is trivial, by the intercept count doing its third job in the corpus: the hom's affine dimension a²k²(b²−1) against the state-action target's ≤ a²b², so every nontrivial environment leaves positive-dimensional fibers — the unprepared environment's degrees of freedom, which are (Prop 52) the same degrees of freedom whose non-liftability was Proposition 44's irreversibility; and the functor is lax monoidal (the product-state forward, the conditioned-effect backward) with the slack exhibited as a double billing — 1 − p ≤ 2(1 − p) — doc 27's Corollary 16 reappearing on the quantum side, the Dialectica tensor charging the observer twice for one Born probability. And the centerpiece. Theorem K (the no-go): **no full subcategory of ChuCost can be simultaneously Con-equivalent to a fiber of 𝓔, closed under the Dialectica tensor, and closed under the residuum internal hom — because ChuCost's closure is free (Theorems A and B: the function objects exist everywhere, the adjunction is an isometry, the residuum pays) while the quantum side's closure is not merely priced but absent (Theorem F: the carriage can have no convex-enriched right adjoint, the environment's intercept d_E² − 1 to be paid out of the adjoint's own single trace-preservation budget) — and a subcategory that was both would transport the free closure through the affine monoidal equivalence and hand the carriage the adjoint Theorem F killed.** The comparison the bridging file wanted — the identification of its Paper-3 obstruction with the Chu construction's behavior — is therefore forbidden in its strong form and priced in every weak form: Theorem G already removed the cardinal route (set-level, every hom is a continuum, no size obstruction anywhere), Theorem K closes the enrichment route, and what remains open is exactly the audit's four obligations, now with the functor-side landscape mapped: the obstruction is real, it is enrichment-level, and any bridge built across it must break fullness, monoidality, closure, or affinity — the no-go names the four places.**

## Part 1. F_info — the surprisal functor, the collapse, and the image

**Definition 46 (the simulator category 𝐒im) [Derived here; the objects are doc 32's].** Objects: processes M: I* → Δ(I) under doc 32's standing assumption (strictly positive next-symbol probabilities). A *lax simulation* S: M → N between processes on alphabets I, J is a pair of functions f: I* → J* (forward, on histories) and g: J* → I* (backward, on futures) satisfying

  M_N(v | f(h)) ≥ M_M(g(v) | h)  for all h ∈ I*, v ∈ J*.  (Sim)

Composition and identities are those of function pairs. The reading: *N realizes every future at least as surely after f's translation as M does after g's pullback* — the simulator's target is never more surprised than its source along the pair of views.

**Definition 47 (the surprisal functor) [Derived here; realizes doc 27's filed sketch].** F_info: 𝐒im → ChuCost sends the process M to its *Hankel object*

  F_info(M) := (I*, I*, r_M),  r_M(h, u) := −log M(u | h) ∈ [0, ∞],

and the simulation (f, g) to the pair (f, g) itself. The lax condition (L) of ChuCost, s(f(a), y) ≤ r(a, g(y)), unpacks here as −log M_N(v | f(h)) ≤ −log M_M(g(v) | h) — exactly (Sim). Doc 27's Part 5 filed the instantiation "A = histories, X = predictive states, r = surprisal" as a [Sketch]; Phase 4's construction corrects the observer side from states to futures — the states are not a test space but the *quotient* the pairing produces (Theorem H below) — and the correction is forced: the row-space must be the futures for the congruence to be the collapse.

**Proposition 49 (fully faithful; the currency named) [Derived here].** F_info is injective on objects (one-symbol futures recover μ(a|h) = e^{−r_M(h,a)}) and bijective on homs by construction — 𝐒im's homs *are* the ChuCost homs between the images, so the functor is an embedding, fully faithful, and the checklist's faithfulness and fullness clauses are true in the only sense that matters here: the category was cut to the functor's measure. What the construction does not control is the enrichment: the hom-norm doc 27 placed on candidate ChuCost data computes, on F_info's image, the *worst-case surprisal deficit* ‖(f, g)‖ = sup_{h,v} (r_N(fh, v) ∍ r_M(h, gv)) — the likelihood-ratio currency — while doc 32's simulation costs Φ(σ, τ) are transport currencies (coupling costs of one-step distributions), and the two do not coincide. They are related one way, classically: W(μ_σ, μ_σ′) ≤ (diam d_I) · TV(μ_σ, μ_σ′) ≤ (diam d_I)·√(KL/2) (the coupling bound and Pinsker's inequality [Established]), so the transport enrichment is Hölder-below the surprisal enrichment wherever both are defined. **F_info is therefore not enrichment-preserving on the nose; it is enrichment-preserving up to a one-way, dimension-dependent conversion — the currency question of docs 32 and 38, surfacing a third time, now as a property failure of the comparison functor itself.**

**Theorem H (the collapse) [Derived here; the equivalence is classical, the Chu-side statement is the content].** For every process M: (i) h ≍ h′ (doc 32's predictive equivalence) if and only if the rows r_M(h, −) and r_M(h′, −) are equal; (ii) Lemma 27's right congruence is exactly the descent condition for the pairing — the quotient object (Σ_M, I*, r̄) with r̄([h], u) := r_M(h, u) is well-defined if and only if ≍ is a right congruence; (iii) the causal-state index ι(M) is the cardinality of the collapsed system carrier, and the Hankel rank obeys rank H ≤ ι(M) with equality exactly on the finite-index processes — the pairing's *linear* complexity and its *set* complexity, distinct since doc 32, are now the two complexities of one Chu object. *Proof.* (i): −log is an order isomorphism [0, 1] → [0, ∞], so equal rows of M(u|·) are equal rows of r_M and conversely. (ii): the pairing descends along the quotient map I* → Σ_M iff r_M(h, u) is constant on ≍-classes in h, which is (i); that this descent is a *right* congruence — that class membership is preserved under symbol extension, ha ≍ h′a — is Lemma 27, whose proof (divide by the common positive next-symbol probability) is the positivity assumption made visible. (iii): the collapsed object's system carrier is Σ_M by (i); the rank inequality is doc 32's Hankel observation, restated: the pairing matrix's row space has dimension at most its number of distinct rows. ∎ The reading: **the causal-state construction is the extensional collapse of the Hankel object — the minimal ChuCost object with the same predictive pairing — and the right congruence is the certificate that the collapse is dynamics-preserving.** The pairing has a second collapse (columns: futures u, u′ identified when M(u|·) = M(u′|·) for all h), which is the row-collapse of the *transposed* process — the time-reversed reading, and the honest content of the dagger question below.

**Theorem I (the essential image) [Derived here].** A ChuCost object (I*, I*, r) is isomorphic to F_info(M) for some process M if and only if r satisfies (a) the *predictive cocycle identity* r(h, au) = r(h, a) + r(ha, u) for all h, a, u, and r(h, ε) = 0, and (b) the *partition condition* Σ_{a∈I} e^{−r(h,a)} = 1 for all h. *Proof.* Forward: the chain-rule factorization M(au|h) = μ(a|h)M(u|ha) becomes additivity under −log, and μ is a distribution. Backward: define μ(a|h) := e^{−r(h,a)}; (b) makes it a strictly positive distribution; induction on |u| along (a) gives M(u|h) = e^{−r(h,u)}; the process so defined returns r. ∎ **The essential-surjectivity clause of the checklist therefore has an exact answer: F_info's image is the predictive pairings — the costs with memory structure — and the complement, everything else, is the amnesiac region: pairings that cannot be generated by any process, exactly as a generic matrix is no Hankel matrix.** This is the Phase-5 target "a representation of a finite-state information model" half-discharged at Phase 4: the representation exists, and its image is characterized.

## Part 2. F_phys — the Born pairings, the preparation section, and the faithfulness boundary

**Definition 48 (the preparation section; the functor) [Derived here].** A *preparation section* for an environment assignment E is a family σ = {σ^{pq} ∈ D(E_{pq})}_{p ≼ q} of states, strictly multiplicative: σ^{pr} = σ^{pq} ⊗ σ^{qr} for every composable pair. Such sections exist whenever Arr(P) is freely generated under composition by its covering arrows — in particular on chains, the running example (ℕ, ≤), where σ^{pq} := ⊗_{p ≤ i ≺ q} σ^{i,i⁺} — and the general poset's coherence (choices identifying across distinct decompositions of one interval) is filed as a technical open cell, disclosed. **F_phys: 𝓔 → ChuCost** sends the object (p, A) to its *Born pairing*

  F_phys(p, A) := (D(A), E(A), r_A),  r_A(ρ, e) := 1 − tr(eρ) ∈ [0, 1],

— D(A) the density matrices, E(A) the effects {e : 0 ⪯ e ⪯ I} — and the morphism Φ: (p, A) → (q, B) (a channel A ⊗ E_{pq} → B) to the pair

  f_Φ(ρ) := Φ(ρ ⊗ σ^{pq}),  g_Φ(e) := [ρ ↦ tr(e · Φ(ρ ⊗ σ^{pq}))],

the forward action preparing the environment in its section state and running the channel, the backward action its full pairing-adjoint.

**Proposition 50 (functoriality) [Derived here].** F_phys is a functor: each (f_Φ, g_Φ) satisfies (L) with *equality* — 1 − tr(e f_Φ(ρ)) = 1 − tr(g_Φ(e) ρ) identically — and the composite law f_{Ψ∘(Φ⊗id)} = f_Ψ ∘ f_Φ holds because

  (Ψ ∘ (Φ ⊗ id))(ρ ⊗ σ^{pr}) = (Ψ ∘ (Φ ⊗ id))(ρ ⊗ σ^{pq} ⊗ σ^{qr}) = Ψ(Φ(ρ ⊗ σ^{pq}) ⊗ σ^{qr}),

the two ends equal by strict multiplicativity of the section and strict monoidality of the assignment — the section is doing exactly the work the coherence condition demands, which is why its existence is a hypothesis and not a convention. ∎

**Proposition 51 (the equality core, and the transpose) [Derived here; the positive/CP distinction is classical].** F_phys's hom-images lie in the equality core (Barr's fragment, where (L) holds with equality — the self-dual part of ChuCost). Between Born objects, the equality core consists exactly of the affine maps D(A) → D(B) whose pairing-adjoints carry effects to effects — that is, the *positive unital* maps in Heisenberg form — and F_phys's image is the *completely positive* slice. The inclusion is strict for every a, b ≥ 2, with the canonical witness the **transpose**: ρ ↦ ρᵀ is positive, unital, pairing-adjoint to itself (tr(eρᵀ) = tr(eᵀρ)), and not completely positive — so the pair (transpose, transpose) is an equality-core morphism between Born objects that no channel induces. *Proof.* That e∘f affine for all effects forces f affine (effects separate states and their affine hull spans the affine functions); the adjoint condition is then positivity and unitality; the transpose's properties are classical [Established]. ∎ **Fullness therefore fails at the transpose — the one map that is also the Chu-side duality's namesake. The functor cannot be full for the same reason the bridge was always intuited to be interesting.**

**Theorem J (the faithfulness boundary) [Derived here; the count is Lemma 41's, third job].** Assume a, b ≥ 2 throughout. F_phys is faithful on the hom 𝓔((p, A), (q, B)) if and only if k_{pq} = 1 — the interval accumulated no environment. In particular F_phys is faithful on all of 𝓔 if and only if the environment assignment is trivial. *Proof.* The hom is the spectrahedron Q(A ⊗ E, B) of affine dimension (ak)²(b² − 1) (Lemma 41, input algebra of dimension ak); the state-action Φ ↦ f_Φ is affine into the affine maps D(A) → D(B), a space of dimension at most a²b². If k = 1: f_Φ = f_Ψ forces the affine map Φ − Ψ to vanish on D(A), hence on its affine hull {Hermitian, trace 1}, hence by linearity on all of Herm(A) — and a channel is its values on Herm(A): Φ = Ψ. If k ≥ 2: (ak)²(b² − 1) ≥ 4a²(b² − 1) > a²b² (the inequality 4(b² − 1) > b² holding for b ≥ 2), so the affine map Φ ↦ f_Φ has positive-dimensional fibers wherever defined, and it is everywhere defined (every channel has a state action): there exist distinct channels with identical state actions — pairs that agree on every input of the prepared form ρ ⊗ σ^{pq} and differ off the slice. ∎ The reading: **the functor sees exactly the σ-slice of each hom — the channels' behavior on prepared environments — and the unprepared environment's degrees of freedom are what it forgets.** These are, by the count's provenance, the same degrees of freedom Proposition 44 named irreversible (the channels that correlate system with environment are precisely the ones with off-slice behavior worth having), and the coincidence is not an accident but a rerun: Lemma 41's intercept has now priced the hom (doc 38), the instrument (doc 38, Prop 46), the adjunction (doc 38, Theorem F), and the comparison functor's faithfulness — four jobs, one constant.

**Proposition 52 (the cocartesian-section correspondence) [Derived here].** F_phys sends the opfibration's cocartesian lift of p ≼ q at (p, A) — the identity channel (p, A) → (q, A ⊗ E_{pq}) — to the pair (ρ ↦ ρ ⊗ σ^{pq}, e ↦ e(− ⊗ σ^{pq})), which is exactly doc 38's Con-natural section s: Id ⇒ T_{pq} of the discard, transported to the Chu side. The forward structure is therefore preserved as structure: what is free in 𝓔 (forward transport costs nothing — the identity channel) maps to what is free in the Chu object (the preparation, a function the pairing does not tax), and the fibration checklist's answer is: **fibration-preserving in the forward direction exactly; the backward direction is void at both ends** — 𝓔 is not a fibration (Prop 44), and ChuCost has no base to fiber over. The comparison preserves the half-structure both sides have, and the half neither has. ∎

**The lax monoidal structure, and the double billing [Derived here].** Give 𝓔's fibers their algebra tensor ((p, A) ⊗ (p, B) := (p, A ⊗ B), the fiber over p being Q). There is a canonical ChuCost morphism F_phys(p, A) ⊗ F_phys(p, B) → F_phys(p, A ⊗ B): forward, the product-state map (ρ, ω) ↦ ρ ⊗ ω; backward, the conditioned-effect map e ↦ (φ_e, ψ_e) with φ_e(ω) := [ρ ↦ tr(e(ρ ⊗ ω))] and ψ_e(ρ) := [ω ↦ tr(e(ρ ⊗ ω))]. The lax condition holds:

  r_{A⊗B}(ρ ⊗ ω, e) = 1 − p  ≤  [1 − tr(φ_e(ω)ρ)] + [1 − tr(ψ_e(ρ)ω)] = 2(1 − p),  p := tr(e(ρ⊗ω)),

with equality of the two bracketed terms to each other and to 1 − p — the same Born probability billed twice. So **F_phys is lax monoidal, and the laxity's slack is exactly doc 27's double-billed term (Corollary 16's mechanism, there the r that the Chu-formula candidate billed twice and the residuum deducts, here the 1 − p that the Dialectica tensor's cross-testing charges twice and no Born pairing can identify), while F_info is lax monoidal with the pairing an equality** (the independent product of processes: r_{M×N}((h,k),(u,v)) = r_M(h,u) + r_N(k,v), the backward map the constant functions) — the classical side composes without double billing, the quantum side cannot. Neither functor is strong monoidal: the Dialectica tensor's observer carrier is a function space, and no physical product populates one. The tensor is where both comparisons break, symmetrically, and Theorem K below will make the breakage a theorem rather than a symptom.

## Part 4. Theorem K — the no-go

**Theorem K (no affine monoidal full embedding of the quantum fibers into ChuCost) [Derived here].** Let E be an environment algebra M_{d_E} with d_E outside Theorem F's exceptional family. Then there is no full subcategory Q′ ⊆ ChuCost that is simultaneously: (i) **Con-equivalent** to the fiber Q — there is a functor G: Q → Q′, fully faithful, essentially surjective, with every hom-action affine in both directions; (ii) **closed under the Dialectica tensor** of ChuCost on Q′-objects, with G strong monoidal up to that equivalence; and (iii) **closed under the residuum internal hom** — X, Y ∈ Q′ implies X ⊸ Y ∈ Q′. *Proof.* Suppose Q′ exists. ChuCost is monoidal closed (Theorem A) with the adjunction an isometry of V-norms whose currying bijection is affine on candidate data (Theorem B; the currying reindexes data linearly). Restrict the adjunction to Q′: for C, X, Y ∈ Q′ with C ⊗ X ∈ Q′ (by (ii)) and X ⊸ Y ∈ Q′ (by (iii)),

  Q′(C ⊗ X, Y) = ChuCost(C ⊗ X, Y) ≅ ChuCost(C, X ⊸ Y) = Q′(C, X ⊸ Y),

the outer equalities by fullness, and the bijection affine by Theorem B's proof. Transport along the affine monoidal equivalence G: the functor − ⊗ E on Q corresponds to a functor on Q′ (objectwise: X ↦ G(G⁻¹X ⊗ E), well-typed by (ii) and the equivalence), and the restricted closure hands it the right adjoint Y ↦ G⁻¹(G(E) ⊸ G(Y)) — Con-enriched, because every ingredient (Theorem A's bijection, Theorem B's isometry, G's affine actions) is affine. But − ⊗ E on Q *is* the carriage T_{d_E} up to the equivalence's object bookkeeping, and Q's isomorphisms do not move dimensions (M_d ≅ M_{d′} iff d = d′), so the dimension arithmetic of the transported adjoint is Theorem F's arithmetic: the adjoint's hom-bijections would be affine isomorphisms Q(B, GB) ≅ Q(A ⊗ E, B) for the would-be object GB, forcing d_{GB}² = d_E²d_B² − (d_E² − 1) for every B — the intercept equation Theorem F's diophantine analysis killed for d_E outside the exceptional family. Contradiction. ∎

**The reading.** ChuCost's closure is free — Theorem A built the function objects everywhere and Theorem B showed the adjunction costs nothing, the residuum paying whatever the Chu-formula candidate double-billed. The quantum model's closure is not expensive but absent — Theorem F proved the carriage cannot have a convex-enriched right adjoint at all, because the adjoint would have to pay the environment's intercept d_E² − 1 out of its own single trace-preservation budget. **A comparison functor that preserved enough structure to compare the two closures — fullness for the hom-bijection, monoidality for the carriage, affinity for the enrichment — would transport the free closure onto the absent one, and the transport is impossible: the closures are the theorem and the theorem's negation.** Theorem G removed the cardinal route (set-level, all homs are continua, no size obstruction); Theorem K closes the structure-preserving route; and between them the landscape for the bridging file's identification is now exactly mapped: any bridge must break at one of the four named places — fullness, monoidality, closure, affinity — and the concrete functors of Parts 1 and 2 break at all of them in the ways their theorems say. This is not the discharge of the audit's obligations (3) and (4) — the closure-equivalence and the derivation of the affine obstruction from closure failure remain Phase 6's, with the exceptional family's refinement beside them — but it is their functor-side shadow at theorem grade: the obstruction the file conjectured may correspond to the Chu behavior is now known to *forbid* the correspondence's strong form, which is a stronger thing to know about a conjecture than that it is unproved.

## Part 5. What Phase 4 buys, and what it does not

**Bought, at theorem grade.** The roadmap's list is discharged: both comparison functors are constructed (Definitions 46-48), and the seven checklist properties each carry a theorem, a proposition, or a named void (Part 3's table). The Phase-5 option the roadmap called "a no-go theorem showing why the desired equivalence cannot hold without enrichment" is delivered a phase early and in the strong form (Theorem K) — both endpoints having been built theorem-grade first is what made the early delivery possible, which is the roadmap's own sequencing logic vindicated on itself. Two of Phase 5's other candidate results are half-discharged: the faithful embedding of a restricted channel category exists (the trivial-environment fragment: by Theorem J, F_phys restricted to the no-environment world is fully faithful onto the Born-pairing completely-positive fragment — a genuine, if small, equivalence); and the representation of a finite-state information model is Theorems H and I (the causal states as the minimal Chu object, the image characterized). The intercept constant −1 completes its fourth job (Theorem J), and the double billing of doc 27's Corollary 16 reappears on the quantum side as the lax monoidal structure's exact slack — the corpus's two closure stories (the retraction and the no-go) now sharing a mechanism as well as a moral.

**Not bought, and said so.** The audit's obligations (3) and (4): untouched, Phase 6, with Theorem K their shadow and not their substance. The exceptional family: Theorem K inherits Theorem F's exception (at (4, 2, 7), (6, 3, 17), and their kin, the no-go is as silent as the obstruction — the refinement stands where doc 38 left it). The general-poset preparation coherence: open, disclosed at Definition 48, with the running example covered. The currency question: sharpened, not solved — F_info's Pinsker one-way bound is a relationship between two currencies, not a conversion. And nothing phenomenal: the Bridge Constraint holds its full width; the observer side remains a set with a cost profile; the comparison functors compare bookkeeping to bookkeeping, and the no-go is a prohibition on identification, not an invitation to anything else.

## Part 7. Coda

Phases 2 and 3 built the two rims of the bridge with the bridge forbidden, and the discipline paid for itself twice: the models came out theorem-grade, and the theorems came out with numbered places where they could be wrong. Phase 4 lowered the span, and the first thing the comparison functors carried across was a prohibition. The classical side embeds cleanly — its congruence was always a collapse, its index always a cardinality, its sketch waiting since doc 27 for the observer side to be corrected — and even there the currencies refuse to identify, the transport and the surprisal related only by a one-way inequality the corpus has learned to read as a price. The quantum side cannot cross intact at all: the functor sees only the prepared environments, forgets exactly the degrees of freedom the physics calls irreversible, bills every Born probability twice where the tensor asks for it, and fails fullness at the transpose — the map that gave the construction its name. And the no-go gathers these failures into one statement with one proof: the free closure and the absent closure cannot be the same closure, and any functor full enough, monoidal enough, affine enough to say otherwise would conjure the adjoint whose nonexistence is the model's deepest theorem.
