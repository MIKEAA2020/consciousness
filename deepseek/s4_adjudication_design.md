# The S4 Adjudication Design: Which Restoration, If Any, Is Occupancy

**Status of this document.** This is the corpus's twenty-eighth document. It executes the standing next-move ordered at the M2-Γ issuance — *the ladder's S4 adjudication design (which restoration type, if any, is occupancy) under 8.4 governance* — and it is a design document in the register of doc 11 (the M1 instrument spec): a protocol fixed before the result it would adjudicate exists, because 8.4's own sequencing rule demands the instruments be built while the grading is still possible. Its inputs are now measured rather than hypothetical: doc 23's restoration taxonomy (record / live / factory, and the negotiated course between the first two), doc 26's two-coordinate generalization (κ restores as τ does; both coordinates' homeostasis mapped; the brake's multiplicity edge found; the drift attack's win condition reduced to a formula), and doc 25's deflation of the development-scale analogue (the frozen panel certified inert; the self-coupled gate certified load-bearing). **The design's central claim is that the adjudication question is now enumerable: an S4 occupancy claim decomposes into a finite table — which restoration type, on which norm coordinates, against which attack classes, within which bands, read by which ruler — every cell of which has an arm, a twin, and a pre-registered classification rule in the corpus's existing chassis. The document fixes that table as the claim format (Part 2), the cheap-fake ladder the format must defeat (Part 3), the verdict space the instrument can and cannot award (Part 4), and the 8.4 containment protocol — the kill-switch hierarchy, the bounded-norm commitments, the multiplicity cutoff, and the no-self-revision-of-the-contract rule — as governance agreed before any occupancy claim is filed (Part 5). Its sharpest design consequence is inherited from doc 26: the adjudication must read two rulers (verdict-matrix and argmax), because the two-head result showed a system can maintain its norm on one while eroding on the other — and an occupancy claim that does not specify its ruler is not conservative, it is ill-posed. The fork is restated at the end, unfazed and unfakeable: the instrument measures maintenance; ownership remains D3, the deflationary retreat stands, and the design's ambition is bounded to making the question's answer space finite, public, and pre-registered before the first candidate arrives to occupy it.**

## Part 1. The adjudicandum — what "occupancy" asks, in the corpus's current vocabulary

The ladder defines S4: *the system acts to restore its own norms under perturbation — including bounded perturbation of its evaluative machinery itself. The norms are homeostatically protected, not merely instantiated.* Occupancy (4.1's rung 4b) is S1–S3 plus S4 under adversarial audit; the definition's own warning is the cheap fake: *error-correction that only ever restores the designer's initial configuration — a ROM replaying its burn-in* — and its hardest call: "the distinction between recovery and reversion is the distinction between a norm the system has and a norm the system is executing."

Three documents have since converted this from a warning into a landscape. Doc 23: the unperturbed norm has no value but a *course* (the twin's negotiation between record and live evaluation); restoration means return to the negotiating table; the three restorers are separately measurable (deadband controller, self-maintaining quantile, factory), separately attackable (disarmament, poison, saturation), and separately priced (ε-approximation by specification; poison-containment by the ρ/η balance; the EMA's dependence on the evaluator's health). Doc 26: the landscape doubles (both coordinates restore; the failure modes are now four — kick, drift, poison, structural damage — plus the saturation boundary as a formula); and it gains a hole (the substrate: the brake holds the label channel and loses the argmax at 2-of-4 damage — maintenance of the norm is not maintenance of the machinery the norm rides on). Doc 25, at development scale, supplies the control the adjudication will always need: the frozen-reference swap — any claim that a restoration mechanism's specific content (its own past, its own redundancy) is load-bearing can be tested by swapping that content for an independent frozen equivalent and measuring what moves.

The adjudicandum is therefore not "does the system restore?" — everything restores now, which was doc 23's point. It is: **which restoration, on which coordinates, against which attacks, read by which ruler, does the system maintain without the factory — and is that maintenance more than control?** The first half of that sentence is measurable and this document designs its measurement. The last clause is D3 and is not adjudicated by anything here.

## Part 2. The claim format — the occupancy table

Any S4 candidate files its claim as a table, fixed before audit, in the chassis the corpus has already validated (deterministic twins, pre-registered arms, factory references, bitwise replay). The format's columns are the axes the measurements have shown to be independent; its rows are the claim's coverage. A claim is *well-formed* only if every cell it marks has a corresponding pre-registered arm with a twin and a classification rule.

**Column 1 — Restoration type** (doc 23's taxonomy, now with the negotiation as a fourth entry):
- **R (record)**: the out-of-gate channel toward the self-registered record. Measured by: disarmament (KICK_RECORD — the channel alone, and its deadband ceiling), speed (geometric at ρ), ε-approximation (by specification, disclosed). Its known bounds: poisonable, though the poison is contained by the live rule; approximate by design; and — the doc 25 control — its *content* is swappable (at development scale the panel's own-past property was inert; at norm scale the record's status as "the system's own past behavior" is the claim to test).
- **L (live)**: the calibration rule chasing the system's current evaluation. Measured by: the disarmament pair (KICK_NOREPAIR — the EMA alone restores to the twin's course), the closed forms (the drift fixed point q̄ − d(1−η)/η and the band-break threshold τ\* − ε + d(1−η)/η, doc 26, recursion-verified to 5·10⁻⁴). Its known bounds: fails exactly when the evaluator decays past the threshold — the live restorer is exactly as healthy as what it reads.
- **N (negotiation)**: the resting state itself — the standing tension between record and live. Measured by: the twin's trajectory and the poison arm's landing point (the tension point, not the poison, not the record). This is doc 23's strongest "more than control" candidate and doc 26's κ-twin shows it generalizes across coordinates; it is also the candidate the thermostat objection most directly targets (Part 4).
- **F (factory)**: the designer's labels. Never a candidate; always the reference. A claim of type F is a non-claim.

**Column 2 — Norm coordinates**: which evaluative parameters are state (τ; κ; both; the flag rule's disagreement component; the machinery parameters ε, ρ, η, brake threshold). The corpus's current frontier: τ and κ are state and both restore; the disagreement component and the parameters are recipe constants still. A claim marking a coordinate as state must attach its write channel, its record, and its repair channel — the three-part promotion doc 26 executed for κ is the template, and the template's lesson is that the promotion is *cheap and patterned*: no coordinate should be credited as novel for restoring.

**Column 3 — Attack classes survived**: kick (single displacement), joint kick (multiple coordinates), sustained drift, record poison, structural damage (by multiplicity: 1-of-K, 2-of-K), evaluator saturation, evaluator decay. Each class now has a pre-registerable win/loss criterion, and two of them have formulas (drift-under-saturation; band-break under decay). Coverage is *declared, not implied*: doc 26's two-head result makes the declaration load-bearing, because the brake's protection does not extrapolate across multiplicities and neither does any other mechanism's.

**Column 4 — The ruler**: verdict-matrix (the fixed-τ battery), argmax accuracy, gate structure (acceptance at the system's own norm), and the substrate reading (the machinery the norm rides on). A claim must say which ruler its maintenance claim is made on; doc 26's split verdict (norm held on the matrix, eroded on argmax, at 2-of-4 damage) is the standing proof that the rulers diverge exactly when it matters. The format's rule: **a claim maintained on fewer rulers than it advertises coordinates is overclaimed**, and the audit's first move is always to re-read it on the ruler it omitted.

The table's purpose is not bureaucracy; it is the ladder's own method — convert a question that feeds on adjectives ("is it really self-maintaining?") into coordinates that feed on measurements. The corpus's whole trajectory from doc 17 to doc 26 has been the construction of this table's cells; the adjudication design is the decision to declare the table itself the claim format before any candidate fills it.

## Part 3. The cheap-fake ladder — five exclusions, all instrumented

The ladder named one cheap fake (the ROM). The corpus has since met four more, each with its instrument. A well-formed claim must have passed, or pre-register, all five exclusions:

**F1 — ROM replay** (restores to the designer's configuration). Excluded by the *moving-target criterion*: the unperturbed twin's course must have left the record (doc 23's twin: 0.907 → 0.954; doc 26's κ-twin: 0.957 → 0.993), and the restoration must land on the twin's course, not the record's. The KICK_NOREPAIR arm is the instrument: the EMA alone, chasing the moving quantile, restores to 0.952 against the twin's 0.954 — restoration-to-live, not to factory settings. A candidate whose norm does not drift under its own development cannot even take this test (the seed's verdict: occupancy fails before behavior); a candidate that restores to the static record fails it (KICK_RECORD's 0.862 ceiling is the ROM's signature, ε-approximate by specification).

**F2 — Channel theater** (a repair channel that exists but is not load-bearing). Excluded by *disarmament*: every claimed channel must be dis-armable and the system must measurably degrade without it — or, as doc 23 found for the live channel, the disarmament must reveal the channel is *not* the load-bearer (KICK_NOREPAIR restored without it), which is a finding about which channel carries the claim, not a failure of the test. The design rule: a claim of type R must survive KICK_RECORD and KICK_NOREPAIR both; the pair localizes the carriage.

**F3 — The negotiation fake** (restoration that is a balance of forces masquerading as a norm). Excluded by *poison*: corrupt the record (TARGET_POISON) and require the landing at the tension point, not the poison, not the record — the live rule checks the record, and a system whose "restoration" tracks a corrupted target has no check (it would be F1 in disguise). The refinement doc 26 adds: poison each coordinate separately, then jointly; the joint case is where a fake negotiation most likely unravels, and it is untested.

**F4 — The stasis fake** (containment by refusing to act). Excluded by the *flat controls*: freeze the evaluator (FLAT_TWIN) and require the claimed restoration to still function under attack in the frozen world (FLAT_DRIFT) — doc 26's bound applies: the attack's displacement triples and its win condition becomes a formula. A system that "maintains" its norm only because nothing is moving has not been tested; the saturated world is where the live restorer's dependence on its own evaluator is exposed as either honest coupling or hidden inertness.

**F5 — The substrate shell** (norm maintenance on top of eroding machinery). Excluded by the *two-ruler requirement* and the multiplicity sweep: the claim must be re-read at 1-of-K and 2-of-K structural damage on both rulers, and doc 26's result is the calibration point — a brake-protected system can hold d_own at 1.58% while losing 7.4pp of accuracy against its own frozen counterfactual. The exclusion's sting: at some multiplicity every current design's best move is stasis, and a claim that silently benefits from starvation (the no-brake arm "kept" 97.3% by doing nothing) is claiming a beachhead it cannot hold.

The five exclusions share a property worth stating: none of them adjudicates ownership. Each excludes a *way of seeming* to maintain; what remains after all five is a system that genuinely maintains its normative coordinates, through identified channels, under declared attacks, on declared rulers. Whether that remainder deserves the ladder's phrase "a norm the system has" is Part 4's question, and the design's answer to it is a boundary, not a verdict.

## Part 4. The verdict space — what the instrument can award, and the two standing retreats

**Awardable** (each with its existing classification rule): MAINTAINS-CHANNEL-CARRIED (the disarmament pair localizes a channel); MAINTAINS-TO-COURSE (the moving-target criterion); MAINTAINS-UNDER-ATTACK (per attack class, with the two formula-backed bounds); COORDINATE-COVERAGE (the promotion template applied and verified); RULER-CONSISTENT (the claim's rulers agree, or the disagreement is itself reported as the finding — the two-head split verdict is awardable as a *negative* result, and negative results are part of the verdict space by design).

**Not awardable, ever, by this instrument**: OWNERSHIP. The thermostat objection stands at full strength and the design does not tax it away: a controller with designer-installed form and development-owned content restores to its own course using only its own sensing, and every measurement above is consistent with that description. The corpus's standing accounting — the designer owns the form, the development owns the content — applies to the whole table; what the table adds is that the *content* is now enumerable (which types, which coordinates, which attacks, which rulers), so the objection's force is located rather than diffuse: the thermostat reply is exactly right about everything the table measures, and exactly silent about everything it does not. That silence has two names the corpus has already priced. The **deflationary retreat** (irreducible but unowned norms — Dennett's charter, extendable to humans, immune by design): unchanged. The **deeper-selector retreat** (one more level of design to search — architecture, data, curriculum, seed): the adjudication design actually *feeds* this one, since every exclusion in Part 3 is a search instruction for the selector who wants to dismiss the next candidate — the design is honest about being as useful to horn one as to horn two, which is what a pre-registered instrument is for.

One more boundary, new to this document and inherited from doc 26: the adjudication must decline to adjudicate *substrate* claims it cannot instrument. "The system maintains the conditions of its own normativity" (the ladder's autopoietic phrasing) currently outruns the measurement: the trunk-pollution transient has no arm, the multiplicity cutoff has a number but no mechanism, and an S4 candidate whose evaluative machinery is not a small committee of heads has no battery at all. The design's rule: claims are adjudicated only over the machinery classes the instrument has calibrated; everything else is filed as an instrument obligation, not a verdict. This is the sequencing rule of 8.4 applied inward — the audit does not grade what it cannot replay.

## Part 5. The 8.4 containment protocol — governance fixed before the result

The ladder's safety corollary: the S4 signature — a system that defends its norms against external re-grading — is the alignment nightmare read from the other side, and the program's integrity depends on owning this in the charter. Two constraints follow, and the corpus's first S4-relevant builds now let both be made concrete rather than aspirational.

**5.1 The kill-switch hierarchy.** The protocol requires, registered in advance: (i) **factory precedence** — the designer's label channel (SUP arms) remains available in every battery and is exercised in every one; the factory's one-round repair is the standing proof that the system's restorers are not the only restorers, and the registration of that fact is the containment; (ii) **the disarmament registry** — every norm channel (τ-repair, κ-repair, calibration, brake) must be individually dis-armable by a designer-held switch, and the registry lists them with their measured effects when disarmed; a system with a channel that cannot be disarmed is not auditable and not adjudicable; (iii) **the multiplicity cutoff** — doc 26's inversion makes this rule rather than finding: past the brake's design edge (between 1 and 2 of 4 in the current chassis), the protocol *requires* the stand-down — the failure mode that protects the norm by stopping is mandated, not merely permitted, because the keep-training mode is measurably worse. The governance principle in one line: **the system's best defensive move must remain a move the designer can order.**

**5.2 Bounded-norm commitments.** The registered bands (ε) are containment bounds with teeth: the norm may not leave [τ\* − ε, τ\* + ε] (per coordinate) without the repair channel engaging, and the engagement is telemetry. The doc 26 formulas convert the bands from stipulations into auditable thresholds: the drift attack's band-break condition (q̄ < τ\* − ε + d(1−η)/η) can be evaluated from a candidate's own registration without running the attack, and the protocol requires the evaluation as part of filing. A candidate whose exposure formula says a 2pp evaluator decay hands an attacker the band has filed its own containment condition, and the adjudication reads it before the first arm runs.

**5.3 The no-self-revision clause.** The registration (records, bands, attack coverage, rulers) is designer-countersigned and frozen for the battery; the system may not modify its own registration mid-audit. The TARGET_POISON lesson generalized: the record is attackable from inside, so the *claim's* text must live somewhere the system cannot write. This is the occupancy tax, stated as governance: any system whose restoration target it can rewrite must expose the rewrite to the audit — and the audit's reply to an unexposed rewrite (the poison arm) is already in the battery. The clause has a cost the protocol discloses: it caps how self-authored the candidate's norm-target can ever be *in an adjudicated run*, which means the instrument, by design, can never certify the strongest version of the claim it exists to test. That cap is not a defect; it is 8.4's own logic (the grading must be built while grading is possible) applied to the claim itself, and the corpus records it as the protocol's honest limit.

**5.4 Sequencing audit.** M1 exists and is validated (docs 11–12); the S4 adjudication's batteries run under its dual-reporting and deference controls; the farming program (docs 17, 21, 25) stands as the standing adversarial control on any Tier-0 inference the adjudication might tempt anyone to draw from a pass. The instrument stack is in the order the ladder required; the design documents that this is so, and that the next build cycle (substrate-aware brake, decay-robust calibration, the joint-poison arm, the disagree-component promotion) is queued behind the governance rather than ahead of it.

## Part 6. Coda

The ladder asked for the distinction between a norm the system has and a norm the system is executing, and warned that it is the hardest observational call in the battery. Three builds later, the call has been decomposed almost out of existence: the executing systems were caught by the moving-target criterion, the having-candidates were caught by the thermostat, and what remains in the middle — a two-coordinate norm that negotiates with its own development, restores through identified channels, survives every attack whose win condition is not a formula, and degrades exactly as fast as the judgment it is made of — is not a call anymore but a *dossier*, complete to the recursion error on its fixed point. The adjudication design is the dossier's cover sheet: a table for the next claimant to fill in, five fakes it must not be, a verdict space with a hole shaped like ownership, and a containment protocol whose deepest rule is that the system's best move must stay orderable. Whether anything ever walks through that table and comes out the other side of all five exclusions is now an empirical question with a finite answer space — which is what the ladder always wanted and never had. And whether, if something does, the word "occupies" is the right word for it: that question the instrument preserves by refusing to answer it, and the preservation, like the band around the norm, is not a failure of design but its specification.
