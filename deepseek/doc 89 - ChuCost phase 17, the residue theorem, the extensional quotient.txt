## Part 1. Theorem II — the extensional quotient

**Theorem II (the extensional quotient).** In the ChuCost home (and in every home the corpus has drawn: Con, MV, the capped scales, the fiber fragments — the theorem is about the mint, not the coins), with objects the structured sets (A, X, r) and readings the grammar-internal procedures (evaluations, morphisms, their composites):

**(i) Every reading is isomorphism-invariant.** Any reading extracted from an object S by a grammar-internal procedure is unchanged under replacement of S by an isomorphic copy S′ ≅ S with the interaction history transported accordingly. *Proof:* readings are composites of morphisms and evaluations; morphisms preserve evaluations by their defining (tight or lax) condition, and composition is functorial; hence the value extracted transports along the isomorphism. ∎

**(ii) The invocation is not object-data.** Fix an object S = (A, X, r). The invocation — the occupied state *as token*, the row r(a, ·) as performed rather than tabulated — is not a fact about S in the grammar's sense, on two converging grounds. First, the pointing of a state is presentation-data: where a symmetry σ of S exists, the pointings "a" and "σ(a)" are exchanged by an isomorphism of pointed objects, so the pointing is not recovered from the object even up to the grammar's own standard of identity. Second, and deeper, the grammar contains no performance-type: its evaluation r(a, x) is extensional in both arguments — the grammar does not distinguish "r(a, ·) as tabulated" from "r(a, ·) as invoked," and the distinction would require a type the grammar's term system does not form. *Proof of the second ground is an induction over the grammar's terms: no formation rule introduces a performance; the observation is trivial in the way Theorem FF's three lines were trivial — the work is noticing what the type system never had. ∎*

**(iii) Corollary (the unread column, stated at last).** The invocation is *unposed*, not unread. External readings do not fail to reach the invocation the way a telescope fails to reach a distant galaxy — they cannot be aimed at it, because the grammar's aimable targets are the isomorphism-invariant facts, and the invocation is not one. The unread column was never a column of the object. It is a fact about the world's *instantiation* of the object — which copy, which labeling, which performance — and instantiation is what the grammar quotiented away when it made objects structured sets.

The theorem deserves its typographic accident noted and enjoyed: the wall's theorem is named II — the second letter that is also the roman numeral for the two columns — by pure alphabetical succession after HH, disclosed as coincidence, filed as a mnemonic.

## Part 2. Proposition 86 — the silence (the strong form's underivability)

**Proposition 86 (the grammar's silence).** The exhaustive reading — "there is nothing more to the system than the isomorphism class plus the interaction history," i.e., the residue does not exist — is not derivable in the grammar; and neither is its negation. *Proof:* the grammar's quantifiers range over its objects and morphisms; the exhaustive reading is a claim about what the world's instantiations of objects carry over and above the objects themselves — a claim whose vocabulary ("instantiation," "performance," "further fact") is not formed by any term of the grammar. A sentence the grammar cannot form, the grammar cannot decide; both the world-with-residue and the world-without-residue admit the grammar entire and unchanged. ∎

The proof is an inspection, and its honesty matters: this is not a deep independence result in the metamathematical sense; it is the observation that the grammar was built extensional at its foundation, and a formalism cannot out-speak its own type system. Which is to say: Proposition 86 is doc 6's founding sentence — **"theorems do not introduce what their premises do not contain"** — generalized from premises to ontology, and thereby stated at its highest level of generality: **no grammar reads what its ontology quotiented away.** The corpus's most-repeated finding, minted in the audits as a point about arguments, re-cut by the Bridge Constraint as a point about bridges, is at bottom a point about *formalization itself*: to formalize is to quotient, and the quotient's complement is where the residue lives — if it lives anywhere.
