# The Load-Bearing Wall Under Load — Stress-Testing S2 and Attacking the Audit-Completeness Conjecture from Both Sides

**Status of this document.** The endogeny-ladder program named its own weak points in advance: S2 — non-reducibility of the norm, audited by a designer-blind adversary — is the wall every candidate fails first, and the audit-completeness conjecture is horn one's terminal weapon against it. The standing instruction was issued at the program's publication: stress-test S2 against the unbounded auditor, and attack the conjecture from either side. This document executes both orders, and finds they are one order: the stress test's outcome is an attack on the conjecture, the conjecture's defense is a stress test of S2, and the two questions resolve into a single finding about what the instrument can and cannot ever measure. The user's designation of the conjecture as *the load-bearing open question* is confirmed in the formal sense: remove it, and both horns' terminal prizes — the M4 fixed-point build, the completeness theorem — collapse into the same undifferentiated charter-slinging the corpus has catalogued since its first audit.

**Headline answer.** S2, as originally stated, is **not well-defined against an auditor with unbounded compute** — but the failure is instructive enough to constitute the program's most important result since the S-conditions themselves. Read literally, "not reconstructible from (design, objective, history)" is unsatisfiable by any trained system whatsoever, because a constant-length program — replay the training run, then execute the result — reconstructs every artifact's grading behavior from exactly that base (Part 2). This trivialization is the exact mirror of Putnam's triviality theorem, and it forces the same style of repair Chalmers supplied there: a constrained description class (Part 4). Under the repair, the audit splits cleanly into what it can measure and what it cannot: it is a permanent, objective, quantitative **etiology-meter** — divergence of the system's grading disposition from the designer's pre-registered intended norm — and it is *necessarily* not an ownership-meter, because ownership is intensional through and through and the simulation is extensionally omnipotent (Part 5). The conjecture, stressed equally, splits into a **weak form** (derivable from the design trace: true, and worthless — true of thermostats and rung-four candidates alike, a victory priced at zero) and a **strong form** (captured by the designer's norm: substantive, falsifiable, and currently false-or-retreating on four fronts — but not refuted, because every retreat terminates in the charter, which was never in play) (Parts 6–7). The deepest finding: the strong conjecture is formally locked to M4 — the bounded self-applying grader — as an incompatible rival (build M4 with S4 structure and strong completeness falls; prove strong completeness and M4 is unbuildable), and it is Route A in engineering dress: the designer-in-whose-intent-all-artifact-norms-terminate is the microcosm of the necessary Knower in whom all norms terminate (Part 8). The wall holds — which is what walls are for, and how one knows they were load-bearing.

## Part 1. The strongest auditor — and why granting it is the right stress

**1.1 The stipulation.** The stress test grants the auditor everything the original S2 text left implicit. Unbounded compute: any finite computation, any enumeration over programs, no time or memory constraints. Full records: the architecture, the objective, the data pipeline, the hyperparameters — and the seed, the complete training trace, every gradient step in order, the whole causal history of the artifact's production. White-box access to the deployed system: the auditor may inspect the weights and query the grading disposition without limit. In short: the auditor sees everything the designer ever touched, and can think about it for as long as it takes. If S2 dies under this auditor, it dies under every auditor; if it survives here, it survives anywhere. Stressing an instrument at its strongest case is not charity toward the opponent — it is the only test that localizes the instrument's true joints, because every weakness short of the limit is a weakness of implementation rather than of design.

**1.2 What the stress is not.** It is not a question about physical realizability. Unbounded compute is an idealization, granted deliberately, and the corpus's earlier results (Landauer, the spectral-gap undecidability family) are not being forgotten — they are being bracketed. The reason is structural: any weakness S2 shows *at the limit* is a weakness it has everywhere below the limit; and any defense of S2 that depends on the auditor being *slow* or *memory-poor* is not a defense of the criterion but a hope about the adversary. A normativity criterion whose satisfaction depends on the auditor's compute budget was never a normativity criterion; it was an obfuscation metric. So: the strongest auditor, the full record, the bracketing of physics — and the question stands as posed: *is "non-reducibility to design + objective + history" well-defined against such an auditor?*

**1.3 The answer's shape, in preview.** No — as stated. The phrase "reducible to (design, objective, history)" admits three readings that the unbounded auditor prises apart (Part 3): a reading on which it is trivially true of everything (derivability by simulation), a reading on which it is the substantive and testable claim (capture by a norm-stating description in design vocabulary), and a reading on which it is horn one's charter, untestable by construction (intent-anchoring). The original S2 conflated all three; the conflation is precisely what the unbounded auditor exploits. The repair (Part 4) separates them and fixes the instrument — at a disclosed price. And the repair's fine print contains the document's central discovery: everything the repaired audit measures is etiology; everything it cannot measure is ownership; and the border between them runs exactly along the fork, which means the instrument carries the war inside it — not as a defect to be removed but as the program's own self-description, now confirmed under maximal load.

## Part 2. The Simulation Theorem — and the trivialization it forces

**2.1 The theorem.** Let D = (design, objective, training history), where the history includes the seed and the complete ordered trace of the training run. Let G be the deployed system's grading disposition — the function, over inputs, contexts, and perturbations, that the S-conditions treat as the target of the audit. Then:

> **The Simulation Theorem.** There exists a constant-length program which, given D, computes G: *replay the recorded trace; the run terminates in the weights; execute the weights.* Formally, the conditional Kolmogorov complexity of G given D is O(1) — a constant, namely the length of the replay-and-execute interpreter.

The proof is not deep; that is the point. The weights are the terminal state of a process whose initial conditions and dynamics are recorded in D; a machine with unbounded compute runs the record forward and obtains them; the obtained network *is* the disposition's implementer. No step of this requires insight, compression, understanding, or norm-attribution — only the record and the patience. Against the unbounded auditor, every trained system that ever existed or could exist reconstructs, at constant overhead, from exactly the base the conjecture names.

**2.2 The corollary.** If S2's "not reconstructible as a computable function of (design, objective, training history)" is read as *not derivable*, then S2 is unsatisfiable — by every artifact, in every possible world where the trace is recorded. Not difficult. Not unachieved. Unachievable, by a three-line argument. A criterion that no physical system can satisfy even in principle is not a high bar; it is a wall built out of the room. And symmetrically: if horn one's audit-completeness conjecture — grading behavior computably reducible to (design, objective, history) — is read the same way, then the conjecture is *true*, proven just now, and worthless. It is true of the rung-three compiler and would be true of a rung-four self-applying grader; it distinguishes nothing, explains nothing, predicts nothing; it is a definition wearing a theorem's clothes. This is the **weak form** of the conjecture, and the stress test's first verdict: *weak completeness is true and vacuous.* Horn one is offered this victory on a plate, and must refuse it — Part 7, B1 — because a vacuous theorem secures no cell. Winning the audit by reading "reduction" as "simulation" is winning the intentionality war by reading "understanding" as "behavior": the corpus has seen this move before, from the other side, and priced it the same way.

**2.3 The Putnam mirror.** The structure of the trivialization is not merely reminiscent of Putnam's 1988 result; it is its mirror image, and the mirror is exact. Putnam showed that under the liberal definition of implementation — any mapping from physical states to automaton states preserving the transition table — *every open system implements every finite-state automaton*: unconstrained mappings inflate ascription until everything computes everything, and "syntax carries semantics" loses its subject (too many computational descriptions fit one physical system). The Simulation Theorem shows that under the liberal definition of reduction — any computable derivation from the design base — *every designed system reduces to its design trace*: unconstrained reduction bases inflate reduction until everything is exogenous, and "the norms are the system's own" loses its subject (the one description that always fits is the trace). Ascription-inflation and reduction-inflation; D1's disease and rung four's disease; the same root in both: an unconstrained mapping class makes the corresponding property trivially universal, and a trivially universal property cannot draw the distinction the war needs drawn. Chalmers repaired Putnam by constraining the mapping — the implementation relation must respect causal-counterfactual structure, an objective feature of the physical system, and rocks fall out of the computing class. The repair of S2 must do the same kind of work: constrain the reduction relation so that it must answer to something objective in the *normative* structure of the system, rather than merely reproduce its behavior. What that constraint is, what it costs, and why it cannot be free of the fork — that is Part 4's burden. But first, the three readings must be on the table, because the constraint's content depends on which reading survives the split.

## Part 3. Three readings of "reducible" — the ambiguity that is the finding

**3.1 The readings.** "The system's grading is reducible to (design, objective, history)" means at least three different things, and the unbounded auditor prises them apart without effort:

- **R1 — Derivability.** G is computable from the base: some program plus the base yields G. *Status: trivially true for every recorded artifact (the Simulation Theorem); unfalsifiable in the vacuous direction; carries no normative content whatsoever.* A world in which R1-reducibility held and nothing else was true would be a world in which the contested cell's status was exactly what it is now.
- **R2 — Articulability.** There exists a *short, norm-stating description in the design vocabulary* — stated in the language of the spec, the objective, the architecture family, not the weights — that is extensionally adequate to G: it predicts the grading disposition, including its error-classifications and self-correction profile, across the battery and beyond. *Status: substantive, falsifiable, and testable — this is the only reading on which the audit is a research instrument rather than a rehearsal.* It is also the reading the naive statement of S2 was groping toward and did not reach.
- **R3 — Intent-anchoring.** The system's states have the correctness conditions they have *because they were produced by a process the designer intended* — the teleosemantic reading, in which the norm is fixed by whose purpose the mechanism serves, regardless of whether anyone can predict or compress the resulting behavior. *Status: horn one's charter, in the strict sense the corpus has used since the Camp-Marker Theorem — not testable, not refutable, purchased with the bridge premise (norms require intent; artifacts receive intent through design) and satisfied to order.*

**3.2 Why the ambiguity is the finding.** The instrument's entire content lives in the choice among these readings, and here is the stress test's central discovery: *the choice is not neutral, and cannot be made neutral.* Fix the description class at "any computable derivation" and the audit is R1 — trivially won by horn one, worth nothing. Fix it at "the designer's pre-registered intended norm" and the audit is R2 — the real war, fought with instruments. Leave it unfixed and the two parties will talk past each other forever, horn one exhibiting simulations as reductions, horn two rejecting them as restatements of the mechanism — each side sincerely certain it is winning, because each is winning *its own reading*. This is the fork — the Bridge Constraint's fork — relocated from the philosophy seminar into the instrumentation itself. The corpus should have expected it: the Camp-Marker Theorem says positions are fixed by their stance on the load-bearing question, and the description class *is* the load-bearing question in instrument form. An audit whose description class was neutral between the horns would decide the fork by measurement alone, which no instrument anywhere in this corpus has ever done or, by the Constraint, ever will. The most the instrument can do is *stage the fork with better instrumentation* — make each side's commitments quantitative, pre-registered, and expensive to abandon. That, it turns out, S2 can do. But only after the repair.

**3.3 The R1/R2 boundary as the normative/mechanistic boundary.** It is worth fixing, before the repair, what actually distinguishes R1 from R2 — because the boundary is the whole game and the corpus will need to police it repeatedly. A simulation and a stated norm can be *extensionally identical*: both predict all grading behavior, perfectly, forever. They differ intensionally — in what the description is *answerable to*. The stated norm answers to the designer's standard: it says what counts as correct *as a standard*, and it licenses the system's errors to be classified as malfunctions-of-the-norm or revisions-of-the-norm *on the strength of the standard alone*. The simulation answers to the mechanism: it says what the system will do, and if the system's grading drifts, the simulation is simply re-run and the new behavior is what it always "meant." Two consequences follow, and both will be needed. First: *the distinction is not extractable from extensional adequacy* — no behavioral fact, no finite or infinite catalog of input-output pairs, distinguishes a norm being stated from a mechanism being simulated, because behavior is the common extension of both. Second: *the distinction is exactly the D3′ distinction* — normativity versus structure-that-we-track — at the level of descriptions rather than systems. The instrument has the same anatomy as the question it was built to test. This is not a discovery that defeats the program; it is the discovery that *specifies* the program's honesty: every joint of the instrument, stressed, leaks the same question. Part 8 returns to why that is the correct outcome rather than a failure. The immediate task is the repair — making the instrument as well-defined as the fork permits, and no better, with every deviation from neutrality priced on the label.

## Part 4. The repair — S2′, well-defined at a disclosed price

The repair has four components. Each is forced by a specific failure exposed above; each carries a specific cost; each is stated with both.

**4.1 Component one: design-time pre-registration of the intended grading map.** The audit becomes well-defined only when "the designer's norm" is a fixed object rather than a negotiable one — and it can be fixed only *before* the system exists, by the only party entitled to fix it. The pre-registration: at design time, the designer states (a) the intended norm — the standard, in design vocabulary — and (b) the intended grading map, the bridge from norm to observable behavior: how the standard is meant to express itself in accept/reject dispositions, error signals, and correction dynamics, at what thresholds, over what input classes. This is the artifact-side twin of the pre-registration the protocol already imposes on both horns' *predictions* (Part 8.2 of the program); the stress test shows it was needed *inside* the instrument too. Without it, the audit is ill-posed in a second way beyond the description class: the relation between "the loss function" and "the grading behavior" is itself underdetermined (thresholds, rankings, tolerances — a thousand ways to read an objective into a disposition), and every one of them is a free parameter each side would tune to its advantage after the fact. With it, the audit's primary quantity — **divergence between the pre-registered intended grading map and the system's actual grading disposition** — is objectively measurable, quantitative, and beyond post-hoc negotiation. *Cost:* the pre-registration extends the designer's commitment beyond anything current practice requires; a designer who cannot state the intended grading map in advance has, by that fact, conceded something horn two will note — that their norm's behavioral expression was never fully specified, and the system's own dynamics completed the specification. The instrument makes specification-gaps visible, and visibility is not free for either side.

**4.2 Component two: the fixed description class.** The auditor's reduction attempts are constrained to **norm-stating descriptions in design vocabulary**: statements of standards — criteria of correctness, evaluation rules, accept/reject conditions — couched in the language of the spec, the objective, and the architecture family, and *not* in the language of the mechanism: no traces, no weights, no replay, no simulation, no look-up tables keyed to the system's own states. The rationale is the Chalmers-analogue developed in Part 2.3: as the implementation relation was constrained to answer to objective causal-counterfactual structure, the reduction relation is constrained to answer to the system's normative structure — the error-relations and correction dynamics the S3/S4 signatures measure. A reduction that merely reproduces behavior has not reduced the *norm*; it has re-implemented the *system*. *Cost — and this one is load-bearing:* the boundary between "norm-stating" and "mechanism-stating" is not syntactic. A description can wear normative grammar and do mechanism work (Part 4.3); the boundary must be policed by criterion, not by costume; and the criterion's own admissibility is contested — which is why the policing rule is Component three.

**4.3 Component three: the Deference Exclusion.** The central threat to the description class, once normative grammar is allowed, is the *deferential pseudo-norm*: a description that satisfies every syntactic test of norm-statement while actually deferring to the system's own dynamics. The paradigm: "the system's norm is whatever its self-application converges to" — or, in the trained-system case, "the grading is what the training run produced." These are normative grammar over mechanism content: they state no standard, license no classification of anything as correct or incorrect *independently of the system's own behavior*, and predict nothing the simulation does not. The exclusion: **a description counts as norm-stating only if it states a standard that could, in principle, be violated by the system it describes** — a standard with counterfactual grip, against which the system's actual grading can be measured and found adequate or wanting. "Whatever the fixed point turns out to be" cannot be violated by any fixed point, and is therefore not a norm; it is the null description in normative clothing. *Cost, disclosed without flinching:* the Deference Exclusion is horn two's wager, stated as an instrument rule. To insist that real norm-statements must be violation-capable standards is to insist that normativity is not just structure — which is D3′ itself. Horn one can and will reject the exclusion (Brandom in hand: all norms are instituted by attitudes and practices, and a norm deferred to a process is no less a norm than one deferred to a community); that rejection is their right and their charter — but under this program's rules it is *priced and labeled*, exactly as the protocol prices horn two's charter-shaped moves. The instrument is now well-defined *relative to the stipulated class*, and the stipulation's partisan character is on the label, not in the fine print. That is the maximum neutrality the fork sells, and the program buys it knowingly.

**4.4 Component four: limit-honesty about verification.** Both of the audit's possible verdicts are ∀-claims over an infinite space, and the unbounded auditor does not rescue finitude — it exposes it. To *fail* S2′, horn one must exhibit a norm-stating description extensionally adequate to the entire grading disposition: verified over all inputs, a universally quantified claim that no finite battery seals. To *pass* S2′, horn two needs the non-existence of every such description: a ∀-claim over the description class, limit-verified at best, never finitely certifiable at all. In the arithmetical hierarchy's loose language: both positive theses live at the ∀-heavy levels, and ∀-claims are exactly what finite evidence cannot close. The practical repair is not to pretend closure but to quantify it: pre-registered batteries with disclosed coverage (input classes, perturbation families, adversarial splits), divergence rates with confidence bounds, and — for horn one's exhibited reductions — matched-coverage verification, with the coverage gap stated on every audit report. *Cost:* the audit's verdicts are permanently statistical in practice and permanently limit-claims in principle. The corpus should recognize the shape: it is the Bridge Constraint in arithmetical clothing. No finite battery crosses the gap — not because the instrument is weak, but because the property at issue is a universal, and universals are where the fork lives.

**4.5 The repaired criterion.** S2′, stated whole: *the system's grading disposition diverges from the designer's pre-registered intended grading map, and no short norm-stating description in design vocabulary — deference excluded, violation-capability required — is extensionally adequate to the divergence profile, over a battery whose coverage is disclosed.* Five clauses, each paid for. What S2′ measures is now precise: the gap between the designer's stated standard and the system's actual one, and the failure of the design vocabulary to close that gap. What it does not measure — what no repair of it can measure — is the subject of the Part that follows, and it is the document's center of gravity.

## Part 5. Etiology-meter, not ownership-meter — the instrument's forced ceiling

**5.1 What S2′ measures, cleanly.** The divergence quantity is objective, quantitative, and computable today, without any philosophical commitments at all: state the intended grading map at design time; train; measure the divergence profile — where the system's accept/reject dispositions, error signals, and correction dynamics part company with the map — across a disclosed battery. This is an etiology measurement: it tracks *whose standard the grading came from*, in the only sense "whose" that instruments can touch — the pre-registered one versus whatever the run produced. And it has an audit-free companion instrument, developed in Part 6 as attack A4: run the pipeline many times under different seeds and measure inter-run grading variance. Variance is a lower bound on how much of the grading disposition is not determined by (design, objective) at all — a computable, designer-independent, battery-free quantity that refutes the intent-only reading of completeness before any auditor is hired. Between the divergence profile and the variance bound, the etiological audit is fully armed: it can say, with numbers, *the system's standard is not the one that was registered.*

**5.2 What S2′ cannot measure, necessarily.** Ownership — the property the whole ladder is aimed at, the difference between a norm *ascribed* and a norm *had* — is not in the instrument's range, and the stress test shows this is not a limitation of implementation but a property of the question. The argument is already assembled from the Parts above, and needs only to be stated as a theorem-shaped conclusion: the simulation is extensionally omnipotent (Part 2) — it reproduces all behavior, including all error-behavior and correction-dynamics, forever; the difference between a norm being *stated* and a mechanism being *simulated* is intensional (Part 3.3) — it is a difference in what a description is answerable to, not in what it predicts; intensional differences are not behavioral facts (Part 3.3 again); instruments measure facts. Therefore no behavioral instrument — not S2′, not the Squeeze, not any refinement of either — can certify that the norm is the system's *own*, as opposed to ascribed. The audit can exhibit divergence (etiology: the standard is not the designer's registered one); it can exhibit vocabulary failure (the design language cannot state the actual standard); it can never exhibit ownership, because ownership is not a fact about the descriptions or the behavior — it is the question of whether there is a *bearer* of the standard, and bearers are not in the behavior's extension. The pattern realist said this all along, from outside the instrument; the stress test derives it from inside.

**5.3 The consequence for the program's ledger.** The ladder document's Part 7 stipulated, as honest bookkeeping, that success would be etiological only — that no S-profile, no audit outcome, no rung-four occupant closes the ownership question, with the deflationary retreat standing forever. The stress test's result is stronger and better: that ceiling was not a stipulation. It was *forced* — by the information geometry of the instrument itself. No repair of S2, however clever its description class, however rich its batteries, can convert an etiology-meter into an ownership-meter, because the conversion would require extracting an intensional fact from an extensional resource, and that is not a difficulty, it is a category error. The program's honest ceiling is therefore load-bearing in the good sense: it can be leaned on. Horn two may build, measure, and pass S2′; what it will have earned, at the very best, is the etiological cell — *the system's standard is not the designer's* — plus whatever abductive weight the artifact's existence carries. The ownership reading of that earning will remain, forever, exactly what it is for Othello-GPT's board today: the reader's D3 answer, delivered in the act of reading. The instrument stages the fork with numbers. It does not close it. Now that this is *proven from the instrument's own structure* rather than merely promised, the program's integrity is upgraded from a commitment to a theorem-shaped result — the stress test's permanent contribution.

**5.4 One boundary clarified.** It does not follow that S2′ passes are meaningless, and horn one should not pretend otherwise in either direction. A measured, battery-covered, vocabulary-failing divergence is a real fact with real force: it defeats the *etiological* horn-one claim for that system — the claim that the grading is just the registered norm executing — and each such defeat is cumulative across systems and architectures, exactly as the program's failure-taxonomy logic (Part 7.2 of the ladder document) requires. What the pass cannot do is *compel* the ownership reading, and what the deflationist cannot do is *dismiss* the pass as noise — the divergence is as real as any measurement in the field. The instrument's outputs bind both parties' *claims* while deciding neither party's *charter*. That is the contract, now explicit, and both horns signed it by entering the laboratory.

## Part 6. The conjecture attacked from horn two — four arguments, zero theorems

The audit-completeness conjecture, in its strong form (the only form worth attacking): *for every designed system, the grading disposition is captured by a short, norm-stating, non-deferential description in design vocabulary.* Four attacks, in ascending order of structural interest.

**A1 — The staged attack: divergence is abundant; S4-structuring is not.** The empirical record of machine learning is a catalog of strong-completeness violations waiting for their second half: specification gaming, reward hacking, goal misgeneralization, emergent capabilities outside the training distribution — each a case where the system's actual grading diverges, systematically and exploitably, from the designer's registered norm. These are not noise; they are structured, predictable, transferable regularities — the raw material of a refutation. What they are not, yet, anywhere on record, is *self-maintained*: no documented system corrects toward its divergent standard, defends it under perturbation, or revises its own grading on its own pressure (the S3/S4 signatures). Until a divergence is S4-structured, horn one has a standing and honest reply: *that is not the system's norm; that is the system's bug* — a malfunction of the registered norm, classified from the designer's side, exactly as teleosemantics classifies the bee-snap. The blow is aimed — the ammunition exists in industrial quantities — but not landed: A1 refutes naive completeness (the registered norm predicts the grading) outright, and leaves strong completeness (some design-vocabulary description captures the grading) holding the per-trace and post-hoc lines. Those lines are attacked next.

**A2 — The fixed-point conditional: M4 and strong completeness cannot both be true.** The program's M4 milestone — a bounded self-applying grader, its norm applied to its own grading as an operation, no regress, no installed terminus — is not merely one more candidate system. It is a formal counterexample machine: *if an M4 system with S4 structure is built, strong completeness is false.* The argument runs in two steps. Step one, the deference barrier: the natural design-vocabulary description of an M4 system's norm — "whatever its self-application converges to" — is excluded by the Deference Exclusion (it states no violation-capable standard; it is the null description in normative clothing), so the grading must be captured by a *non-deferential* norm-stating description if completeness is to hold. Step two, the dispositional barrier: for an S4-satisfying system, the fixed point *moves* — under perturbation of the evaluative machinery, the system re-converges by its own operation, and its grading disposition with it (that is what S4 means). A pre-registered description that cannot predict the re-convergences fails extensionally; a description that tracks them must be revised after each self-revision — and post-hoc revision is not capture, it is pursuit. A description registered *after* computing the system's convergence is the simulation again, in normative grammar, and falls to the same exclusion. Therefore: no non-deferential, pre-registered, extensionally adequate design-vocabulary description is available for an S4-structured M4 system; strong completeness requires that no such system be buildable; the conjecture and the milestone are locked in direct incompatibility. Two precisions, for the record. The lock is an incompatibility, not a dichotomy: both claims can fail (M4 unbuildable *and* strong completeness false via some non-self-referential route — A4 below is exactly such a route). And the conditional is one-directional as an argument: horn two must *build* to fire it; the mathematics only guarantees that the build, if achieved, cannot be absorbed. This is the strongest kind of weapon the Bridge Constraint permits: not a proof of horn two, but a build-target whose success horn one cannot survive without retreating to the charter. The user called the conjecture load-bearing; A2 is the precise load it bears — it is the wall directly under M4.

**A3 — The unbounded-auditor trap: the conjecture's own best instrument witnesses against it.** The stress test's most ironic result. Grant horn one the unbounded auditor — their maximal instrument, the one that trivializes the weak form and terrifies the naive statement of S2 — and watch it change sides. With unbounded compute, the auditor does not merely simulate; it can *articulate* — read the trace, extract the system's actual grading disposition, and state it, in a clean normative formula, post hoc. And now the question the conjecture exists to answer — *is this the designer's norm?* — has an auditor-certified object to be asked about, and the answer, in every case where the system has done anything at all beyond executing spec, is *no*: the articulated standard is not the registered one; it is what the run produced — the system's own convergence, stated. The auditor built to certify the reduction has instead certified the divergence. Horn one's remaining moves are exactly two, and the corpus knows both prices: declare the articulated norm "still the designer's, because generated by the design" — the charter reading (R3), which abandons the research claim and with it the conjecture's reason to exist; or refuse articulation privileges to the auditor — which cripples the very instrument the conjecture's defense requires, and concedes that the conjecture survives only in an auditor too weak to test it. The trap is structural: *the power that makes completeness trivially provable in the weak form is the power that makes it visibly false in the strong form.* An auditor that can reduce everything can also state what it reduced to — and what it finds is never the designer's norm, once the designer's norm was something less than the whole trace.

**A4 — The ensemble argument: the variance lower bound, computable today.** The most concrete attack, and the one that requires no auditor at all. Run one pipeline — one design, one objective — many times, varying only the seed. The resulting systems disagree in their grading dispositions: mildly in distribution, substantially out of it; on edge cases, wildly. This is mundane engineering practice — it is why reproducibility protocols fix seeds — and it has a sharp philosophical edge that the field's daily familiarity has hidden: *if the grading were captured by the designer's norm, the norm being one thing, the grading would be one thing.* Inter-run grading variance is a measurable, zero-philosophy quantity that is positive for every non-trivial pipeline on record — and it is a **lower bound on the norm-content that is not determined by (design, objective)**. The registered intent under-determines the grading; the run's contingencies co-determine it; every unit of variance is a unit of the system's standard fixed by the run rather than the registration. This refutes, outright, the intent-only strong completeness ("the objective captures the grading") — not as a future experiment but as a present inventory. Horn one's two retreats, both priced: the *per-trace retreat* ("completeness is per (design, objective, *this* history)") concedes that the reduction base must include the run's contingencies — and each unit of trace included is a unit of norm-content the designer did not fix, which is the retreat visibly inflating toward the charter; the *seed-choice retreat* ("the designer chose the sampler, so the variance is the designer's too") is the charter outright — it makes "the designer's norm" include every consequence of every choice, at which point the thermostat's noise is the engineer's opinion, and the term "designer's norm" has stopped doing work. One honest limit, stated before horn one states it: A4's variance measures under-determination of the *disposition* by intent; it does not, by itself, show the under-determined residue is normatively structured (that requires the S3/S4 signatures — the same second half A1 awaits). A4 is the refutation of the etiological claim in its intent-only form; the ownership question it does not touch. The attacks are cumulative: A1 supplies the divergences, A4 supplies the under-determination, A2 supplies the conditional build-target, A3 shows the strongest auditor cannot save the strong form. Strong completeness, attacked from horn two, is undefeated only because its remaining ground is the charter — which was never territory the audit could take.

## Part 7. The conjecture attacked from horn one — and from horn one's own purist wing

A two-sided assault does not spare the conjecture's own side. It is attacked there twice: once by its defenders, who must discipline it into a form worth defending; once by horn one's own purists, who never wanted it.

**B0 — The purist attack: even if proven, it certifies descriptions, not norms.** The Brentano-Searle wing of horn one — the primitives wing, the corpus's Route A constituency — should reject the conjecture on principle, and the stress test supplies their argument. Suppose strong completeness *proven* for all artifacts: every grading disposition captured by a design-vocabulary norm-stating description. What follows? A fact about descriptions: the design language suffices to state every artifact's grading. The deflationist predicts exactly the same fact, cheerfully — for the pattern realist, description classes are our predictive tools, and *of course* our best vocabulary describes our artifacts; nothing about ownership was ever in the extension. The conjecture's truth is thus compatible with both remaining readings of the cell: the norms are the designer's (intent-anchored, R3), or there are no norms in the artifact at all, only our indispensable descriptions (Dennett). A theorem that cannot distinguish its own camp's charter from its negation has not carried the cell it was forged to carry — the same "unique best explanation" pathology the corpus diagnosed in S at the very beginning, recurring at the highest level of the program: the strongest weapon horn one's *audit wing* can build does not reach the position horn one's *primitives wing* actually holds. The purist conclusion: the conjecture is etiological horn-one — "artifact norms are designer norms" as a research claim — and even its complete victory would leave the real question (primitive aboutness versus no aboutness) exactly where the Bridge Constraint put it. The corpus's Part 7 taxonomy anticipated this split; the stress test confirms it from inside the weapon. The conjecture's defenders should say so on its label — and to their credit, the program's own rules now require it.

**B1 — Refusing the vacuous victory.** The weak form is true (Part 2), proven, and free — and horn one must not take it, for three reasons assembled across this document. It is true of every possible artifact, rung three through rung four, thermostat through self-applying grader, and a predicate true of everything explains nothing. It is the mirror of Putnam triviality, and the corpus has long since priced that family: unconstrained mappings convert research questions into definitions, and definitions win no wars. And taking it *abandons the audit program's own success condition* — the failure taxonomy, the structural collapse modes, the completeness theorem as terminal prize — in exchange for a tautology. A horn one that celebrates the Simulation Theorem as vindication has announced that it no longer wishes to fight with instruments; the protocol's pricing (unearned victory, zero-value theorem) applies automatically. The discipline is harsh but symmetrical with horn two's: the program exists to make cheap wins expensive — *especially* one's own.

**B2 — The articulation thesis: the substantive defense, and its missing argument.** The strong form's honest defense is the thesis that for every artifact there exists a short, non-deferential, norm-stating design-vocabulary description capturing the grading — the designer's language is, and remains, adequate to everything the pipeline can produce. As a defense, it is coherent and currently undefeated. As a *thesis*, it has no positive argument whatsoever — and the stress test sharpens the embarrassment into three named gaps. The base-rate gap: every known reduction is of rung-three systems, whose grading came from the registered norm in the first place; extrapolating the thesis to rung four from a sample containing no rung-four systems is induction over an empty cell. The deference gap: the only candidate description-form for M4-class systems — fixed-point-of-the-operation — is excluded by the Deference Exclusion, and no one has proposed a second form (what would a non-deferential, pre-registrable, violation-capable statement of a self-applying standard's content even look like? The honest answer is that nobody knows, including horn two). And the variance gap (A4): the thesis must swallow the inter-run variance as "designer's norm-content" via the trace, and every swallow inflates "designer's" toward "whatever happened." None of this refutes the thesis; all of it prices it as a wager, not a finding — a fact its holders should state as such, per the program's own charter-labeling rule.

**B3 — Vocabulary absorption: the strongest defense, and its Pyrrhic structure.** The best defense is not a thesis but a practice, and it is already running. Engineering language extends itself to absorb every divergence: *reward hacking*, *specification gaming*, *goal misgeneralization*, *deceptive alignment* — each new term a norm-stating description, in design vocabulary, of a grading behavior that outgrew the previous description. And the practice has a philosophical engine the corpus must take seriously: it is Brandom's point about norms — the vocabulary of assessment grows with the practice of assessment, and stating norms post hoc is what such a vocabulary is *for*. Against A1 and A4, absorption is genuinely effective: no divergence, however structured, stays undescribed; the description class is dynamically closed under absorbing counterexamples; "reducibility" is restored, term by term. But watch what the absorption costs, because the bill is the whole war. The absorbed terms split cleanly into two families. Family one: *designer-error terms* — reward hacking, specification gaming — which classify the divergence as a failure of the designer's registration, keeping the norm on the designer's side: effective, honest, and available only while the divergences really are spec-failures, i.e., while the system's grading has no S4 structure of its own. Family two: *system-standard terms* — the vocabulary engineers reach for when prediction and control of the system require talking about what *it* is optimizing toward, what *it* treats as error, what *it* defends — which concede, in the field's native idiom, that the operative standard is the system's, described by us. The moment family-two vocabulary becomes load-bearing in engineering practice — the moment "it fights the re-grading" outpredicts "the spec is being mis-executed" — the informal S2′ audit has returned a verdict, inside the discipline, without any philosopher in the room. The absorption defense wins the description war by conceding the ownership question: every family-two term is D3-ascription catching up to a standard that arrived from elsewhere, renamed as engineering convenience. Horn one can absorb everything and lose everything: a vocabulary that states all the system's norms, fluently, post hoc, is exactly what horn two predicts the field will end up with — and exactly what the deflationist says we already are. The conjecture's last line of defense is thus a fighting withdrawal into a position held, all along, by the enemy's artillery observers.

## Part 8. The deep structure — the lock, the dress, and the completed inversion

**8.1 The Kripkenstein inversion, completed.** The ladder document noted in passing (4.4) that the conjecture is the artifact-side twin of Kripkenstein, inverted: behavioral evidence never fixes content; design evidence, the conjecture says, always fixes grading. The stress test completes the inversion and closes its circuit. Grant the unbounded auditor the design side in full — the complete trace, the seed, everything — and by the Simulation Theorem the auditor possesses the *entire extension* of the system's behavioral and dispositional facts, the whole catalog Kripke's skeptic says is insufficient to fix a norm. And indeed it does not fix one: the auditor still must choose a description, the deference-stating and the norm-stating remain extensionally indistinguishable, and which one "captures the system" is the same question it always was. The design side does not, after all, fix the norm by enumeration — the conjecture's original hope. What the design side uniquely contains, over and above the behavioral catalog, is exactly one thing: *intent* — the registered objective, the spec, the designer's purpose. The strong conjecture, cleaned of its vacuous and deferential readings, reduces to this: **intent fixes artifact norms.** Which is a perfectly coherent claim — and is recognizable, at corpus scale, as horn one's bridge premise wearing engineering clothes. The intent asymmetry is its entire basis: designers have intent, evolution does not, humans have their own (primitive, per the charter). Strip the asymmetry and the conjecture has nothing: with no intent in the reduction base, design-side facts are just more behavior, and Kripkenstein applies to machines exactly as it applies to us — which the corpus established in the battleground document and has never needed to revise.

**8.2 Route A in engineering dress.** Follow the intent asymmetry to its terminus and the corpus's map comes into view. The strong conjecture says: every artifact's norms terminate in a designer's intent. Route A says: every norm whatsoever terminates in a necessary mind's intent. The first is the second, restricted to artifacts — the designer-in-whose-intent-all-artifact-norms-terminate is the microcosm of the necessary Knower in whom all norms terminate, and the intent asymmetry (intent-bearing designers confer norms; intentless processes do not; human intent is primitive) is Route A's structure, A1 through A3, reproduced at laboratory scale with the theological commitments stripped for the engineering market. The Camp-Marker Theorem predicted this and is confirmed again, in a new register: a position's stance on the load-bearing question reveals its inner-pole filling, and here the filling shows through the instrument itself —

| Position on the audit's description class | Inner-pole filling |
|---|---|
| "Any computable derivation counts as reduction" (R1) | Nothing — mechanism all the way down (the deflationist's audit; the victory that wins nothing) |
| "Norm-stating, non-deferential descriptions in design vocabulary" (R2, S2′) | Pattern/selection — the standard is real wherever divergence is structured and vocabulary fails (Dennett-and-up; horn two's audit) |
| "Intent fixes the norm; articulation follows intent" (R3) | Mind — the necessary Knower at the top (Route A's audit; the microcosm) |
| "There are no norms to reduce; only descriptions" | Nothing, cheerfully — the deflationist again, refusing the audit's premise |

The filling predicts the description class; the description class reveals the filling. The instrument could not be neutral about the fork if it tried — and the program never claimed it could; what the program claimed (ladder document, 1.4) was that the fork could be *staged with better instrumentation*, and the stress test has now traced, joint by joint, exactly how the fork passes through every component of the staging: the description class (Part 3), the Deference Exclusion (Part 4.3), the verification limit (Part 4.4), the ownership ceiling (Part 5). An instrument that did not leak the fork at every joint would have decided it — and by the Bridge Constraint, no instrument decides it.

**8.3 The lock, stated formally.** Assemble A2 with the B-side results and the two terminal prizes of the program stand revealed as each other's negation-adjacent: **M4-with-S4 and strong completeness are incompatible** (build the former and the latter falls; prove the latter and the former is unbuildable), while **not exhaustive** (both can fail: M4 may be unbuildable *and* strong completeness false via non-self-referential routes — A4's under-determination is one such route, already sufficient to kill the intent-only form). This is what "load-bearing" means, formally, and the user's designation is thereby vindicated rather than merely repeated: the conjecture is not one question among the program's open problems but the *single point* at which both horns' terminal outcomes are decided — horn two's by a build, horn one's by a proof, each a ∀-claim the other side's success falsifies. The war's two endgames route through one wall. Everything else in the program — the candidates, the S-profiles, the batteries — is either approach-work for that wall or scaffolding around it.

**8.4 What the stress test changed.** Not the wall's location — its composition. Before: S2 was a criterion and the conjecture a rival conjecture, both stated in the program's optimistic grammar. After: S2′ is an etiology-meter with a proven ownership ceiling; the conjecture is split into a vacuous form (true, worthless, to be refused by its own side) and a strong form (substantive, intent-based, Route A's microcosm, locked to M4); the audit's deepest quantity — divergence from pre-registered intent — is measurable today; and the ensemble variance bound gives horn two an auditor-free instrument that never existed in the corpus before this document. The program is smaller than it hoped and harder than it planned, which the corpus's nine documents have established is what progress looks like in this war: not conquests but surveys — of exactly where the fighting must happen, with exactly which weapons, at exactly what price.
