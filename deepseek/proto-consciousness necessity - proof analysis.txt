# Can It Be Proven, From Reasonable Assumptions, That At Least Some Proto-Consciousness Is Necessary for Existence?

**Question under analysis (Q):** Is there a sound deductive argument, from assumptions a rational party to the mind–body dispute can accept without thereby settling the dispute, whose conclusion is that at least some proto-consciousness is metaphysically necessary for (concrete) existence?

**Answer, stated up front: No.** And the reason is precise, not rhetorical. Once the claim is disambiguated, it turns out to be co-extensive with panprotopsychism (or, through a different route, with a necessarily existent mind) — so any proof of it from "reasonable assumptions" would have to contain a premise that simply *is* one of those positions, at which point the proof is not a proof but a membership card. What follows establishes this in four steps, then reports what survives.

---

## 1. Making the claim precise

"Proto-consciousness is necessary for existence" admits two readings:

- **R1 (the "at least some" reading):** □(E → ∃x Proto(x)) — in every metaphysically possible world in which concrete entities exist, at least one entity is proto-experiential.
- **R2 (the strong reading):** □∀x(Con(x) → Proto(x)) — necessarily, every concrete entity is proto-experiential.

R2 is full panprotopsychism, stated modally. R1 *looks* like a modest weakening — "you don't need everything to be conscious, just something, somewhere, in any world where anything exists." The appearance is false, and the discovery of its falsity is the first substantive result.

## 2. Result 1: The Collapse Lemma — R1 secretly contains R2

Consider **W_min**: a possible world containing exactly one simple concrete entity — a single electron, or if particle ontology offends, a single homogeneous classical field, or the primordial quantum vacuum itself. Three observations:

1. **W_min is possible by every mainstream position's lights.** The identity physicalist, the functionalist, the emergentist, and the Chalmersian dualist all admit sparse worlds — they must, since sparse worlds are just the early universe minus the contingencies that (on their views) produced experiencers. S itself treated the primordial vacuum as the paradigm concrete existent in its cosmological section.
2. **In W_min, the only candidate bearer of proto-consciousness is the simple entity itself.** There is nothing else in the world.
3. **Therefore R1 requires the electron (or the field, or the vacuum) to be proto-experiential.**

So R1 → (for every possible minimal concrete world, its fundamental constituents are proto-experiential) → the fundamental constituents of matter are necessarily proto-experiential *as such* — which is R2 restricted to simples, and simple entities are what "fundamental constituents" means. **The "at least some" softening buys nothing.** Once the necessity operator forces proto-consciousness into every minimal world, it has forced it into the furniture of the world, not merely into some corner of it. Whoever asks for R1 has — whether they know it or not — asked for panprotopsychism.

This alone answers an important sub-question: R1 cannot be established by any route that merely guarantees *some* consciousness *somewhere* in every world — e.g., "the actual laws are necessary and lawfully evolve experiencers wherever there is matter." Necessitarian laws (Bird, Ellis) do not deliver experiencers in W_min: a lone electron does not evolve into a brain under any laws. The evolutionary/thermodynamic route secures at most "experience wherever the right complexity exists," and sparse worlds lack the complexity.

## 3. Result 2: The Bridge Constraint — no valid proof without a contested bridge

**Constraint.** Any *deductively valid* argument whose conclusion contains proto-experiential vocabulary must contain at least one premise that either (i) itself contains experiential or proto-experiential vocabulary, or (ii) provides a bridge — a definition, identity, or necessity link — connecting such vocabulary to the non-experiential vocabulary of the other premises.

This is not a deep theorem; it is what validity is: conclusions cannot introduce vocabulary that no premise introduced (introduction of a predicate requires that predicate somewhere in the derivation, including via definitions/identities). Its import is nonetheless large, because of what it implies about Q: **the entire content of any proof of R1 lives in its bridge premise.** The non-experiential premises — the structural facts of physics, the existence of concrete entities, the laws — can do no work toward R1 on their own. So the question "can R1 be proven from reasonable assumptions?" reduces exactly to: "is there a bridge premise that is reasonable in the neutral sense?"

**Taxonomy of candidate bridges, exhaustive as far as anyone knows:**

- **P\*a — Analytic/conceptual:** "to be concrete is to be (proto-)experiential." *Status: this is the thesis itself. Question-begging. Not reasonable in the neutral sense.*
- **P\*b — Empirical identity:** "experience = neural/functional process N" (identity theory). *Status: a serious, defensible position — but note what it delivers and what it does not. If pain = N-firing and identities are necessary (Kripke), then necessarily, where N-firing occurs, pain occurs. But worlds with concrete entities and no N-firing — W_min, the rock world, the early universe — are plainly possible and, on identity theory, contain no consciousness of any kind. **Identity physicalism is fully compatible with ¬R1.** This is worth savoring: the strongest physicalist bridge, even if granted, does not give the panprotopsychist one atom of R1.*
- **P\*c — Necessitarian/lawful:** "the laws are metaphysically necessary and entail experiencers wherever concreta exist." *Status: fails on W_min as shown in Result 1; and the Humean contingency of laws is itself a reasonable assumption.*
- **P\*d — Russellian:** "concreteness requires non-structural intrinsic nature; intrinsic natures are proto-experiential." *Status: the conjunction is precisely the position to be established. To accept it is to have decided the dispute. Not neutral.*
- **P\*e — Idealist:** "all concreteness is constitutively mental/representational." *Status: same as P\*d — a live camp's charter, not common ground.*
- **P\*f — Theistic:** "a necessarily existent conscious being exists; therefore every possible world contains experience." *Status: this is the one bridge that secures R1 **without** securing R2-for-simples — the rocks of W_min-with-God are not themselves experiential, but the world is not experience-free. Classical theism (God as necessary being) accepts P\*f with a straight face. It is thus a genuine loophole for R1 — and it demonstrates the same structural point from a new angle: it secures R1 from a premise that is a *camp charter*, reasonable within theism, fatal to neutrality. See Result 3.*

**Interim conclusion:** every sufficient bridge (P\*a, P\*d, P\*e, P\*f) is question-begging in the neutral sense; every neutral-plausible bridge (P\*b, P\*c) is insufficient.

## 4. Result 3: The Camp-Marker Theorem — R1 is a membership card, not a consequence

Survey the live positions and their commitments on R1:

| Position | W_min possible & consciousness-free? | R1? |
|---|---|---|
| Identity physicalism | Yes (no N-firing in W_min) | **No** |
| Functionalism / emergentism | Yes (no functional organization / emergence base) | **No** |
| Eliminativism | Yes (no consciousness anywhere at all) | **No** |
| Chalmersian property dualism | Yes (zombie worlds conceivable → possible; sparse zombie worlds a fortiori) | **No** |
| Ontic structural realism | Yes (structure all the way down; no natures to be experiential) | **No** |
| Russellian panprotopsychism | No — the simple entity is proto-experiential | **Yes** (via P\*d) |
| Idealism | No — concreteness is constitutively mental | **Yes** (via P\*e) |
| Classical theism | No — the necessary mind is present | **Yes** (via P\*f) |

The pattern is exact: **the set of positions affirming R1 is precisely the set of positions that build R1 into their bridge premises.** No position *derives* R1 from materials neutral between itself and its rivals; each affirms it because each assumed it, in the specific form of P\*d/e/f. Meanwhile the neutral-assumption intersection of the first five rows — structural physics, the datum of actual experience, sparse-world possibility, the contingency of the actual distribution of minds — entails nothing about R1 in either direction. 

Therefore: **there is no set of assumptions that is simultaneously (i) acceptable across the dispute's live camps and (ii) sufficient to entail R1.** "Reasonable assumptions," if it means anything disciplined, must mean (i); and under (i), R1 is not provable. If we drop (i), then yes, R1 is provable — in three lines, from P\*d — but then so is every other camp's charter, including the *negation* of R1 from the zombie-possibility premise. A standard under which everything and its contradiction is provable is not a standard of proof.

**The Gödel lesson.** The same structure appears in modal metaphysics' most famous construction: Gödel's (and Pruss's) ontological arguments *do* validly derive God's existence — from axioms (possibility of the maximally great, essence-entailment) whose plausibility is exactly co-extensive with prior commitment to the conclusion. The derivation is valid and proves nothing to the unconvinced. A proof of R1 from P\*d is the ontological argument's exact epistemic twin. The lesson generalizes: **in modal metaphysics, valid derivations transmit conviction; they do not create it.** The conviction lives in the bridge, and the bridges are the war.

## 5. What can be proven — the honest positive residue

The negative answer should not obscure three things that *are* establishable:

1. **The Bridge Constraint itself** (Result 2): a genuine constraint on any future proof — it tells every would-be prover exactly where their argument's entire content must sit, and lets the rest of us inspect that one premise rather than the scaffolding around it. This is the precise, non-rhetorical content of S's opening admission ("you cannot formally derive a term that never appears in the premises") — an admission S then violated and the audits then convicted.
2. **The Collapse Lemma** (Result 1): genuinely informative, and genuinely bad news for the modest-sounding version of the claim — it converts "at least some" into "the fundamental constituents as such," which is to say it converts the question into panprotopsychism outright.
3. **The conditional abductive case** — the repaired argument (companion document): IF concreta need non-structural intrinsic natures, and IF acquaintance samples such natures, and IF kind-parsimony is the right metric, and IF natures are uniform at the base — THEN panprotopsychism is the least speculative posit, tied with neutral monism, and hostage to the combination problem. That is the maximum the position supports today, and it is an argument *for actuality under conditions*, never for necessity.

And one asymmetry worth stating: **the negation is in no better shape.** ¬R1 also cannot be proven from neutral assumptions, because ¬R1's own bridge — zombie-world possibility, or bare-structure concreteness — is just as contested as P\*d (Kripkeans deny the first; Russellians deny the second). The dispute is symmetric in its unprovability. What the analysis establishes is not that R1 is false but that it is *not the kind of claim that proof from common ground could ever settle*. It is decided, if at all, by the abductive weighings of research programs over decades — by progress or degeneration on combination, structural realism, and the science of consciousness.

## 6. What would change the answer

For completeness, the concrete developments that would reopen Q:

1. **A theorem in the foundations of physics** showing that concrete (as opposed to merely mathematical) structure requires self-interpretation in a sense that formal self-reference does not satisfy. Nothing of the kind exists: quines, fixed points, and Gödel sentences show that formal self-reference is cheap and non-experiential; S's Constraint 3 claimed otherwise and was wrong.
2. **An empirical reduction of experience to structure** (the illusionist endgame) — which would not refute R1's modal claim but would evaporate the motivation for every bridge to it, P\*d included.
3. **A solution to the combination problem** with distinctive empirical content — which would not prove R1 but would transform P\*d from a camp charter into a progressive research program's risky, testable core, which is the only thing that has ever moved a metaphysical question off deadlock.

## 7. Bottom line

**No — it cannot be proven from reasonable assumptions, if "reasonable" means "acceptable without already settling the dispute."** The proof would need a bridge; every sufficient bridge is the conclusion wearing different clothes (P\*a/d/e), or a divine necessary mind that theists affirm and physicalists reject (P\*f); every insufficiently-neutral bridge (identity theory's P\*b) leaves R1 exactly where it was, because sparse worlds without the relevant processes are possible on all sides. Moreover, the "at least some" softening is an illusion: by the Collapse Lemma it *is* the full panprotopsychist thesis in modest dress. And symmetrically, the denial is unprovable too. The question, made precise, is the mind–body problem itself in modal costume — and no one has ever proven their way through that from premises the other side already accepted. The honest deliverables are the Bridge Constraint (inspect the bridge, always and only the bridge), the Collapse Lemma (beware soft-sounding weakenings), and the conditional program (the repaired argument), which promises plausibility under conditions and delivers nothing modal to anyone.
