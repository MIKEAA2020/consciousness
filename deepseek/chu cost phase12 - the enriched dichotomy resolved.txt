## Part 0. The obligation, and the level error

Doc 70 closed the alphabet by refuting the unit-inclusive implication at its plain form (Theorem Z: the value-gap fragments — full, replete, tensor-closed, unit-containing, plainly internally adjointed, not residuum-closed) and filed the Con-enriched form as the corrected-corrected residual, with the dichotomy as its shape. The filing's implicit picture: the enrichment is a stronger hypothesis, so the enriched question is a harder theorem, and the dichotomy asks whether the extra strength closes the gap. The picture contains an error of level, and the error is this document's door. Con-enrichment, in the corpus's own definition (doc 38, Definition 40: "homs are convex sets, composition and the functor actions are affine in each variable"), is a condition on the hom-SETS — and a hom-set with at most one element is a convex set in exactly one way, with every operation trivially affine. The plain form's counterexamples failed the Con-clause because their homs were large finite sets (doc 70: "whose homs are function sets... satisfy the plain form and not the enriched one"); but Theorem Z's fragment contains a sub-fragment whose homs are all subsingletons, and on that sub-fragment the Con-clause costs nothing. The question the dichotomy asked — can convexity close the fence? — was therefore asked one level too high to have the answer its first limb hoped for: convexity cannot close what it cannot see, and on a preorder it sees nothing.

## Part 1. Lemma 74 — preorders are Con-enriched for free

**Standing conventions.** Con is doc 38's category: convex sets ( subsets of real vector spaces closed under binary convex combinations) and affine maps, monoidal under the pointwise structure on function spaces. The corpus's usage already requires the empty convex set: ChuCost homs can be empty (capped, hom((1,1,0), (1,1,K)) = ∅: the condition K ≤ 0 has no solution), and Definition 55(b) quantifies over homs that may be empty; the lemma's enrichment uses ∅ and the singleton as they stand. A PREORDER here means a category whose every hom-set has at most one element.

**Lemma 74 (preorders are Con-enriched for free) [Derived here].** Every preorder C carries a Con-enrichment, unique on underlying data: each hom C(X, Y) is the convex set it is as a set (empty or a point); composition, identities, and every functor's action are affine; and every natural transformation between Con-functors on preorders is Con-natural. *Proof.* A convex structure on a set with at most one element is unique if it exists, and exists: the empty set is a convex set vacuously, and a singleton carries exactly one binary operation λx + (1−λ)x = x, satisfying idempotence, commutativity, associativity, and parametric continuity trivially (every map from [0,1] to a subsingleton is constant, hence continuous; and the degenerate segment axiom holds since all points coincide). Affinity: a function f between convex sets is affine when f(λx + (1−λ)x′) = λf(x) + (1−λ)f(x′); if the domain is a subsingleton then x = x′ and both sides are f(x); if the codomain is a subsingleton then both sides are its unique point; every function between subsingletons is therefore affine. Composition C(Y,Z) × C(X,Y) → C(X,Z) is a function between (products of) subsingletons — a product of subsingletons is a subsingleton — hence affine in each variable, and the enrichment's bifunctoriality (composition's bilinearity) holds. Functor actions: a functor between preorders acts by functions between subsingletons, affine as above. Naturality: a naturality square of post-composition maps commutes because between two fixed subsingletons there is at most one function when the domain is nonempty (and when the domain is empty the square commutes vacuously; the remaining case — domain a point, codomain empty — cannot arise for post-composition by an existing morphism, since composing that morphism with the domain's element would exhibit a codomain element). Uniqueness: the convex structures were forced. ∎

The lemma's reading is the document's thesis in miniature, one level down from Lemma 72's: an S-valued scale of tests cannot distinguish a pairing from its quantization; a convex structure on a hom cannot distinguish an adjunction from its preorder collapse. Enrichment's content begins exactly where subsingletonhood ends — at the second morphism.

## Part 2. Theorem AA — the singleton value-gap preorders

**Standing setup.** Additive home: ChuCost over V = ([0, ∞], +, 0) (doc 24's Definition 1, doc 27's Theorem A). Capped home: ChuCost^[0,K]_⊕ over [0, K] with the capped tensor (doc 66's Proposition 67), K ∈ (0, ∞) fixed. Value sets: S = {0} ∪ [a, ∞) additively (a > 0; whether ∞ ∈ S is immaterial — the verification covers both), S = {0} ∪ [a, K] capped, with a > K/2. Write ⊕ for the composition of values (c + e additively; min(c + e, K) capped), ∸ for the residuum difference (b ∸ c := max(b − c, 0) — the hom's pairing arithmetic, Lemma 69: the capped and additive homs agree on the same carriers), and h_S for the S-quantization (doc 70's Lemma 72: h_S(x) = 0 if x = 0, a if 0 < x ≤ a, x if x > a).

**Theorem AA (the Con-enriched unit-inclusive implication, refuted in the additive and capped homes) [Derived here; Lemma 72 is doc 70's, Lemma 74 this document's].** Let Q′ be the full subcategory of the home on the objects (1, 1, ≡c), c ∈ S. Then Q′ is full, replete, tensor-closed, contains the monoidal unit (and, capped, the dualizer), is internally adjointed in the Con-enriched sense (Definition 55(b) at full strength), and is not residuum-closed. *Proof.* **(a) Full** by definition. **(b) A preorder:** a morphism (1,1,c) → (1,1,d) is a pair of functions between one-point carriers — the unique pair (id, id) — and the (L)-condition reads d ≤ c; so each hom is a point (d ≤ c) or empty, and Q′ is a preorder, Con-enriched by Lemma 74. **(c) Replete:** an isomorphism (f, g) with lax inverse forces the pairings equal pointwise (each (L)-inequality against the other), and carrier bijections force singleton carriers; so (1,1,d) ≅ (1,1,c) iff d = c, and Q′ contains its iso-closures. **(d) Tensor-closed:** the tensor of (1,1,c) and (1,1,e) has carriers (1 × 1, 1¹ × 1¹) = (1, 1) and pairing c ⊕ e; if either value is 0 the composed value is the other, in S; if both are ≥ a then additively c + e ≥ 2a ≥ a and capped min(c + e, K) = K (c + e ≥ 2a > K) — in S either way. The gap (0, a) is never entered: ⊕ is inflationary on S × S. **(e) The distinguished objects:** I = (1, 1, 0) ∈ Q′ (0 ∈ S); capped, D = (1, 1, K) ∈ Q′. **(f) Not residuum-closed:** the internal hom of singletons is the singleton at the residuum value (Lemma 69: the hom's carriers are (1¹ × 1¹, 1 × 1) = singletons, the pairing b ∸ c). Additively (1,1,1.5a) ⊸ (1,1,2a) = (1,1,0.5a) with 0.5a ∈ (0, a) ∉ S; capped (1,1,a) ⊸_⊖ (1,1,K) = (1,1,K−a) with a > K/2 giving K − a ∈ (0, a) ∉ S. **(g) Internally adjointed, Con-enriched.** For A = (1,1,c) define R_A on objects by R_A((1,1,b)) := (1,1, h_S(b ∸ c)) — a Q′-object, since h_S takes values in S — and on morphisms by the unique maps (R_A is a functor because b′ ≤ b implies h_S(b′ ∸ c) ≤ h_S(b ∸ c), the order-functoriality of x ↦ h_S(x ∸ c), which is Lemma 72(iii) read at one carrier: monotone composed with monotone). The adjunction's bijections are the Galois equivalences: for all b, c, e ∈ S,

  (†)  [ b ≤ c ⊕ e ]  ⟺  [ h_S(b ∸ c) ≤ e ],

since Q′((1,1,c⊕e), (1,1,b)) is nonempty iff b ≤ c ⊕ e and Q′((1,1,e), R_A((1,1,b))) is nonempty iff h_S(b ∸ c) ≤ e. The verification of (†), four cases. (1) c = 0: c ⊕ e = e (additively e; capped min(e, K) = e), and b ∸ 0 = b, so (†) reads [b ≤ e] ⟺ [h_S(b) ≤ e]: at b = 0 both sides are true (0 ≤ e, h_S(0) = 0 ≤ e); at b ≥ a, h_S(b) = b and the two sides are the same inequality. (2) c ≥ a and b ≤ c: the left side always holds (b ≤ c ≤ c ⊕ e: additively c + e ≥ c; capped b ≤ c ≤ min(c + e, K) since b ≤ c and b ≤ K); the right side always holds (b ∸ c = 0, h_S(0) = 0 ≤ e). (3) c ≥ a and b > c and b − c ≥ a: the left side is [b ≤ c + e] (capped: b ≤ K always, so b ≤ min(c+e, K) ⟺ b ≤ c + e), i.e. [e ≥ b − c]; the right side is [b − c ≤ e] since h_S(b − c) = b − c ∈ S — the same condition. (4) c ≥ a and 0 < b − c < a — the gap: the left side is [e ≥ b − c]; evaluated within S this is [e ≥ a], because e = 0 fails (b − c > 0) and every e ≥ a satisfies e ≥ a > b − c; the right side is [a ≤ e] since h_S(b − c) = a — the same condition. (Capped rider: b ≤ K and c ≥ a give b − c ≤ K − a < a, so case (3) never arises capped and case (4) is the whole b > c regime — the cap makes the gap the ONLY path.) So (†) holds; the hom-bijections Q′(A ⊗ C, B) ≅ Q′(C, R_A(B)) hold for all B, C; they are natural in B and C because all squares between subsingletons commute (Lemma 74); they are affine because all functions between subsingletons are; and the unit η_C: C → R_A(A ⊗ C) and counit ε_B: A ⊗ R_A(B) → B exist because the bijection applied to id_{A⊗C} and id_{R_A(B)} cannot map a nonempty hom to an empty one. By the standard criterion, A ⊗ − ⊣ R_A on Q′, in the Con-enriched sense. ∎

**The witness, read aloud (capped).** The preorder contains a = (1,1,≡a) and the top D = (1,1,≡K). The ambient computes their hom as K − a — the full budget minus the gap point, a value outside S, an object outside the fragment. The fragment computes it as a itself: R_a(D) = (1,1,≡a), the quantization lifting the gap's contents to the gap's edge. Both answers cut the preorder's tests identically — (†) at b = K, c = a reads [K ≤ min(a + e, K)] ⟺ [a ≤ e], and within S these are the same condition (e ≥ K − a forces e ∈ [a, K]) — and they differ. The corrected-corrected question asked whether convexity forces the fragment's function space to agree with the ambient's; the answer is that the question can be asked entirely inside a preorder, where convexity has no vote.

**Corollary AA.1 (the dichotomy resolved; the fence relocated to the dimensions) [Derived here; Theorem F and Lemma 41 are doc 38's, Theorem K doc 42's].** (i) The Con-enriched unit-inclusive implication is false in the additive and capped homes (Theorem AA), so doc 70's dichotomy resolves to its second limb: the enrichment was never the point — the coupling's invisibility is an arithmetic phenomenon (the gap's interval, Theorem Z) that operates at the level of the order of values, below the level where the Con-clause could interfere. (ii) What protects the corpus's applications is a DIFFERENT and stronger hypothesis than the enrichment clause: Theorem K's Con-EQUIVALENCE to a fiber, with affine actions — and an affine equivalence fixes hom-dimensions (an affine bijection carries affine hulls onto affine hulls), so a category Con-equivalent to the fiber Q has Q′(X, Y) affinely bijective to the spectrahedron Q(A_X, A_Y), of affine dimension d_{A_X}²(d_{A_Y}² − 1) (doc 38's Lemma 41) — positive whenever the target algebra is nontrivial (d ≥ 2), and a singleton otherwise (Q(A, ℂ) is the trace, one map; Q(ℂ, B) is the state space, a singleton only at B trivial). A preorder's homs have affine dimension 0; hence no preorder is Con-equivalent to a fiber containing any nontrivial target; a fortiori Theorem AA's fragment satisfies Definition 55(b) while failing Theorem K's hypothesis (i) — the enrichment clause is strictly weaker than the fiber-equivalence, by exactly the dimensional content. (iii) The corrected-corrected-corrected residual: the unit-inclusive implication for full replete tensor-closed fragments Con-equivalent to a NONTRIVIAL fiber — the fence Proposition 61 built (objects outgrowing their probes), relocated from "convex homs" to "fiber-dimensional homs" — remains open, and is now the only open form of the question (the plain form refuted by Theorem Z; the Con-enriched form by Theorem AA; the MV forms below). ∎

## Part 3. Proposition 75 — the MV home resists the preorder route

**Proposition 75 (the MV home resists the preorder route) [Derived here; Proposition 73 is doc 70's].** (i) No bottom-gap value set S = {0} ∪ [a, K] with a < K has its singleton preorder ⊙-closed: if a > K/2 then a ⊙ a = 2a − K ∈ (0, a), and if a ≤ K/2 then a ⊙ (K − a/2) = a/2 ∈ (0, a) — outside S both times; the Boolean exception a = K ({0, K}) is ⊙-closed and ∸_MV-closed, hence residuum-closed and no counterexample (Proposition 73(i), restated at one carrier). (ii) The top-gap value sets S = [0, b] ∪ {K}, b < K/2, have ⊙-closed singleton preorders (values ≤ b compose to 0; c ⊙ K = c; K ⊙ K = K) containing the unit (1, 1, K), and these preorders are NOT internally adjointed: at A = (1,1,c) with c ≤ b and B = (1,1,K), the required bijection fails at C = (1,1,K) for every choice of R_A(B) — the left side is empty (A ⊗_MV C = (1, 1, c ⊙ K) = (1, 1, c), and hom((1,1,c), (1,1,K)) is nonempty iff K ≤ c, false), while the right side is nonempty for every R = (1,1,ρ) (hom((1,1,K), (1,1,ρ)) is nonempty iff ρ ≤ K, always true). This generalizes Lemma 66's scalar corner — Proposition 73(iii) located the quantization's failure at the single pair (⊥, I); the preorder reading shows the adjunction itself fails at every (c ≤ b, K), with or without quantization. The MV unit-inclusive status therefore stays open with both routes closed: the quantization route by Proposition 73(iii), the preorder route by this proposition. *Proof.* (i) and (ii)'s arithmetic are the displayed computations; the failure in (ii) is the emptiness contrast just verified. ∎

**The ledger's shape, and what survives.** Doc 70's conditional — "any proof of the enriched form must spend the enrichment — the convex structure must do work that no set-level argument can do" — is discharged by refutation: there is no proof of the enriched form, and the conditional's antecedent is false (the corpus's convention for conditionals whose consequent priced a proof-strategy that cannot exist). No mathematical content of doc 70 is retracted: Theorem Z, Corollary Z.1, and Proposition 73 stand exactly as proved; Theorem AA's fragment is a sub-fragment of Theorem Z's (every singleton is finite-carrier and S-valued), and what is new is not the fragment but the observation that the sub-fragment suffices and is Con-enriched — the level error of Part 0, corrected. What survives of the first limb's MOTIVE is relocated, not lost: the fibers' world — the world of channels and spectrahedra, where every application lives — is still closed for a reason, and the reason is now named: not the convexity of its homs (Theorem AA: convexity alone sees nothing a preorder cannot fake) but their dimensions (Corollary AA.1(ii): the affine-dimension invariant that Theorem F's intercept priced). The retroactive explanation doc 70 hoped a proof would give — why the applications were never threatened by the plain failure — is delivered by the refutation instead: the applications were never threatened because their fragments are fiber-equivalent, and fiber-equivalence carries the dimensional teeth that the enrichment clause lacks.

## Part 5. Coda

Doc 70 closed the alphabet with a gap priced exactly — an interval of values where a tensor cannot go and a difference arithmetic can — and a question held open in two hands: convexity load-bearing, or convexity beside the point. This document answers by dropping one level: the question was asked on the homs, and the counterexample lives on the order. A preorder cannot be told apart from its enrichment — that is Lemma 74's whole content — and the value-gap arithmetic needs nothing a preorder has thrown away: inflation composes, difference lands in the gap, quantization rounds it away, and the Galois connection (†) is Lemma 72's threshold equivalence with the sets collapsed to truth values. Convexity was never given a chance to close the fence, because the fence's counterexample never stood on convex ground. What the fibers' world has — and the preorder fakes — is not convexity but size: dimensions that an affine equivalence must carry, dimensions Theorem F priced into a diophantine intercept two phases ago, dimensions that are the reason the applications were safe all along. The alphabet reopened at AA finds the corpus's oldest bridge-lesson waiting one level down: the construction's failures are never where the file posted them — doc 24 posted closure where the candidate was wrong, doc 66 posted the idempotents where the route was pattern-bound, doc 70 posted convexity where the arithmetic was order-level — and each relocation has bought a sharper question than the one it retired. The sharpened question now on file: the fiber-equivalent unit-inclusive implication, the fence in its dimensional form. The base poset, under it all along, keeps its silence; and the budget — which opened this bridge as a currency for what information costs — spends its newest coin on the cheapest lesson it has ever minted: before asking what a structure forbids, ask whether the counterexample can even see it.
