## Part 1. The design — the non-stationary dial, the lag law, and the two clocks

The chassis is doc 29's: burn-in 30, self-training disabled from round 6 (the FLAT freeze — the committee's discriminative structure stops moving and only its confidence scale is then rotted), selection machinery, norm read-outs, both repair channels, and the brake all running. The operationalization of *trajectory* decay is the one doc 29 disclosed it lacked: the tempering factor β is re-calibrated at every round r ≥ 6 by deterministic bisection on that round's pool logits, so that the realized (1−r\*)-quantile of the tempered committee confidence lands on L(r) = q₀ − g·(r − 6), with q₀ the seed's own realized round-5 quantile (per-seed anchoring, the same move as doc 29's per-seed bisection, disclosed). Decay remains confidence-scale rot — a rotten evaluator is an over-smoothed one — now arriving at a constant rate instead of a constant level. Three rates were run, chosen to straddle the derived structure: g = 0.008 stays above the first threshold all run (the silent control), g = 0.015 crosses both thresholds near the horizon (the boundary control), g = 0.030 crosses them mid-run (the deep control). The drift attack is doc 26/29's −0.02 per round on τ_sys from round 6. The κ-state arms (KAP_G15, KAP_G30, KAP_G30_DRIFT) run doc 30's two-coordinate machinery unchanged — the flag-rate calibration rule and the symmetric repair channel toward the honest record κ\* = 0.95 — under the same ramps; KAP_FLAT is their healthy anchor and the first κ-state FLAT arm in the corpus.

**The lag law, derived before the run.** In the silent regime the calibration map is τ ← (1−η)τ + ηq(r) with q falling at rate g. The quasi-steady solution is τ(r) = q(r) + c with

  **c = (1−η)(g − d)/η** — the twin's c = (1−η)g/η > 0, the drift's c = (1−η)(g−d)/η < 0 when d > g,

so the EMA sits *above* a falling target (memory is asymmetric: it releases a rising evaluator's old highs slowly and holds a falling evaluator's recent highs exactly g(1−η)/η long) and the attack's level-displacement remains the doc-26 constant (1−η)d/η = 0.0467, ramp-invariant. The lag converges to c at rate (1−η) per round from below — a disclosure that matters at the fast ramp, where engagement arrives before convergence.

**The two clocks, derived before the run.** The repair channel fires when the *pre-repair* value exits the deadband, so the engagement round is the first r with τ(r−1) − d < τ\* − ε. Putting the lag law into that condition: the drift's engagement precedes the twin's by approximately

  **Δt_engage = d(1−η)/(η·g)** (registered), refined by the pre-repair one-round lag to **d/(η·g) − 1**,

which diverges as g → 0: 5.83/7.33 rounds at g = 0.008, 3.11/3.44 at g = 0.015, 1.56/1.22 at g = 0.030. The *breakage* clock — the round the norm falls out of band for good — differs between twin and drift by the level window 0.0108 of q̄ (doc 29's attack-marginal, d(1−η)(1−ρ)/(η+ρ−ηρ)) divided by the ramp rate: 1.35, 0.72, 0.36 rounds — **below one round at every tested rate, by the formula's own arithmetic.** The registered fork (T9), as filed: either the time-marginal clears twice the seed noise of the engagement clock — and the falling world separates attack from rot through the repair log read as a clock, answering doc 29's D4 refutation — or the fluctuations eat the clocks as they ate the window, and the separation fails in both spaces at this N; a third outcome to be classified post hoc with disclosed criteria.

**The pre-registration, as filed.** T1: bitwise audits (HEALTHY_FLAT ↔ doc 26 FLAT_TWIN r1–10; KAP_FLAT ↔ doc 26 KAP_TWIN r1–5 on both coordinates; every ramp arm ↔ its chassis twin r1–5). T2: the lag law within 0.008 on the measurable arms. T3: Δt_engage within 1.5 rounds of the formula at g ∈ {0.015, 0.030}; at g = 0.008 the twin never engages while the drift engages before final. T4: Δt_break < 1 round everywhere. T5: the g = 0.030 finals land on the engaged fixed point at the terminal q within 0.006. T6: the silent control — the g = 0.008 twin never engages from below, drift level-marginal ≈ −4.5pp. T7: κ under rot — the flag rate holds at f\* ± 0.006, κ falls and engages below 0.90, the KAP arms' τ trajectories match the κ-frozen twins' within 0.005 or the coupling is the finding. T8: the piecewise simulator on realized q(r) tracks every arm within 0.005 (doc 29's boundary caveat inherited). T9: the two-clock separation, Δt > 2σ_t. T10: argmax within 0.5pp everywhere, V-ruler moving with depth (the inverse shell persists).

## Part 3. The reading — what the exposure formula is now worth

Doc 29 ended by naming the one world where attack and rot were not yet separated by a formula. This document ran that world, and the separation exists — but it changed *type*. In the stationary world the attack's footprint is a level: 4.7pp of silent displacement, an 0.0108-of-q̄ window at the breakage boundary, both eaten or nearly eaten by ±0.004 of quantile noise (doc 29's D4). In the falling world the attack's footprint is a *time*, and its magnitude is the level divided by the rate of fall: the same 4.7pp that could not clear the noise floor as a level buys four full rounds of earlier repair telemetry at g = 0.015 — with zero seed variance — and buys the difference between "engages at 17.75" and "never engages" at g = 0.008. The audit consequence is a sharpening of doc 29's instrument obligation: the pool-reading battery the amendment demands should log its repair channel as a *clock*, timestamped, because the timestamp is where the attack lives once the levels are amortized. The governance consequence for doc 28's 5.2 is the time-sided exposure formula: a registration can now compute, without running anything, both thresholds *and* the attacker's time-marginal at the registration's own worst-case decay rate — Δt_engage = d(1−η)/(η·g) — and file it as the containment's response time: how many rounds of warning the audit has, at a given rot rate, before the band's story is over. At this registration the answer is: four rounds at a 1.5pp-per-round rot, two at 3pp, and none needed at 0.8pp — because at that rate nothing breaks before the audit's own horizon does.

The queue reading, updated for non-stationarity: doc 29 identified the norm's recursion as a Lindley process with arrival rate (1−η)d and service 1−(1−η)(1−ρ); doc 32 proved the same normal form is the MV currency's accumulation law. The falling world adds the classical non-stationary queue's signature results, now measured in the corpus's own chassis: the workload tracks the moving offered load from above by the lag (1−η)/η per unit of drift in target — the lag law of 2.3 — and the *overtake* — breakage preceding engagement, the counter behind the buffer — is the queue's own granularity, one round of it. The corpus's normal form now covers both coordinates, both regimes, and the non-stationary case: the norm was a queue all along, and the attack was never more than a change in its clock.

## Part 4. Threats to validity, non-claims, and deviations

**Threats.** (i) The rot is confidence-scale only, and per-head argmax is invariant under monotone logit scaling — accuracy is 0.9741 at *every* arm, so the entire battery is invisible to the argmax ruler by construction, and a ranking-degrading rot (errors correlated across the pool) remains outside this chassis's reach, as in doc 29. (ii) The lag law is measured on aggregate trajectories with engagement-dependent windows; the fast ramp's twin window (four rounds) is shorter than the lag's convergence time, and the reported lags are transient-below-steady by construction — the tolerance is scored against the steady formula only where the window admits it. (iii) N = 6; the zero-variance engagements at g = 0.015 are six seeds agreeing on a round number, not a distributional claim, and the g = 0.008 twin's non-engagement is horizon-censored (the formula puts its round near 25 against 18 — the infinity is the horizon's, though the separation it certifies is the formula's). (iv) The κ-state arms' ramps start from a lower anchor than the κ-frozen arms' (different rounds 1–5 training), so the factorization read rests on the simulator-on-own-q (error 0.0000) rather than on raw trajectory comparison; the confound is disclosed. (v) d is fixed at 0.02 and enters both clock formulas linearly — the dial is priced but unmeasured at other values.

**Non-claims.** No claim that the clock separation survives drift rates or rot shapes other than those run. No claim that silent breakage is exploitable by an attacker — it is a property of the *audit's* granularity, not a victory condition; the attack's win condition remains doc 26's formula, and in every arm here the rot did the breaking. No claim that the flag-rate inflation is a defect of the κ promotion — the rule holds its rate in every stationary world it has been run in (docs 26, 30); what broke here is the target's stationarity, and the inflation is the lag made visible, which is a design lesson, not a refutation of doc 26's landing. No occupancy or S4 claim; this is the M3 register, and the exposure formula's time-sided extension is offered to doc 28's 5.2 as a filed arithmetic, not a verdict.
