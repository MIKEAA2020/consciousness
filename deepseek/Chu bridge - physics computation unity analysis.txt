# The Chu Bridge: Can Physics and Computation Be Proven One Self-Dual Reality?

**Question under analysis (Q):** Can it be proven that (i) Physics and Information/Computation are not separate disciplines but the outer and inner views of a single, self-dual mathematical reality (the "Chu bridge"), and (ii) this unified view provides the *only* structural language capable of doing justice to both objective mathematical certainty and subjective conscious experience?

**Answer, stated up front: No — but the reason is more interesting than in any previous case.** The question entangles five distinct theses: one is theorem-grade mathematics, one is a live and partially successful research program, one is coherent-but-undecided metaphysics facing genuine counter-theorems, one is refutable as stated, and one is bridge-premise-dependent in the sense the Bridge Constraint fixes. Separating them is the whole game. The Chu construction itself is real, deep, and genuinely embodies "outer and inner views of one thing" — and precisely none of that transfers to experience without a premise no theorem supplies. What *is* new here, and worth taking seriously, is that this is the first bridge candidate in the entire exchange with theorem-grade scaffolding standing on *both banks* of the gap.

---

## Part 1. Disambiguation — five theses hiding in one question

- **T1 (Formal-unity thesis).** Physics and computation are deeply formally intertwined: information-theoretic axioms reconstruct quantum theory; computation is physically constrained; information is thermodynamic. *A research program with published theorems and experiments behind it.*
- **T2 (Identity thesis).** Physical reality *is* computational/informational structure — Wheeler's "It from Bit," Zuse's Rechnender Raum, 't Hooft's automaton interpretation, Lloyd's universe-as-quantum-computer. *Ontology, not methodology.*
- **T3 (The Chu articulation).** The right formal shape of this unity is the Chu construction / linear-logic duality: physics as the *extensional/outer* pole, computation as the *intensional/inner* pole, glued by an evaluation pairing — a self-dual category where the two views are one object.
- **X-claim (Exclusivity).** This is the *only* structural language doing justice to both mathematical certainty and subjective experience.
- **C-claim (Phenomenal adequacy).** The unified view does justice to subjective conscious experience at all.

The question "can it be proven?" has a different answer for each. In order: partially (T1), no — undecided (T2), no — a schema, not a theorem (T3), no — refuted as stated (X), no — bridge-dependent (C).

## Part 2. What is actually theorem-grade

Honesty requires assembling the real results first, because the question's seductiveness rests on them — and so does its refutation.

**2.1 The Chu construction and self-duality (real).** Given a symmetric monoidal category C and a dualizing object K, Chu(C, K) has objects (A, X, r) — two sets glued by an evaluation r: A ⊗ X → K. Barr showed Chu(C, K) is *-autonomous (1979, generalizing Chu's 1978 double-dualization construction on topological vector spaces). The signature theorem for our purposes:

> **Chu(Set, K) ≅ Chu(Set, K)^op** — the transpose (A, X, r) ↦ (X, A, r^T) is an involutive, arrow-reversing equivalence.

This is the precise mathematical content of "outer and inner views of a single reality": an object *is* the pair of its two views, and the category is literally equal to its own opposite. No interpretation needed; that is the theorem. Pratt pressed Chu spaces toward physics ("rational mechanics," complementarity and uncertainty in Chu-space dress); game semantics (Abramsky-Jagadeesan-Malacaria; Hyland-Ong) realized proofs and refutations as Player/Opponent interaction — the two "views" became theorem-grade in proof theory, with full-completeness results tying proofs to their dialogues.

**2.2 The pattern generalizes (real).** The Chu construction is one instance of a family of dualities of the form *dual-pair-glued-by-evaluation*:
- **Isbell duality:** a space and its algebra of functions, glued by evaluation — geometry (outer) and algebra (inner) as conjugate views of one object.
- **Gelfand duality:** commutative C\*-algebras ≅ locally compact Hausdorff spaces; and its noncommutative generalization is *literally the algebraic formalism of quantum theory*: observables form an algebra, states are its linear functionals, and the expectation ⟨state, observable⟩ is the evaluation pairing. Heisenberg's picture (algebra-first: inner) and Schrödinger's (state-first: outer) are the two transposes of one Chu-like object. This is not a metaphor; it is why the algebraic and Hilbert-space formulations of QM are equivalent.

**2.3 Physics–computation connections (real, with the status of each carefully labeled).**
- **Landauer's principle** (erasure costs kT ln 2): theorem-grade thermodynamics–information link, experimentally confirmed (Bérut et al., 2012), with the Szilard-engine/Bennett line resolving Maxwell's demon information-theoretically.
- **Reconstructions of quantum theory** (Hardy 2001; Chiribella–D'Ariano–Perinotti 2011 "informational derivation," with purification as the key axiom): quantum theory *derivable* from information-theoretic axioms. Theorems — from axioms whose own status (deep constraints vs. convenient summaries) is the open interpretive question.
- **The Church–Turing–Deutsch principle** (every finitely realizable system is perfectly simulable by finite means): Deutsch's 1985 framing made computability a *physical* principle — falsifiable, and spectacularly vindicated in its quantum form by the rise of quantum computation. But note its direction: it says physics is *simulable*, not that physics *is* computation. The gap between those two is where T2 lives.
- **Constructor theory** (Deutsch–Marletto): physics recast as possible-vs-impossible transformations, with interoperable information as a primitive. A program, not yet a theorem.

**2.4 What T3 legitimately claims.** As a *schema*, the Chu articulation is genuinely illuminating: computation-as-interaction (game semantics, geometry of interaction) makes the system/environment duality constitutive of the computational process itself; physics' observable-state pairing instantiates the dual-pair-plus-evaluation shape. If one wanted a single formal picture in which "physics" and "computation" are two views of one thing, Chu-type self-duality is the canonical current candidate. That is a real and defensible claim. It is not a theorem that reality * instantiates* the schema — it is a choice of language whose fruitfulness is being tested.

## Part 3. The counter-theorems — what mathematics proves *against* the strong identity

The identity thesis (T2) is not merely unproven; specific theorems strain it, and any proof would have to accommodate them:

1. **Spectral-gap undecidability** (Cubitt–Perez-García–Wolf, *Nature* 2015): for 2D quantum lattice systems in the thermodynamic limit, whether a Hamiltonian is gapped is *undecidable* — no algorithm answers it for the general case. A perfectly well-posed physical question with no computable answer. Physics outruns computation at idealization limits.
2. **Pour-El–Richards** (1979): the wave equation with computable initial data can have solutions that are non-computable at specified spacetime points. Computationally specifiable physics, non-computable physical output.
3. **The open physical-reals question.** Physical theories traffic in real-valued quantities; Turing-computable reals are measure-zero among reals. Whether *physical* reals are all computable reals is undecided — and T2 lives or dies on it. (The BSS real-machine model and computable analysis (Weihrauch) formalize the two sides.)

**The finite-nature defense and its cost.** The identity theorist can answer: undecidability and Pour-El–Richards phenomena live in infinite idealizations (thermodynamic limits, exact reals); finite-energy finite-volume regions carry finite entropy (Bekenstein/holographic bounds), finite state spaces are trivially computable, so *bounded* physics is computational. This is coherent — but it is itself an unproven finitism about nature (quantum gravity's verdict on the continuum is outstanding), i.e., another premise, not a proof. **Verdict on T2: coherent, fertile, undecided; the theorems cut against the limiting version, not the bounded one; nothing decides it in either direction today.**

## Part 4. The exclusivity claim — refuted by the exchange's own recurring diagnosis

The X-claim ("the *only* structural language capable of...") fails in both tenses.

**Present tense:** live rivals already do substantial parts of the job:
- **Topos-internal logic** (Isham–Döring): each physical context/observer gets a topos whose *internal language* expresses that observer's propositions — literally a structural treatment of the inner/outer distinction in physics, by other means.
- **Homotopy type theory / proof-relevance:** arguably does *more* justice to mathematical certainty than Chu-style set-level duality — proofs become first-class mathematical objects (univalent foundations); not self-dual in the Chu sense, but a rival for "the structural language of objectivity."
- **Dagger-compact categories and the ZX-calculus** (Abramsky–Coecke; Coecke–Duncan): the actually-working graphical language of quantum structure — cousins of the Chu story, not instances of it, and by any engineering measure currently *more* successful at quantum reasoning.
- **Sheaf-theoretic contextuality** (Abramsky–Brandenburger): local sections vs. global obstruction — a precise structural account of why quantum facts are perspective-relative. The objective/relative structure of QM, done with sheaves.
- **IIT's cause-effect structures** (Oizumi–Albantakis–Tononi): the most developed attempt to formalize the *inner* view as intrinsic information structure — rivaling the Chu story precisely on the phenomenal side (see Part 5).

**Future tense:** exclusivity quantifies over all possible formalisms — including ones not yet invented. A uniqueness claim over an open-ended space cannot be proven (it can only be falsified, which Part 4's present-tense list already does). This is the same pathology the audits diagnosed in S's "unique best explanation" (Z B6): a curated menu with the winner pre-installed. **The X-claim must be dropped, exactly as S's uniqueness claims had to be.** What survives is the modest version: Chu-type self-duality is the *canonical current* framework whose defining feature is the outer/inner symmetry itself.

## Part 5. The phenomenal claim — the Bridge Constraint returns

**The "inner pole" of the Chu duality is intensional, not phenomenal.** States, expectations, strategies, refutations — these are structural notions. Nothing in the self-duality theorem, in linear logic, in full completeness, or in any reconstruction of QM introduces *felt* vocabulary. By the Bridge Constraint (established in the necessity analysis): no valid derivation can introduce phenomenal vocabulary without a bridge premise; therefore any argument from the unified formalism to experience requires an explicit identification — *experience = the inner pole* — and that premise is a camp charter (the panprotopsychist/idealist bridge in category-theoretic dress), not a theorem. The formalism relocates the hard problem; it does not dissolve it. "Why is the inner pole *felt*?" stands exactly where "why does this process hurt?" stood.

**The fixed-point observation — where S's Constraint 3 finally finds its true home.** The original document's self-interpretation argument (information needs an interpreter; only experience self-interprets) was refuted in the audit by formal counterexamples: quines, Gödel sentences, fixed points, the Y combinator — self-reference without phenomenality. In the Chu picture this becomes precise: the formal objects that "view themselves" are the self-dual objects and fixed points of the involution — and they are formally *cheap*. Self-duality is an equilibrium of the formalism, not an awakening. The most interesting way to state the finding: **the gap between self-reference and self-experience is invariant across every formalism in which self-reference can be defined.** The Chu construction, the best formalism we have for the two-views picture, makes the gap *clearest* — and does nothing to close it.

**What would count as progress on the phenomenal side:** a psychophysical law implemented as the evaluation pairing. Chalmers's structural-coherence conjecture (phenomenal structure mirrors information structure) would be exactly such an implementation: the duality's pairing becomes the correlation. That is a conjecture about where to build the bridge, not a theorem that one exists — and IIT, its most developed rival formalization of the inner view, is itself contested (it makes claims the unfolding-argument literature disputes). The honest statement: the Chu framework is the best currently available *schema* for a future psychophysical law, because it has the right shape (dual poles + pairing), and it is no closer to crossing the gap than any other formalism.

## Part 6. The grand synthesis — the slot, and its two strongest candidates

Every document in this exchange has circled one formal structure: a **duality with an inner pole**. Structure/nature (Russell), third-person/first-person (epistemic symmetry), syntax/semantics (interpretation), algebra/geometry (Isbell), state/observable (Gelfand), system/environment (Chu), proof/refutation (linear logic). The dualities are theorems or solid formal facts — the *pole-fillings* are the war:

| Camp | Filling of the inner pole | Bridge premise |
|---|---|---|
| Russellian panprotopsychism | experiential intrinsic nature | acquaintance samples natures |
| Theism / idealism | necessary Mind | aboutness is essentially mental |
| Digital physics (this question) | computation / self-dual process | aboutness is information-theoretic |
| OSR | nothing — the pole is projection | intrinsic natures are a hangover |

The user's two questions in this round are the two strongest candidate fillings — **theistic mind** and **self-dual computation** — and they are *rivals for the same slot*: if aboutness is essentially mental (Route A of the steelman), the inner pole cannot be merely computational, and the Chu bridge's phenomenal claim fails *while its mathematics stands*; if aboutness is information-theoretic, the conceptualist route to the necessary mind fails at its beam, and the inner pole is open for the computational filling — which then inherits all the phenomenal debts undischarged. The two documents of this round are, formally, the decisive battle of the exchange: one slot, two fillings, no theorem to arbitrate.

**And the deepest observation the exchange affords:** mathematics can prove the *existence of the slot* (self-duality, evaluation pairings, the necessity of an intensional pole in any adequate two-view formalism) but is silent — provably silent, by the Bridge Constraint — on the *filling*. That silence is the mind–body problem's precise formal residue.

## Part 7. Layered verdict

1. **T1 (formal unity of physics and computation): the strongest "yes" available.** Information-theoretic reconstructions of QM, Landauer's confirmed bound, the CTD principle vindicated in its quantum form — a genuine, deep, growing unification. Not a single theorem "physics = computation," but a converging body of theorem-grade evidence that the two disciplines are formally inseparable at the core.
2. **T2 (ontological identity): no — undecided, and the counter-theorems are real.** Spectral-gap undecidability, Pour-El–Richards non-computability, and the open physical-reals question mean the identity is a coherent metaphysics under active mathematical pressure, not a provable fact. Its defense requires an auxiliary finitism about nature that is itself unproven.
3. **T3 (the Chu articulation): no — a schema, honestly the best one.** The self-duality theorem is real; the observable-state pairing genuinely instantiates the dual-pair shape; computation-as-interaction genuinely lives in the system/environment duality. But "reality is a Chu space" is a language choice, not a derivation; the identification of *physics with the outer pole and computation with the inner pole* is an interpretive posit with real traction and zero theorem-status.
4. **X-claim (exclusivity): no — refuted as stated, drop the "only."** Rivals (topos-internal logic, HoTT, dagger-compact/ZX, sheaf contextuality, IIT) already deliver substantial parts of the job; uniqueness over future formalisms is unprovable in principle. The same uniqueness pathology as S's "unique best explanation."
5. **C-claim (phenomenal adequacy): no — bridge-premise-dependent.** The inner pole is intensional, not phenomenal; self-duality does not self-awaken (the fixed points are formally cheap — S's Constraint 3, given its true resting place); the hard problem relocates to the inner pole's felt character and stands there, undissolved. The Chu framework is the best available *schema* for a future psychophysical law (dual poles + evaluation pairing is the right shape), and it is a conjecture, not a crossing.

**The final formulation, answering the question as asked:** It cannot be proven that physics and computation are outer and inner views of one self-dual reality — what can be proven is that *if* reality has a two-view structure, self-dual categories of the Chu type are its canonical formal articulation, that quantum theory already instantiates the shape in its observable-state pairing, and that the physics–computation entanglement is theorem-deep and growing. It cannot be proven that this is the only adequate structural language — the claim is false as stated today and unverifiable tomorrow. And it cannot be proven that the unified view does justice to subjective experience — what is true is subtler and better: the Chu bridge is the first bridge candidate in this entire exchange that arrives at the gap with theorem-grade scaffolding on *both* banks, and can therefore state exactly where the crossing is missing. The missing span has a name — the phenomenality of the inner pole — and it is the same span that was missing in every other bridge: the one no theorem has ever crossed, because (the exchange's last theorem, and its first) theorems do not introduce what their premises do not contain.
