## Part 3. The reading — the mooting, in both halves of the corpus

Three lessons. **A clause for a world that cannot occur is not a clause; it is a scope statement.** The overfill clause's premise — an armed, attacked, awardable world with a runaway ground — decomposes under measurement into mutually exclusive requirements: the runaway needs the record's in-band cooperation (slow), the attack needs the reading out of band (fast), and the award needs the threat (the anchor's degradation) that brings the fast phenomenon with it. The unattacked runaway holds its ruler and reads NO-THREAT. The mooting is structural, not statistical — no seed draw could occupy the cell, only a redesign of the walk's gate (a record that re-registers out of band, which no dial in doc 40's inventory does) — and the filing honors that by refusing to legislate for it: (v) stands, BURNED-only, and the overfill reading keeps the home doc 47 gave it, the maintenance world's honest positive. The corpus's formal half met this shape first: Q.2's detection clause, mooted because the premise fragments contradict themselves before the question is posed. The two mootings rhyme across the corpus's halves — one proved by arithmetic on dimensions, one measured by telemetry on grounds — and together they say the same thing about instrument design: **before writing a clause, price whether its world exists.** **The attack protects the record it came to break.** The battery's quietest irony: the REREG dial is a maintenance failure, and the attack disables it — not by repairing anything, but by holding the reading out of the band the walk needs. The threat model's grammar (attack versus maintenance) has been the corpus's working distinction since doc 30's coda named the stream as the unperturbed thing; this battery finds the two interacting worlds coupling through the band gate, the attack's noise shielding the record from the drift's slow corruption. A registrar reading the armed REREG world's telemetry sees one walk, twenty-two armed rounds, a drained ground, and a held ruler — a world that looks *better maintained* than its unattacked twin (which walks seven to eleven times and overfills), because the alarm that never stops ringing keeps the schedule honest. The instrument-side lesson generalizes: **false positives and maintenance failures are not independent axes — a continuously-armed channel is a channel whose record cannot walk.** **The bracket, like the bar, is horizon-relative — and the corpus's letters keep learning their own clocks at the horizons they were never run at.** RR_U10's refusal on a −0.0005 late slope is the third letter to fail only at double horizon (doc 48's write-side clock, doc 53's bar, now the bracket's monotone clause), and the pattern is now a filed expectation: every absolute letter the corpus registered at fourteen rounds carries an implicit horizon, and the first battery to double the horizon finds it.
