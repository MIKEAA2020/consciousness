## Part 0. The obligation, and the third way

Doc 70's dichotomy stated the enriched question's two possible endings: either the enrichment closes the fence (and convexity is load-bearing), or an enriched counterexample exists (and the enrichment was never the point). Doc 74 resolved the dichotomy to its second limb — Con-enrichment is vacuous on preorders, the singleton value-gap preorder refutes the enriched form — and relocated the fence to the dimensions: what protects the corpus's applications is Theorem K's Con-EQUIVALENCE to a fiber, which fixes hom-dimensions (Lemma 41), and the unit-inclusive implication for fiber-equivalent fragments became the only open form. The relocation carried an implicit picture of the ending: either the dimensions close the fence (a theorem: fiber-equivalent + tensor-closed + unit + (b) ⟹ residuum-closed), or a fiber-equivalent counterexample exists (and Theorem K, which forbids fiber-equivalent + tensor-closed + residuum-closed, would force it to satisfy (b) without (c) — the fence's sharpest possible instantiation). This document finds the third ending, which the dichotomy's grammar did not reserve a limb for: **the hypotheses are jointly inconsistent.** No fiber-equivalent tensor-closed unit-containing fragment is internally adjointed at all. The implication is true because there is nothing to falsify it — and the emptiness is not a technicality but the theorem: the same intercept that doc 38 priced against the carriage and doc 46 transported against closure is extracted here directly from the fragment's own adjunction, by evaluating it where every classical category theory evaluates it first — at the unit — and where the fiber's arithmetic is most exposed — at the diagonal.

## Part 1. Lemma 76 — the diagonal has no solutions

**Standing notation (docs 38/42/46).** Q is the fiber: objects the matrix algebras M_d (d ≥ 1), homs the trace-preserving completely positive maps, Con-enriched, with affine hom-dimension dim Q(M_a, M_b) = a²(b² − 1) (doc 38, Lemma 41 — the trace-preservation intercept). The intercept equation at a pair (d_E, d_B) is the diophantine equation in d_G:

  (IE)  d_G² = d_E²d_B² − (d_E² − 1) = d_E²(d_B² − 1) + 1.

It is Theorem F's equation (at A = ℂ: Q(T_E ℂ, B) ≅ Q(ℂ, GB) forces it), and it will be Theorem BB's equation (at C = I, B = A). Doc 38 established: solutions require the factorization (m − d_G)(m + d_G) = d_E² − 1 with m = d_E d_B, both factors positive of equal parity, hence d_E d_B + d_G ≤ d_E² − 1; the exceptional family (4, 2, 7), (6, 3, 17), … consists of the exact solutions.

**Lemma 76 (the diagonal lemma) [Derived here].** (i) For every d ≥ 2, the integer d⁴ − d² + 1 is not a perfect square: it lies strictly between (d² − 1)² = d⁴ − 2d² + 1 and (d²)² = d⁴. (ii) Every solution of (IE) with d_E ≥ 2 has d_B ≤ d_E − 2/d_E < d_E; in particular no solution has d_B ≥ d_E, and the diagonal instances (d_E, d_B) = (d, d) at d ≥ 2 are all unsolvable. (iii) At d_B = 1 the equation reads d_G² = 1 and is solved by d_G = 1 at every d_E. *Proof.* (i) d⁴ − d² + 1 − (d⁴ − 2d² + 1) = d² > 0 and d⁴ − (d⁴ − d² + 1) = d² − 1 > 0 for d ≥ 2; the interval between consecutive squares (d² − 1)² and (d²)² contains no square. (ii) From (IE), (d_E d_B)² − d_G² = d_E² − 1, i.e. (m − d_G)(m + d_G) = d_E² − 1 with m = d_E d_B; both factors are positive integers (d_G < m because the right side is positive), so m + d_G ≤ d_E² − 1, and since d_G ≥ 1, d_E d_B ≤ d_E² − 2, i.e. d_B ≤ d_E − 2/d_E < d_E. (iii) d_G² = d_E²(1 − 1) + 1 = 1. ∎

The reading that matters for what follows: the exceptional family, being a family of pair-solutions, lives strictly BELOW the diagonal (d_B < d_E) — its thinness was measured in doc 38, but its position was not read: every solution crouches under the diagonal, and the adjunctions that need the equation solved will be shown to need it solved ON the diagonal, where nothing solves it. The intercept's geometry: the environment pays one dimension per input unit, and the would-be adjoint must reproduce that payment against the environment's OWN dimension — a bill the arithmetic cannot coin.

## Part 2. Theorem BB — the hypothesis class is empty

**Standing setup.** ChuCost is docs 24/27's category: objects (A, X, r) with r: A × X → [0, ∞], morphisms (f, g) with the lax condition (L), the Dialectica tensor with the unit I = (1, 1, 0), and the residuum internal hom A ⊸ B of doc 27's Definition 15. A fragment is a full replete subcategory Q′ ⊆ ChuCost. "Con-equivalent to a fiber" is doc 74's hypothesis: there is a functor E: Q′ → Q, fully faithful and essentially surjective onto a subcategory of Q containing at least one nontrivial algebra (some M_d, d ≥ 2), with every hom-map Q′(X, Y) → Q(EX, EY) affine — a Con-equivalence onto its image. Write δ(X) for the dimension of EX. By the affine full-faithfulness and Lemma 41:

  (†)  dim aff Q′(X, Y) = δ(X)²(δ(Y)² − 1)  for all X, Y ∈ Q′,

every Q′-hom being nonempty (the fiber hom Q(EX, EY) contains the trace-then-prepare channel, and the affine bijection forbids emptiness).

**Theorem BB (the fiber-equivalent unit-inclusive implication, vacuously true; the hypothesis class empty) [Derived here; Lemma 41 doc 38's; Lemma 76 this document's].** Let Q′ ⊆ ChuCost be a full, replete, tensor-closed subcategory containing the monoidal unit, and suppose Q′ is Con-equivalent to a fiber with a nontrivial algebra in the equivalence's image. Then Q′ does not satisfy Definition 55(b): some A ∈ Q′ has A ⊗ − without a Con-enriched right adjoint on Q′. Consequently the unit-inclusive implication for fiber-equivalent fragments — (b) plus the standing hypotheses implying residuum-closure — holds vacuously: there is no fiber-equivalent unit-containing tensor-closed fragment that is internally adjointed, hence none whose residuum-closure could be asked. *Proof.* Since Q′ is full, Q′(X, I) = ChuCost(X, I), and a morphism X → I is exactly a choice of point of X's backward carrier X_X (f: A_X → 1 unique, g: 1 → X_X, the condition 0 ≤ r(a, g(∗)) always true) — so Q′(X, I) ≅ X_X as a set for every X ∈ Q′. By (†) at Y = I, once δ(I) is known, this hom is an affine image of the fiber hom at (EX, E I). First, δ(I) = 1: Q′(I, I) is a single point (the unique pair of identity maps on one-point carriers), so 0 = dim Q′(I, I) = δ(I)²(δ(I)² − 1), forcing δ(I) = 1 (dimensions are positive integers). Then for every X: dim Q′(X, I) = δ(X)²(1 − 1) = 0, and Q′(X, I) is nonempty — so the backward carrier X_X is a single point (Corollary BB.1 records this separately). Now let A ∈ Q′ with d := δ(A) ≥ 2, which exists by nontriviality of the image. Suppose, for contradiction, that A ⊗ − has a Con-enriched right adjoint R_A on Q′: affine bijections Q′(A ⊗ C, B) ≅ Q′(C, R_A(B)), natural in B and C. Evaluate at C = I and B = A. Since A ⊗ I ≅ A in ChuCost (the unit isomorphism, a Q′-isomorphism by fullness, with both objects in Q′ by tensor-closure), the left side has dim Q′(A ⊗ I, A) = dim Q′(A, A) = δ(A)²(δ(A)² − 1) = d²(d² − 1). The right side has dim Q′(I, R_A(A)) = δ(I)²(δ(R_A(A))² − 1) = δ(R_A(A))² − 1. The bijection is affine by hypothesis (the Con-enriched adjunction), and an affine bijection between nonempty convex sets carries affine hulls onto affine hulls, preserving affine dimension. Hence δ(R_A(A))² = d²(d² − 1) + 1 = d⁴ − d² + 1, which by Lemma 76(i) is not a perfect square — but δ(R_A(A)) is a positive integer, and its square is a perfect square. Contradiction. So R_A does not exist, and (b) fails at A. ∎

**The witness, read aloud.** Take the fragment's own nontrivial object and ask its function space for the adjoint of tensoring with it, probed at the unit and at the object itself: the adjoint's fiber-dimension would have to be the square root of d⁴ − d² + 1 — a number that sits in the gap between (d² − 1)² and (d²)², one d²-wide interval of the number line that the intercept owns outright. The fragment cannot build there, because nothing can: the arithmetic of trace-preservation reserves that interval, at every dimension, for the empty set. Where Theorem Z's counterexample hid inside an interval of VALUES the probes could not see, Theorem BB's emptiness lives inside an interval of SQUARES the dimensions cannot reach — the same grammar (a gap the tests cannot distinguish) at two levels of the construction, one falsifying the implication, the other vacating it.

**Where each hypothesis was spent.** Fullness gave the hom-into-unit reading of the carrier; the unit gave C = I and forced δ(I) = 1; tensor-closure gave that A ⊗ I is in the fragment at all (it is what makes (b) type-check); repleteness was not needed for the kill (it keeps the fragment honest for the corollaries); the Con-equivalence gave (†); nontriviality gave d ≥ 2; and the Con-ENRICHMENT of the adjunction gave the dimension-preservation — the one place the enrichment does work no set-level argument can do, which is doc 70's conditional discharged with the sign reversed: any proof of the enriched form must spend the enrichment, and the disproof spends it too, for without the affineness the bijection carries no dimension and the diagonal lemma never engages (Theorem Z's fragments survive on exactly that absence — their adjunctions are plain). The enrichment is load-bearing at last: it bears on the hypothesis side, where it empties the class.

## Part 3. Corollary BB.2 — the exceptional family emptied, and doc 38's obligation discharged

**Corollary BB.2 (no carriage has an enriched right adjoint, at any nontrivial dimension) [Derived here; Theorem F doc 38's, corrected in scope].** For EVERY environment algebra E with d_E ≥ 2, the carriage T_E = − ⊗ E on Q has no Con-enriched right adjoint. *Proof.* Suppose G is one. The adjunction gives affine bijections Q(T_E A, B) ≅ Q(A, GB) for all A, B ∈ Q. At A = ℂ: T_E ℂ = E, so dim Q(E, B) = dim Q(ℂ, GB), i.e. d_E²(d_B² − 1) = 1²(d_{GB}² − 1) — the intercept equation (IE) at (d_E, d_B), for every B in Q. B ranges over ALL matrix algebras; take B = M_{d_E}: d_{GB}² = d_E⁴ − d_E² + 1, not a square by Lemma 76(i), contradicting d_{GB} ∈ ℕ. ∎

**The correction to doc 38, stated at retraction grade.** Doc 38's Theorem F(ii) exhibited (4, 2, 7), (6, 3, 17), … as dimensions "at which the dimension count does not decide the adjunction," and its Part 4(iii) filed the refinement as Phase 3's one open mathematical obligation, with the conjecture that the Con-naturality in B and the coherence with the discard δ kill the exceptions. The pair-wise reading was the error, and it was doc 38's own: the equation's solvability AT a pair (d_E, d_B) says nothing about the adjunction, because the adjunction quantifies over all B simultaneously — and at B = M_{d_E} the count decides, negatively, at every d_E ≥ 2. The exceptional family is thereby emptied: its members solve (IE) below the diagonal (Lemma 76(ii)) and die on it. The filed obligation is discharged, and it is discharged with less machinery than the filing conjectured would be needed: no naturality, no discard-coherence — the count alone, read functorially. The conjecture is proved in its conclusion and refuted in its mechanism, which is the corpus's standard grammar for a discharge of this kind. The downstream statements inherit the cleaned hypothesis: Theorem K forbids its fragment at every nontrivial environment dimension (no "outside the exceptional family" rider); Corollary O.1's no-go for Con-equivalent tensor-closed residuum-closed fragments holds for every nontrivial E; Theorem P's derivation needs no exceptional caveat; and doc 45's Prop 53 — the trivial environment d_E = 1 located as the exceptional family's degenerate edge — now reads more sharply still: the family has exactly one edge and no interior, and Theorem L's equivalence lives at the family's only point.

## Part 4. Corollary BB.1 — the observer side is trivial

**Corollary BB.1 (the shape of fiber-equivalence) [Derived here].** Let Q′ ⊆ ChuCost be full and Con-equivalent to a fiber (δ as above), and suppose I ∈ Q′ with δ(I) = 1. Then every object of Q′ has a one-point backward carrier; Q′ is a full subcategory of the cost-profile world — objects (A, r̄: A → [0, ∞]), morphisms the carrier maps f: A → B with s̄ ∘ f ≤ r̄, tensor (A, r̄) ⊗ (B, s̄) = (A × B, r̄ + s̄), unit (1, 0). *Proof.* The computation is in Theorem BB's proof: Q′(X, I) ≅ X_X, nonempty of affine dimension δ(X)²(δ(I)² − 1) = 0, hence a single point; so X_X = 1 for every X. The pairing of such an object is a function A × 1 → [0, ∞], i.e. a cost profile r̄; the (L) condition between profile objects is s̄(f(a)) ≤ r̄(a); the tensor's carriers were computed with one-point backward factors: (A × B, 1^B × 1^A = 1), pairing ū(c) + r̄(a). ∎

Two readings. First, the fence's mechanism, finally seen from inside: Proposition 61 located the implication's whole content in "objects outgrowing their probes" — the coupling (A, X, r) being what no probe-count sees. Corollary BB.1 says that in the fiber-equivalent world the coupling is not outgrown but REMOVED: the observer side is a point, the pairing is a profile, and the coupling the ambient residuum would build (A ⊸ B's backward carrier is A × Y — as large as the source's forward) simply has no hom to land in. The fiber-equivalent fragments cannot lose their ambient homs to invisibility, doc 74's reading of Theorem AA; they lose them by construction, before the question opens. Second, the existence question, honestly left open: Theorem BB does not exhibit any full tensor-closed unit-containing fiber-equivalent fragment, and Corollary BB.1's profile world is a constraint, not an example — the corpus knows of no such fragment (the Born CP slice is Con-equivalent to the fiber but not full; its homs are a slice, not the ambient hom), and the theorem's emptiness is the emptiness of the (b)-class, not of the fiber-equivalent class itself. Any example must live in the region Theorem K and Theorem BB leave between them: fiber-equivalent, tensor-closed, unit-containing, and adjoint-free — a fragment that has given up its function spaces entirely.

## Part 6. Coda

Doc 74 ended with the fence relocated to the dimensions and one form left open, and the corpus's habit — four refutations deep into this question — was to expect a fifth hand: either the dimensions would close the fence or a counterexample would slip between them. Theorem BB is neither hand. It is the table being turned over: the fragment the question posits cannot exist, because the adjunction the question grants it cannot exist, because the dimension that adjunction would need is the square root of a number that lies in the one interval of the number line the intercept has owned since doc 38. The question closes not with an answer but with a vacancy — and the vacancy is informative in the way the corpus has learned to price: the fibers' world is closed for the strongest reason a world can be closed, which is that there is no fragment in it to ask about. The exceptional family's death is the same lesson at one remove: doc 38 guarded a door for three phases, saving the exceptions for a refinement (naturality, coherence with the discard) that was never going to be needed, because the count — read all the way to the diagonal, where every adjunction must eventually stand — had already killed everything behind it. The corpus's oldest bridge-lesson, posted at doc 24 and reposted at 66, 70, and 74, posts itself one last time here: the construction's failures are never where the file says they are. The alphabet that reopened at AA finds BB already waiting with the intercept's last coin — the cheapest the bridge has ever minted: a square that cannot be a square, spent where the unit meets the object it came from. The base poset, under it all along, keeps its silence; the question that opened this bridge — what closure costs — has its final entry in the ledger: sometimes it costs nothing, because there was nothing there to close.
