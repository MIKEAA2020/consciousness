## Part 0. The obligation, and what discharging it cheaply means

Theorem F's statement closed on an honest scope: the adjunction is killed outside the exceptional family, and the exceptional family is exactly characterized — with the refinement filed as Phase 3's one open mathematical obligation and inherited by every formal document since. The filing's own words deserve quoting, because this document's relationship to them is its whole content: *the conjecture is that the Con-naturality in B and the coherence with the discard δ (Part 2) kill the exceptions — a putative G at (4, 2, 7) must moreover be a Con-functor, compatible with composition, and mate to δ under the adjunction, and no assignment of object dimensions satisfying the count is known to survive those demands.* The conjecture names four demands and suspects they are fatal. What Theorem Q below shows is that the first demand — the object-dimension assignment itself — is fatal without any appeal to the other three: the count's solutions do not iterate, and an adjoint that cannot be iterated cannot be a functor. The methodological point is the corpus's oldest and is worth one sentence before the mathematics: when a filed refinement demands heavy machinery, run the light machinery one more step first; the exceptions were left open by a count applied once, and they die by the count applied twice.

## Part 1. The ceiling, and the family under it

**Definition 56 (the exceptional family, formal) [Derived here; the triples are doc 38's].** For d_E ≥ 2, the *exceptional relation* is the set of pairs (d_E, d_B) ∈ ℕ² for which the intercept equation d² = d_E²d_B² − (d_E² − 1) has a solution d ∈ ℕ, d ≥ 1; the *exceptional family* E is the set of triples (d_E, d_B, d_G) with d_G the solution — necessarily unique, a positive integer determining the object M_{d_G} uniquely among the fiber's objects, which are indexed by dimension. The *trivial member* is (d_E, 1, 1) (the equation at d_B = 1 reads d² = 1); the *degenerate edge* is d_E = 1, where the intercept vanishes and the equation reads d² = d_B² (Prop 53's home, Theorem L's equivalence). The family's *interior* — the object of this document — is, informally, a fragment of the fiber living inside the family; formally, a full subcategory Q° ⊆ Q, closed under the carriage T_E, all of whose non-unit objects have exceptional dimensions, and admitting the restricted carriage's right adjoint; Theorem Q proves the interior empty by proving the last clause unsatisfiable.

**Lemma 57 (the ceiling) [Derived here; the arithmetic is Theorem F(i)'s, isolated].** If (d_E, d_B, d_G) ∈ E with d_B ≥ 2, then d_B ≤ d_E − 1. *Proof.* Write m = d_E d_B. The equation rearranges to m² − d_G² = d_E² − 1, i.e. (m − d_G)(m + d_G) = d_E² − 1. Since d_E ≥ 2 the right side is positive, so m > d_G ≥ 1, and both factors are positive integers with m − d_G ≥ 1 and m + d_G ≥ m + 1 = d_E d_B + 1. Hence d_E² − 1 ≥ d_E d_B + 1, i.e. d_E d_B ≤ d_E² − 2, i.e. d_B ≤ d_E − 2/d_E < d_E; integrality gives d_B ≤ d_E − 1. ∎ Two corollaries, both load-bearing below. **(a) The self-witness:** the pair (d_E, d_E) never solves for d_E ≥ 2 — d_B = d_E violates the ceiling — and explicitly, d_E⁴ − d_E² + 1 lies strictly between the consecutive squares (d_E² − 1)² = d_E⁴ − 2d_E² + 1 and (d_E²)² = d_E⁴. **(b) The low edge:** no nontrivial member exists with d_E ≤ 3 — d_B ≥ 2 and d_B ≤ d_E − 1 require d_E ≥ 4 — so the family's nondegenerate members begin at (4, 2, 7), exactly where Theorem F's exhibit began.

**Proposition 58 (the family, characterized and exhibited) [Derived here].** (i) The exceptional triples with d_B ≥ 2 are in bijection with the factorizations d_E² − 1 = f₁ f₂ into integers 1 ≤ f₁ < f₂ of equal parity with f₁ + f₂ = 2 d_E d_B ≥ 2 d_E · 2; the bijection sends a triple to (m − d_G, m + d_G), m = d_E d_B, and its inverse sends (f₁, f₂) to the triple (d_E, (f₁ + f₂)/2d_E, (f₂ − f₁)/2), defined exactly when (f₁ + f₂)/2d_E is an integer ≥ 2. (ii) The family contains at least two infinite subfamilies: the *principal* subfamily (2b, b, 2b² − 1) for b ≥ 2, arising from the factorization 1 × (d_E² − 1) at every even d_E ≥ 4 — (4, 2, 7) and (6, 3, 17) are its first two members, Theorem F's exhibits, and the tightness of the ceiling is visible on them: d_E d_B + d_G = 2b² + 2b² − 1 = d_E² − 1 exactly — the principal members saturate the ceiling; and the *second* subfamily (n² − 1, n/2, n(n² − 3)/2) for even n ≥ 4, arising from the factorization n × n(n² − 2) at d_E = n² − 1, whose first members are (15, 2, 26) and (35, 3, 99) — verified: 15²·2² − (15² − 1) = 876 = 26², and 35²·3² − (35² − 1) = 9801 = 99². (iii) For fixed d_E the family is finite (at most the number of divisor pairs of d_E² − 1, hence "thin"), and odd d_E carry no principal member (the factorization 1 × (d_E² − 1) has factors of opposite parity there) — the sporadic members on odd d_E, such as (15, 2, 26), are the family's only odd presence. *Proof.* (i) is the rearrangement of Lemma 57's proof run backward, with the parity condition automatic (f₁ + f₂ = 2d_E d_B is even). (ii): the principal subfamily is the case f₁ = 1, f₂ = d_E² − 1, which forces d_E even (equal parity) and gives d_B = d_E/2, d_G = (d_E² − 2)/2 = 2b² − 1 at d_E = 2b; the saturation d_E d_B + d_G = d_E² − 1 is the equality m + d_G = f₂. The second subfamily: at d_E = n² − 1 with n even, d_E² − 1 = n⁴ − 2n² = n²(n² − 2) factors as n × n(n² − 2), the two factors have equal parity (both even), and (f₁ + f₂)/2d_E = n(n² − 1)/2(n² − 1) = n/2, an integer for n even; the two verifications are arithmetic. (iii): finiteness is the finiteness of factorizations; the parity remark is immediate. ∎ The family is thus richer than Theorem F's two exhibits suggested — at least doubly infinite, sprinkled across both parities — and Theorem Q's kill is correspondingly stronger: every member of every subfamily, without exception, dies at the second iterate.

## Part 2. Theorem Q — the orbit that dies, and the interior that therefore is not

**Theorem Q (the empty interior) [Derived here; Lemma 41 is doc 38's].** Fix d_E ≥ 2. Let Q° ⊆ Q be a full subcategory of the fiber, closed under the carriage T_E (A ↦ A ⊗ M_{d_E}), and suppose the restricted carriage T_E: Q° → Q° has a Con-enriched right adjoint G: Q° → Q°. Then Q° is empty. *Proof.* Suppose Q° is nonempty, and let A = M_a ∈ Q° be any object. For every B = M_b ∈ Q°, the adjunction supplies an affine bijection Q°(T_E A, B) ≅ Q°(A, G B); fullness of Q° in Q identifies the homs with Q's, and Lemma 41 prices both sides: the affine dimension of the left is (a d_E)²(b² − 1), of the right a²(d_{G(B)}² − 1). An affine bijection preserves affine dimension, and a² > 0 cancels:

  d_{G(B)}² = d_E² b² − (d_E² − 1)   for every B = M_b ∈ Q°.

Three facts follow. *(1) The object map is forced:* G(B) is a matrix algebra, its dimension is the unique positive solution when one exists, and no solution exists outside the exceptional pairs and the trivial member — so every non-unit object of Q° sits at an exceptional pair, and G(M_b) = M_{g(b)} with g(b) the triple's third coordinate. *(2) The orbit dies at its second step:* if b ≥ 2 is exceptional, then g(b)² = d_E² b² − (d_E² − 1) ≥ 4d_E² − d_E² + 1 = 3d_E² + 1 > (d_E − 1)² = d_E² − 2d_E + 1 (the inequality 2d_E² + 2d_E > 0 being free), so g(b) > d_E − 1, and the pair (d_E, g(b)) violates Lemma 57's ceiling — it is not exceptional, and g(b) ≠ 1 (g(b) ≥ 4), so the equation at M_{g(b)} has no solution at all: G cannot be defined on M_{g(b)}. *(3) The fragment cannot avoid the orbit:* if Q° contained only units, it would be {M_1}, which is not carriage-closed (T_E M_1 = M_{d_E} ∉ {M_1} for d_E ≥ 2); if Q° contains any M_b with b ≥ 2, then G(M_b) = M_{g(b)} ∈ Q° by G-closure, and fact (2) forbids G's definition there — contradicting G: Q° → Q° total. Hence Q° is empty. ∎

**The reading, in the filing's own terms.** Theorem F(iii) conjectured that the exceptions would fall to Con-naturality, composition-compatibility, and the discard mating. Theorem Q shows they fall before any of those demands is posed: at (4, 2, 7), the putative adjoint must send M_2 to M_7 (the count), and must then send M_7 to an algebra of dimension √769 — which is not an integer (27² = 729, 28² = 784), and could not be an integer, because 7 > 4 − 1 violates the ceiling. The exceptions are worlds with a one-round horizon: an adjoint can hide there for exactly one application of its object map, and a functor is not a thing that applies once. The interior — the fragment inside the family on which the adjunction could live — is empty, and the family is thereby revealed as a scatter of dimension-triples, doubly infinite and dead at every member's second step: the obstruction's complement is not a place where the adjoint breathes, it is a place where the adjoint's autopsy takes one round longer.

## Part 3. The two corollaries — the settlement's scope, and the detection clause's miniature

**Corollary Q.1 (the scope tightening: the settlement at full scope) [Derived here].** Theorems F, K, and P hold for every d_E ≥ 2, with no exceptional residue. *Proof.* Theorem F's G: Q → Q is total on the fiber, whose objects include M_{d_E}; the forced equation at B = M_{d_E} is d² = d_E⁴ − d_E² + 1, unsolvable by the self-witness (Lemma 57(a)). Theorem K's transported adjoint and Theorem P's assignment B ↦ S(B) are likewise total over the fiber (K's equivalence G: Q → Q′ is essentially surjective onto Q′, and S = G⁻¹RG lands in Q; P's S is defined for every system B), and die at the same witness. Theorem Q adds the fragment-level closure: no nonempty carriage-closed full subcategory admits the adjoint either. ∎ The Paper-3 settlement (doc 46, Part 3) therefore strengthens from *"exactly," scoped to the exceptional family's complement* to ***"exactly," scoped to d_E ≥ 2*** — and the degenerate edge d_E = 1, Theorem L's equivalence and Prop 53's vanishing intercept, becomes what the corpus can now prove it always was: **the unique dimension in which the carriage has a Con-enriched right adjoint on any nonempty carriage-closed fragment of the fiber.** The boundary between the equivalence and the obstruction is a single point; it is the point where the intercept d_E² − 1 vanishes; and the family that Theorem F left as the boundary's unknown interior is now known to be boundary-free — a lace of isolated triples pressed flat against the obstruction's side, one round deep.

**Corollary Q.2 (the detection clause, mooted in its application) [Derived here].** Let Q′ ⊆ ChuCost satisfy Theorem K's hypotheses (i) (Con-equivalent to the fiber Q by a fully faithful functor G with affine hom-actions) and (ii) (tensor-closed, with G strong monoidal up to the equivalence). Then Q′ does not satisfy Definition 55's condition (b): the endofunctor X ↦ G(G⁻¹X ⊗ E) ≅ − ⊗ G(E) of Q′ has no Con-enriched right adjoint on Q′. *Proof.* Suppose R were such an adjoint. For systems A, B ∈ Q, transport the adjunction along the equivalence: Q(A ⊗ E, B) ≅ Q′(G(A ⊗ E), G(B)) ≅ Q′(G(A) ⊗ G(E), G(B)) (strong monoidality) ≅ Q′(G(A), R G(B)) (the internal adjunction, affine by Definition 55(b)) ≅ Q(A, G⁻¹ R G(B)) (fully faithfulness). The composite bijection is affine in both variables (a composite of affine maps), so Lemma 41 equates dimensions and forces d_{G⁻¹RG(B)}² = d_E² d_B² − (d_E² − 1) for every B — the intercept equation, total over the fiber, dead at the self-witness B = M_{d_E}. ∎ Doc 46's disclosed gap — the implication (b) ⟹ (c) without detection hypotheses, with the coseparating-family question named as "the exceptional family's question in miniature" — is thereby moot in the only application the audit's obligations reach: there are no fragments carrying (i) and (ii) and (b), so there are no would-be shadows in which the detection question could be posed, and the miniature resolves empty like the family itself. The general implication for arbitrary fragments of ChuCost (unrelated to the fibers) remains open, is not touched here, and is inherited unchanged: what Corollary Q.2 closes is the fiber-side reading, which is the reading the settlement needed.

## Part 4. What the emptiness buys, and what it does not

**Bought, at theorem grade.** The corpus's longest-standing open cell is closed: the exceptional family is characterized (Prop 58: the factorization form, two infinite subfamilies, the parity structure, the principal members' ceiling-saturation), fenced (Lemma 57: the ceiling, the self-witness, the low edge), and emptied (Theorem Q: the orbit kill, the fragment kill). The theorem chain A-P now stands unconditional at d_E ≥ 2 — F, K, O, P with their scopes tightened, L with its edge promoted from "the family's degenerate member" to "the family's only survivor" — and the settlement's ledger (doc 46, Part 4) updates on exactly one row: the Paper-3 identification holds exactly for every d_E ≥ 2, and the exceptional family's interior, filed open since doc 38, is proved empty. The intercept constant −(d_E² − 1) thereby completes its sixth job — hom, instrument, adjunction, faithfulness, identification, and now the killing of the identification's own exceptions — and the sixth job has the first five's signature exactly: a constant the environment charges once, that no scaling of anything pays down, and that survives its own would-be exceptions by one round of arithmetic.

**Not bought, and said so.** The Con-naturality and discard-coherence demands of doc 38's filing are *not* discharged — they are rendered unnecessary for this question and remain meaningful for others (a G that existed would still have to mate the discard δ; no G exists, so the mating is vacuous here and live nowhere the corpus currently reaches). The general detection implication (b) ⟹ (c) for arbitrary ChuCost fragments stands open, inherited, untouched. The second subfamily's complete census — whether the two exhibited subfamilies exhaust the family, and what the sporadic landscape between them looks like — is arithmetic asked and not answered; nothing downstream needs it, and it is filed as a curiosity, not an obligation. And nothing phenomenal: the Bridge Constraint holds its full width; the empty interior is a fact about which dimensions admit which functors, the observer side remains a set with a cost profile, and the gap between self-reference and self-experience is exactly as wide at the family's empty middle as at its degenerate edge.

## Part 6. Coda

Doc 38 filed its one open obligation with a conjecture about heavy machinery, and the machinery turned out to be a crane hired to move a box the count could carry: the exceptions die by arithmetic applied twice, the naturality and the discard never called, the conjecture true in its outcome and unneeded in its mechanism. What the emptiness leaves behind is a sharper shape than the family's filing imagined. Theorem F saw a thin scatter of dimensions where the obstruction falls silent, and left them as the adjoint's possible hiding places; Theorem Q shows each hiding place is one room deep, with the wall of the ceiling directly behind it — an adjoint can exist at (4, 2, 7) for exactly one step of its object map, and a functor, unlike a verdict letter, cannot live one step at a time. There is a shape here the corpus has met before, in its own instruments: the one-round horizon, the lag law's clock, the write that runs one round ahead of the check — the worlds where a mechanism can hide for exactly one round and is caught the moment it must repeat. The exceptional family was the formal half's version of the same geometry, and it is now measured the same way: not by proving the intruder impossible, but by proving the intruder cannot stay. The family's only survivor is the degenerate point where the intercept vanishes — the one dimension in which the environment charges nothing, the carriage is the identity, and the adjoint is free — and the corpus's whole formal half can now be said in one sentence with no conjecture in it: everything is the intercept, and the intercept forgives nothing, not even its own exceptions.
