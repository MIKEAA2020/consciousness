## Part 1. Lemma 78 — the probability intercept

**Definition (the MV fiber) [Registered here].** Q_MV := St: objects the positive integers d; hom(a, b) := St(a, b), the set of row-stochastic a × b matrices (each row a probability distribution on b points); composition the matrix product; identities the unit matrices; each hom carrying its affine structure as a subset of ℝ^{a×b}; the currency layer the Łukasiewicz budget of doc 38's Proposition 48.

**Lemma 78 (the probability intercept) [Derived here].** For all a, b ≥ 1: dim aff St(a, b) = a(b − 1). *Proof.* St(a, b) is the a-fold product of copies of Δ^{b−1} (the rows), one factor per input point; Δ^{b−1} = {p ∈ [0,1]^b : Σpᵢ = 1} is a simplex of affine dimension b − 1; the affine hull of a finite product is the product of the hulls, and dimensions add. ∎

The reading: per unit of input (there are a of them — POINTS, not algebra units), the output pays one dimension of bookkeeping that no scaling reproduces — b − 1, never b − c for c ≠ 1. The intercept family (doc 38's "intercept principle": scale times dimension squared minus an invariant constant) gains its square-free member: quantum a²(b² − 1), classical a(b − 1); and Prop 46's dictum — the square appears the moment outcomes may superpose — now reads as the family's own diagonal, separating the members that carry the input's operator space from the member that carries the input's points. Every emptiness theorem in the question's history lives on the quantum side of that diagonal: Lemma 76's interval (d⁴ − d² + 1 between (d²−1)² and (d²)²) and doc 79's iterate gap (m² + 1 between m² and (m+1)²) are both statements about SQUARES of dimensions. The MV member has no squares to trap.

## Part 2. The unit arithmetic, and Theorem DD — the diagonal disarmed

**The MV home's unit channel (contrast with Corollary BB.1).** In ChuCost_MV the monoidal unit is I = (1, 1, K) (the ⊙-unit is the top cost K). The unit-homs read differently than in the additive home, and the difference structures everything that follows: a morphism I → X is a choice of forward point a₀ ∈ A_X together with the trivial backward map, and the lax condition r(a₀, x) ≤ K holds ALWAYS (costs live in [0, K]) — so **Q′(I, X) is the whole forward carrier**, and the St-equivalence forces dim aff Q′(I, X) = δ(X) · (δ(I) − 1) = δ(X) − 1: the fiber dimension is the carrier's dimension plus one. A morphism X → I is a choice of backward point x₀ with the condition K ≤ r(a, x₀) for all a — a TOP-COST column — and the count forces dim aff Q′(X, I) = δ(X)(δ(I) − 1) = 0 with the hom nonempty, so **every object has exactly one top-cost backward column, but the backward carrier may be arbitrarily large**: the observer SURVIVES in the MV world, where Corollary BB.1 reduced the Con world's observers to a point. The coupling that Proposition 61 fenced — objects outgrowing their probes — is present, not removed, in any MV fiber-equivalent fragment; what is removed (Part 3) is the instrument that measured it.

**Theorem DD (the diagonal disarmed; the BB-analogue fails to engage) [Derived here; Lemma 78 this document's; Definition 55(b)'s enriched-adjunction clause doc 46's].** Let Q′ ⊆ ChuCost_MV be full, replete, tensor-closed, containing I, and St-equivalent via E with nontrivial image (write δ(X) for the dimension of EX; then dim aff Q′(X, Y) = δ(X)(δ(Y) − 1) for all X, Y, and δ(I) = 1 since Q′(I, I) is the unit's unique endomorphism). Suppose Q′ is internally adjointed in the Con-enriched sense. Then for every A, B ∈ Q′ the adjunction's affine hom-bijections, evaluated at C = I (using A ⊗ I ≅ A), force
  δ(R_A(B)) = δ(A)·δ(B) − δ(A) + 1,
an integer at every pair; at B = A: δ(R_A(A)) = δ(A)² − δ(A) + 1. *Proof.* dim aff Q′(A ⊗ I, B) = dim aff Q′(A, B) = δ(A)(δ(B) − 1); the adjunction gives an affine bijection with Q′(I, R_A(B)), of dimension δ(I)(δ(R_A(B)) − 1) = δ(R_A(B)) − 1; affine bijections preserve affine dimension. ∎

The equation SOLVES — at every pair, in every dimension — and that is the theorem's content. The Con world's corresponding equation, δ(R_A(A))² = d⁴ − d² + 1, dies on Lemma 76's interval of squares; the MV equation is linear in the unknown δ, and d² − d + 1 is an ordinary positive integer (1, 3, 7, 13, 21, …). The diagonal lemma does not transport because it CANNOT: its proof is the statement that a certain quadratic in the dimension lands strictly between consecutive squares, and the MV arithmetic never squares. The enrichment is not load-bearing on the hypothesis side here — doc 76's reading of BB — because there is nothing for it to bear: the class the Con world emptied, the MV world merely fails to empty.

## Part 3. Lemma 79 and Proposition 80 — the trap and the failure to transport

**Lemma 79 (the self-hom trap; the pointwise route was never a route) [Derived here].** Let X = (A, r̄) be a profile object (one-point backward carrier) in ANY home — additive, capped, or MV — with |A| ≥ 2, and let the hom X → X carry the pointwise convex structure (the structure the function-set inherits when the sublevels {b | r̄(b) ≤ t} are convex, i.e. the cost is quasi-convex). Then dim aff Q(X, X) = ∞. *Proof.* For a point a ∈ A let S_a := {b ∈ A | r̄(b) ≤ r̄(a)} — the a-th constraint set; the hom is the product Π_{a∈A} S_a with the product structure. Let z be a minimizer of r̄ (compactness or a disclosed choice; if none exists, replace z by any point of strictly smaller cost than some a — the argument needs only one comparable pair). For EVERY a ≠ z with r̄(a) ≥ r̄(z): the set S_a contains both z and a — two points — so S_a is non-degenerate. The affine hull's direction space contains, for each such a, the difference Q_a − P where P is any fixed choice-function and Q_a differs from P only at a (both lie in the hom, being pointwise admissible); the differences {Q_a − P} are linearly independent (they have disjoint single-coordinate supports), and there are |A| − 1 ≥ 1 of them — infinitely many when A is infinite. A continuum carrier makes the support of the hom all of A minus at most the minimizer: infinite dimension. ∎

The corollary for this document's question: no full fragment of any home on continuum-carrier profile objects with pointwise hom-structures is St-equivalent or Q-equivalent (the required dimensions δ(X)(δ(Y) − 1) are finite and positive; the self-hom is infinite). The lemma is retroactive bookkeeping the corpus owes itself: Theorems Z and AA's counterexamples were finite-carrier or subsingleton BECAUSE the pointwise route dies at the first self-hom; doc 76's fiber-equivalent candidates were profile objects with ABSTRACT convex structures because that was the only route left; and doc 79's Theorem CC then closed that route in the additive and capped homes by forcing the product structure through the projections. In the MV world the abstract route is the only route too — and the question is whether doc 79's forcing transports. It does not:

**Proposition 80 (the projections fail; Theorem CC does not transport) [Derived here; Lemma 77 doc 79's].** In ChuCost_MV, for profile objects X = (A, r̄), Y = (B, s̄) with tensor (A × B, u), u(a, b) = r̄(a) ⊙ s̄(b) = max(0, r̄(a) + s̄(b) − K): the coordinate map π_A: A × B → A (with the trivial backward map) is a ChuCost morphism X ⊗ Y → X if and only if [r̄(a) ≤ r̄(a) ⊙ s̄(b) for all (a, b)], which holds exactly when every point of A with positive cost faces s̄ ≡ K on B (or r̄ ≡ 0 on A). In particular the projection is not a morphism in general, Lemma 77's induced affine bijection Q′(I, X⊗Y) ≅ Q′(I, X) × Q′(I, Y) has no MV counterpart, and Theorem CC's iterate equation is not forced: when the carrier arithmetic is forced by fiat instead (dim A_{X⊗n} = n · dim A_X, the forward carrier of a Dialectica tensor being the product), it reads δ(X⊗n) = n(δ(X) − 1) + 1 — an integer at every n ≥ 1, forever. *Proof.* The lax condition for (π_A, id) is r̄(π_A(a, b)) ≤ u((a, b), ∗), i.e. r̄(a) ≤ max(0, r̄(a) + s̄(b) − K). If r̄(a) > 0 and s̄(b) < K the right side is max(0, r̄(a) + s̄(b) − K) < r̄(a) (adding s̄(b) − K < 0 and truncating at 0 only lowers it further): the condition fails. If r̄(a) = 0 it reads 0 ≤ anything: holds. If s̄(b) = K it reads r̄(a) ≤ r̄(a): holds. The rest is Lemma 77's proof run in reverse: every step of doc 79's Theorem CC passes through the affine product identification, and the identification passes through the projections. ∎

The mechanism deserves its sentence: the additive home's tensor composes costs by SUM, and a sum can only exceed its terms — so the projection's inequality r̄(a) ≤ r̄(a) + s̄(b) is free, non-negativity paying for it. The MV home's tensor composes costs by ⊙, and ⊙ can only DISCOUNT — the composed cost sits below r̄(a) whenever the budget has absorbed anything — so the same inequality reverses. The zero-cost regions multiplied in the additive home (Z(X⊗Y) = Z(X) × Z(Y), doc 79's engine); the top-cost regions multiply in the MV home (u = K exactly where both factors are K) — but the unit-hom in the MV home reads the CARRIER, not the top region, and the carrier's product structure is exactly what the failed projections cannot force into the enrichment. The two homes' kills are mirror images, and the mirror's axis is the direction in which the composition law moves a cost.

And its openness is now the unit-inclusive question's ONLY live fiber-form: the plain form refuted (Theorem Z), the Con-enriched form refuted (Theorem AA), the fiber-equivalent form empty in the additive and capped homes (Theorem BB, with Theorem CC's cheaper cause), and the MV form — open, with both kills disarmed and the trap named, open PRECISELY because the square is absent. The corpus records the shape of this openness without pretending it is a result: the question the MV home asks is whether an enrichment can be abstract enough to dodge the self-hom trap while affine enough to carry the St-arithmetic through a tensor that discounts — and no instrument in the corpus's inventory addresses that combination.
