## Part 0. The obligation, and what mapping it means

Doc 46's remark closed Theorem O with an honesty the audit it answered had been written to extract: the implication (b) ⟹ (c) "has a genuine gap," the two representing objects "are Q′-indistinguishable (they admit the same Q′-indexed probes) but are not thereby isomorphic in ChuCost unless Q′'s objects detect isomorphisms at those points," and a coseparating family was filed as sufficient, unproved. Doc 49's Corollary Q.2 then mooted the fiber-side application — no fragment carrying Theorem K's (i) and (ii) satisfies (b) at all, so there are no would-be shadows — and inherited the general question unchanged: "the general implication (b) ⟹ (c) for arbitrary fragments of ChuCost remains open, inherited, and is not touched." This document touches exactly that cell, and its method is the corpus's oldest: before proving or refuting, run the light machinery one more step — here, the single map the two representing objects already carry, and the question of who can see it.

## Part 1. The comparison, and the probes that cannot tell

**Standing setting.** Q′ ⊆ ChuCost is full, replete, tensor-closed, contains ⊥_V, and satisfies (b): for each A ∈ Q′ there is R_A: Q′ → Q′ with Con-enriched natural affine bijections Q′(A ⊗ C, B) ≅ Q′(C, R_A(B)) for all B, C ∈ Q′, the unit and counit lying in Q′'s homs. The ambient category supplies Theorems A and B's tensor and residuum with the currying ChuCost(C, A ⊸ B) ≅ ChuCost(A ⊗ C, B), affine by Theorem B's proof. All of this is docs 27 and 46's, recalled.

**Definition 59 (the comparison) [Derived here].** For A, B ∈ Q′, let ε_{A,B}: A ⊗ R_A(B) → B be the internal adjunction's counit — a Q′-morphism, by (b). Its mate under the ambient currying is **χ_{A,B}: R_A(B) → A ⊸ B**, the *comparison map*: the canonical morphism from the fragment's internal adjoint to the ambient residuum at the same pair.

**Lemma 60 (the probe equivalence) [Derived here; Theorems A, B are doc 27's].** For every C ∈ Q′, the map ChuCost(C, R_A(B)) → ChuCost(C, A ⊸ B) given by post-composition with χ_{A,B} is, up to the identifications of the two adjunctions, the composite bijection Q′(C, R_A(B)) ≅ Q′(A ⊗ C, B) = ChuCost(A ⊗ C, B) ≅ ChuCost(C, A ⊸ B) — in particular it is a bijection, affine, and natural in C ∈ Q′. *Proof.* The internal adjunction's bijection sends g: C → R_A(B) to ε_{A,B} ∘ (A ⊗ g); fullness identifies the hom-sets with ChuCost's; the ambient currying sends k: A ⊗ C → B to its exponential transpose k̃: C → A ⊸ B, and transposition is natural in the pair, so the composite sends g to (ε_{A,B} ∘ (A ⊗ g))˜ = ε̃_{A,B} ∘ g = χ_{A,B} ∘ g — post-composition by χ, as claimed. Each stage is affine (the internal adjunction by (b)'s Con-enrichment; Theorem B's currying by its proof), and composites of affine bijections are affine. ∎

**Proposition 61 (the incorrigibility fence) [Derived here].** (i) For any fragment satisfying the standing hypotheses, and any A, B ∈ Q′, the restricted representables ChuCost(−, R_A(B))|_{Q′} and ChuCost(−, A ⊸ B)|_{Q′} are isomorphic as Con-functors Q′ᵒᵖ → Con; consequently, any proof of (b) ⟹ (c) must invoke structure beyond the covariant hom-probes of Q′ — the objects to be identified are probe-equal by construction. (ii) The gap is not a general phenomenon of closed categories: in **Set**, and in **finite-dimensional vector spaces over any field**, with "fragment" meaning full, replete, tensor-closed, and containing the monoidal unit, every fragment satisfying (b) satisfies (c). *Proof.* (i) is Lemma 60 restated in enriched-Yoneda grammar. (ii) In Set: evaluate the internal adjunction's bijection at C = 1 (the singleton, a member of any tensor-closed fragment containing the unit): Set(1, R) ≅ Set(A × 1, B) = Set(A, B), and Set(1, R) is naturally R's underlying set, so R ≅ B^A = A ⊸ B as sets; repleteness absorbs the isomorphism and B^A ∈ Q′. In Vec_fd the same evaluation at C = k gives Hom(k, R) ≅ Hom(A, B), and Hom(k, R) ≅ R linearly, so R ≅ Hom(A, B) ≅ A* ⊗ B = A ⊸ B; repleteness again. ∎ The fence's reading: the classical categories close the gap because their objects *are* their unit-probes — a set is its cardinality's worth of points, a vector space its dimension's worth of vectors — while ChuCost's general objects are couplings (A, X, r), and the coupling is exactly what no probe-count sees. The question the general implication poses is therefore not "is (b) strong enough" in the abstract but "can ChuCost's couplings support a fragment strong enough to build its own function spaces and weak enough to lose the ambient ones" — and part of that question, part (i), is now known: no probe argument will answer it.

## Part 2. The two detections — structural and arithmetic

**Theorem T (the structural detection) [Derived here; doc 46's filed sufficient condition, proved].** Let Q′ satisfy the standing hypotheses, and suppose Q′ contains a family S that coseparates ChuCost (for every morphism f of ChuCost, if ChuCost(S, f) is a bijection for all S ∈ S, then f is an isomorphism). Then every comparison χ_{A,B} is an isomorphism; hence A ⊸ B ≅ R_A(B) ∈ Q′ by repleteness, and Q′ is residuum-closed — (c). With Theorem O, (b) plus a coseparating family in the fragment restores the full closure-equivalence: (a) and (c) both. *Proof.* By Lemma 60, ChuCost(C, χ_{A,B}) is a bijection for every C ∈ Q′, in particular for every S ∈ S ⊆ Q′; coseparation makes χ an isomorphism. ∎ The proof is one line, and the line was in doc 46's remark — what the promotion from "filed sufficient" to "proved" buys is the lemma it stands on: the map that coseparation must test is not any map but *the* comparison, and the probes test it affinely and naturally, which is to say they test it in the enrichment the whole bridge is built in. The leaner statement also improves the filed one: the family needs only to lie in Q′ and coseparate ChuCost — no closure of S under the tensor is required.

**Definition 62 (arithmetic fragments) [Derived here].** A fragment Q′ (standing hypotheses) is *arithmetic* when it carries: **(i) a profile** δ from objects of ChuCost to a set of codes, with δ(X) = δ(Y) whenever X ≅ Y; **(ii) membership by profile** — X ∈ Q′ if and only if δ(X) ∈ δ(Q′) (rigidity: the fragment is a union of isomorphism-classes selected by codes); **(iii) probe-dimensionality** — a function f with affdim ChuCost(C, X) = f(δC, δX) whenever the hom's affine hull is taken; **(iv) an internal-hom profile** — a function h with δ(A ⊸ B) = h(δA, δB) for all A, B ∈ ChuCost; and **(v) separation** — for codes x, y, if f(c, x) = f(c, y) for all c ∈ δ(Q′), then x = y.

**Theorem U (the arithmetic detection) [Derived here; Lemma 41 is doc 38's].** Every arithmetic fragment satisfying (b) is residuum-closed. *Proof.* Let A, B ∈ Q′ and R = R_A(B) ∈ Q′. For every C ∈ Q′, Lemma 60's affine bijection gives affdim ChuCost(C, R) = affdim ChuCost(C, A ⊸ B), so f(δC, δR) = f(δC, h(δA, δB)) for all δC ∈ δ(Q′); separation (v) gives δR = h(δA, δB) = δ(A ⊸ B). Since R ∈ Q′, δ(A ⊸ B) = δR ∈ δ(Q′), and membership by profile (ii) gives A ⊸ B ∈ Q′. ∎

**Corollary U.1 (the instances, and the fiber's consistency) [Derived here].** (a) The fibers of the corpus's bridge instantiate the schema with δ the algebra dimension, f(x, y) = x²(y² − 1) (Lemma 41), and h the would-be intercept function — f(c, −) is separating for every c ≥ 1 — but fail (b) outright (Theorem F: the carriage has no Con-enriched right adjoint; Theorem Q: no nonempty carriage-closed fragment of a fiber admits one), so Theorem U is vacuous there, which is exactly Corollary Q.2's kill read from the detection side: the fibers are rigid enough to detect and broken enough to need no detection. (b) Set and Vec_fd satisfy Definition 62 with δ the cardinality, respectively the dimension, f(c, x) = |x|^{|c|} at the level of hom-*sets* (affine dimensions after linearization), and h(a, b) = b^a, respectively ab — and Theorem U re-proves Proposition 61(ii) inside the schema. (c) The residual question — whether ChuCost supports a non-rigid (b)-fragment with no coseparating family — is now precisely the general implication's whole content, and Proposition 61(i) is its boundary: such a fragment, if it exists, cannot be shown closed by any probe argument, because its probes were built blind to the difference.

**Remark (the dualizer's probes, and the cheapest open hypothesis).** Definition 55's standing hypotheses put ⊥_V in the fragment — the one object every fragment owns. Proposition 61(ii)'s unit-evaluation suggests the corresponding move in ChuCost: if ChuCost(⊥_V, −) were generating in the relevant sense (the dualizer's probes determining isomorphism classes at internal-hom points), the Set-argument would transfer and (b) ⟹ (c) would hold unconditionally. Whether the Chu construction's dualizer probes that deeply — the coupling r is precisely what a (⊥_V, −)-probe has to read through — is filed as the cheapest open detection hypothesis after coseparation, and the corpus's Prop 51 machinery (the equality core, the transpose) is where its answer would live. Nothing in the audit's obligations reaches it; it is inherited open with a shape now attached.

## Part 3. What the map buys, and what it fences

**Bought, at theorem grade.** The corpus's last inherited formal cell from the fourth-candidate filing is mapped: the general detection implication decomposes into a provable region (Theorems T and U: fragments that carry a coseparating family, or whose objects are profile-rigid — and the second region is the classical world's, where the implication holds outright) and an unanswerable-from-inside region (Proposition 61: non-rigid fragments, where the comparison is probe-invisible by construction). Doc 46's filed sufficient condition is proved (Theorem T), with its hypothesis weakened; the filed gap is precisely located — it is the coupling, the r in (A, X, r), the structure that no profile counts and no unit-probe reads; and the fiber-side story closes consistently (Corollary U.1(a)): the fibers are the arithmetic world's image in ChuCost, rigid and adjoint-broken, which is why doc 49's mooting needed no detection — the fragments that would have needed it do not exist. The theorem chain A–S gains T and U; the detection clause's ledger reads: proved where detection lives, fenced where it cannot.

**Not bought, and said so.** The residual is open and named: a non-rigid, non-coseparated, internally-adjointed fragment of ChuCost whose ambient internal homs escape it — the implication's only remaining counterexample candidate, and by Proposition 61 the only kind of object the question has left. No example is constructed here and none is excluded; the document does not claim the implication, does not refute it, and does not speculate. The dualizer-generation hypothesis is filed, not tested. And nothing phenomenal: the Bridge Constraint holds; "detection" throughout is the bookkeeping sense — which probes see which isomorphisms — and the gap between self-reference and self-experience is exactly as wide at the map as at the filed remark it settles.

## Part 5. Coda

Doc 46 disclosed the gap with a registrar's care: the two representing objects admit the same probes, and sameness of probes is not sameness unless something detects. Doc 49 killed the question's only application and left the general form standing in the ledger's last row. This document's map says the question was never one question: in the worlds where objects are their sizes — the sets, the vector spaces, the fibers — the implication is a theorem twice over (structural and arithmetic), and the probes close it because there is nothing else to be; in the world of couplings, the probes were never going to close it, because the comparison map is exactly the thing they were built equal on. The corpus has now met this shape three times in one session: the overfill clause, mooted because its premise worlds cannot occur; the detection clause, mooted for the fibers because their fragments cannot carry the premise; and the general implication, fenced because its undecided region is invisible to the only arrows pointed at it. Three faces of one discipline — price the world before legislating for it — and the formal half's version of the sentence the instrument half keeps relearning: an instrument's honesty is measured less by what it certifies than by where it can see. The residual lives or dies beyond the probes; if the corpus ever builds the example, it will be built, not found; and the base poset, under it all along, keeps its silence one more phase.
