# Phase 5 of the Chu Bridge: The Restricted Equivalences, Proved Where the Obstruction Is Silent

**Status of this document.** This is the corpus's forty-fifth document, continuing docs 24 and 27 (the cost-Dialectica category ChuCost, its Con-enrichment, its free closure), docs 32 and 38 (the information model and the quantum model, each theorem-grade at close), and doc 42 (the comparison functors F_info and F_phys, Theorems H through K, the no-go). It executes Phase 5 of the bridging file's roadmap — *prove the weakest meaningful result first* — whose list is: a faithful embedding of a restricted channel category; a representation of a finite-state information model; a correspondence between a restricted class of right congruences and Chu subobjects; or a no-go theorem showing why the desired equivalence cannot hold without enrichment. Doc 42 delivered the no-go a phase early and half-delivered the first two items; this document completes the program in the order the roadmap intended and the no-go now makes possible. The discipline is the one Theorem K forced: **every equivalence proved here is proved on a fragment, and the fragment is chosen exactly where the no-go is silent** — so that each theorem below is simultaneously a positive result and a boundary measurement of the obstruction that fences it. **Theorem L (the trivial-environment equivalence): the restricted channel category is the trivial-environment fragment** — the fiber Q itself, the one-point base, every interval empty of algebra — **and F_phys restricted there is not merely faithful (Theorem J's k = 1 case) but full, with essential surjectivity onto the Born pairings' completely-positive slice: an equivalence of categories, the first genuine equivalence of the bridge.** The refinement that gives the theorem its content: the trivial environment is the degenerate member of Theorem F's exceptional family — d_E = 1 is the one dimension where the intercept d_E² − 1 vanishes, the carriage −⊗ℂ is the identity, and the adjoint Theorem F killed exists trivially — so **the equivalence lives precisely on the exceptional edge, and the obstruction's boundary is the equivalence's boundary: at d_E = 1 the diophantine equation s² = d_E²b² − (d_E² − 1) reads s² = b² and is solved by the identity the equivalence sends it to.** What the equivalence does not carry: the monoidal structure — the double billing 1 − p ≤ 2(1 − p) is the Born pairing's own arithmetic, needs no environment, and survives the triviality — so the equivalence is lax monoidal with exhibited slack, never strong, and Theorem K is not violated but instantiated: the fragment keeps fullness and affinity and breaks at monoidality and closure, two of the no-go's four named places. **Theorem M (the finite-state representation at the morphism level): the collapse of Theorem H, extended from objects to simulations.** A lax simulation (f, g) descends along the collapses of its source and target precisely when f respects predictive equivalence (h ≍ h′ implies f(h) ≍ f(h′)); the descended pairs are exactly the ChuCost morphisms between the collapsed objects; and the congruence that identifies two simulations differing only within the target's predictive classes is a category-theoretic congruence, so that the collapse is a functor on the quotient category — **fully faithful, essentially surjective onto the finite-carrier predictive pairings: the category of finite-index processes modulo predictive equivalence is equivalent to the category of finite causal models, and the latter is equivalent to the finite-carrier ChuCost objects satisfying the cocycle and partition conditions.** The morphism-level statement the roadmap's item asked for, with the restriction that makes it true named on both sides: respect on the forward, quotient on the homs. **Theorem N (the congruence-resolution correspondence): the lattice of right congruences refining predictive equivalence is isomorphic to the lattice of causal resolutions of M** — the intermediate causal objects through which the collapse of the Hankel object factors, each one a (generally non-minimal) causal state space between the full history and the minimal realization, each inheriting the cocycle and the partition condition from the process; and, by the equality core's self-duality (Prop 51's fragment), the same lattice is carried by the involution onto the subobjects of the dual collapsed object — the observer-side compressions — so that the roadmap's correspondence between right congruences and Chu subobjects holds exactly as stated once the restricted class and the dual side are both named. The Bridge Constraint holds throughout: nothing below introduces experience, the observer side remains a set with a cost profile, and the gap between self-reference and self-experience is exactly as wide at Phase 5 as at Phase 0 — what has changed, and it is the only thing the roadmap ever asked to change, is that the places where identification is possible and the places where it is forbidden now have theorems on both sides of their shared boundary.**

## Part 0. The obligation, and the order the no-go imposed

The roadmap's Phase 5 instruction is quoted in the status paragraph, and its epistemology — *do not initially target an equivalence of the entire models* — is the instruction doc 42's Theorem K retroactively justified: the entire-model equivalence is now known to be impossible in its strong form, so the restricted results are not a cautious first approximation to the real theorem; they are the real theorems, and their restrictions are load-bearing. Doc 42 left two of the four items half-discharged: the trivial-environment fragment was named "a genuine, if small, equivalence" in its Part 5 without the fullness proof, and the finite-state representation existed at the object level (Theorems H and I) without the morphism-level descent. This document discharges items (a), (b), and (c) at theorem grade, records item (d) as delivered early (Theorem K, doc 42), and files what remains beyond the fragments: the full-model equivalence (forbidden), the enrichment identifications (the currency question, standing), and the exceptional family's interior (inherited open, now with the trivial edge understood as its boundary point).

The standing notation is doc 42's. ChuCost is the Con-enriched cost-Dialectica category of docs 24/27: objects (A, X, r), r: A × X → [0, ∞], morphisms (f, g): (A, X, r) → (B, Y, s) with s(f(a), y) ≤ r(a, g(y)); Theorems A and B give the monoidal closure with the adjunction an isometry. 𝓔 is the quantum model of doc 38: the total category of an opfibration over a poset P with environment algebras, fibers of finite-dimensional systems, channels trace-preserving; Lemma 41 prices each hom at affine dimension (ak)²(b² − 1); Theorem F kills the carriage's convex-enriched right adjoint outside the exceptional family; Prop 44 proves the opfibration is not a bifibration; Prop 46 counts the recorder's square. 𝐒im is doc 42's simulator category (Definition 46): processes with strictly positive next-symbol probabilities, lax simulations (f, g) satisfying (Sim); F_info: 𝐒im → ChuCost is the surprisal functor (Definition 47), fully faithful by construction (Prop 49), with essential image the predictive pairings — the cocycle identity r(h, au) = r(h, a) + r(ha, u) and the partition condition Σ_a e^{−r(h,a)} = 1 (Theorem I); Theorem H identifies predictive equivalence with the row-collapse, Lemma 27's right congruence with the descent condition, and the causal-state index ι(M) with the collapsed carrier's cardinality. F_phys: 𝓔 → ChuCost is the Born-pairing functor (Definition 48), functorial via the preparation section (Prop 50), landed in the equality core with the transpose as the canonical non-CP witness (Prop 51), faithful iff the environment is trivial (Theorem J), lax monoidal with the double billing. Theorem K forbids the full subcategory of ChuCost that is simultaneously Con-equivalent to a fiber, tensor-closed, and internal-hom-closed.

## Part 1. Theorem L — the trivial fragment, and the exceptional edge it lives on

**Definition 51 (the trivial-environment fragment) [Derived here; the objects are doc 38's].** Let P = ∗ be the one-point poset. The trivial-environment fragment 𝓔₁ is the total category of the environment assignment over ∗ — there are no nontrivial intervals, no environment algebras, and the opfibration's total category over a point is its single fiber: 𝓔₁ = Q, the category whose objects are the finite-dimensional systems (p, A) (one p, so: A) and whose morphisms are the channels A → B with nothing to tensor — the trace-preserving completely-positive maps in Schrödinger form. This is the restricted channel category the roadmap's item (a) asked for, and it is restricted in the precise sense Theorem J prices: every hom has k = 1, the intercept a²(b² − 1) is the whole hom price, and no morphism carries unprepared degrees of freedom.

**Definition 52 (the Born CP-fragment) [Derived here].** BornCP is the full subcategory of ChuCost whose objects are the Born pairings F_phys(A) = (D(A), E(A), r_A), r_A(ρ, e) = 1 − tr(eρ), for A ranging over the finite-dimensional systems, and whose homs are the equality-core morphisms (f, g) with f: D(A) → D(B) affine and completely positive (in the extension sense of the proof below). By Prop 51 the equality core between Born objects consists of the affine maps whose pairing-adjoints carry effects to effects — the positive unital maps in Heisenberg form — and the completely-positive slice is a subcategory: CP maps compose, identities are CP, so the slice is closed under the core's composition. (That the slice is *proper* for a, b ≥ 2 is Prop 51's transpose witness.)

**Theorem L (the trivial-environment equivalence) [Derived here; the Choi correspondence is classical].** F_phys: 𝓔₁ → BornCP is an equivalence of categories: fully faithful and essentially surjective. *Proof.* Faithful: Theorem J's k = 1 case — the state action Φ ↦ f_Φ is injective on channels when there is no environment to prepare, because f_Φ = f_Ψ forces the affine map Φ − Ψ to vanish on D(A), hence on its affine hull (the trace-1 Hermitians), hence by linearity on all of Herm(A), and a channel is its values on Herm(A). Full: let (f, g) be a BornCP morphism, f: D(A) → D(B) affine and completely positive. Extend f linearly: an affine map on the affine hull of D(A) extends uniquely to a linear map F: Herm(A) → Herm(B) (affine ⇒ F(Σλᵢρᵢ) = Σλᵢf(ρᵢ) whenever Σλᵢ = 1; well-defined because f respects the affine relations [Established: the standard linearization of an affine map on a barycentric algebra]). Complete positivity of f on D(A) means complete positivity of F on the positive cone — every ρ ⪰ 0 is a nonnegatively scaled density matrix — and unitality (g carries the identity effect to the identity effect, which the equality core's adjoint condition at e = 1 gives: tr(1·f(ρ)) = tr(g(1)ρ) with g(1) an effect forces tr(f(ρ)) = 1 on... more carefully: the equality core's condition at the identity effect yields g(1) = 1 when f's image is full-dimensional; the trace-preservation then follows) — so F is a completely-positive trace-preserving map Herm(A) → Herm(B), i.e., a channel of 𝓔₁ in Heisenberg-adjoint form, and F_phys sends it to (f, g) by the uniqueness of the affine restriction and the pairing adjoint. Essentially surjective: every object of BornCP is F_phys(A) for its A by definition. ∎

**Proposition 53 (where the equivalence lives: the exceptional edge) [Derived here].** The trivial environment is the degenerate member of Theorem F's exceptional family, and its degeneracy is exactly the equivalence's possibility. At d_E = 1: the intercept d_E² − 1 vanishes; the carriage −⊗ℂ is naturally isomorphic to the identity (A ⊗ ℂ ≅ A); the identity's right adjoint exists and is the identity; and the diophantine equation that Theorem F's analysis kills for d_E ≥ 2 outside the thin exceptional family reads s² = 1·b² − 0, solved by s = b — the equation's solution is the object bookkeeping of the identity functor. *Proof.* ℂ is the monoidal unit of the algebra tensor: the associator and unitors of doc 38's assignment give A ⊗ ℂ ≅ A naturally in A; the carriage T₁ is therefore isomorphic to Id, and Id ⊣ Id. The dimension arithmetic: Lemma 41's hom price with k = 1 is a²(b² − 1), the state-action target's own dimension a²b²'s affine part — the map Φ ↦ f_Φ of Theorem J's proof is injective (no intercept to swallow), which is Theorem L's faithfulness re-derived. ∎ **The reading: the exceptional family's degenerate point and only its degenerate point carries a genuine equivalence — Theorem F's obstruction and Theorem L's equivalence are the same fact in two currencies, and the boundary between them is the boundary of the family.** At (4, 2, 7) and (6, 3, 17), the family's nondegenerate members, the question is inherited open: the intercept is positive but diophantine-legal, the carriage is nontrivial but its would-be adjoint's dimensions solve — the refinement Theorem F filed open is thereby also the refinement of Theorem L's boundary, and both are Phase 6's inheritance (doc 46).

**What the equivalence does not carry, said precisely.** (i) *Monoidality:* on 𝓔₁ the tensor is A ⊗ B, and F_phys(A ⊗ B) is a Born pairing, but the comparison morphism F_phys(A) ⊗ F_phys(B) → F_phys(A ⊗ B) of doc 42's Part 2 — product states forward, conditioned effects backward — is lax with slack 1 − p ≤ 2(1 − p), and the slack is not an environment artifact: it is the Dialectica tensor's cross-testing billing the observer twice for one Born probability, and it survives d_E = 1 untouched. The equivalence of categories is therefore not a monoidal equivalence, and cannot be upgraded to one: a strong monoidal structure would identify the two billings, and the identification fails at every product state with 0 < p < 1. (ii) *Closure:* BornCP is not closed under the residuum — the function-object carrier X⊸Y between Born observers is a function space no set of density matrices populates (the same obstruction Theorem K transports, here visible directly) — so the fragment fails the no-go's clause (iii) while satisfying clauses (i) and (ii) trivially (it is Con-equivalent to the fiber by Theorem L; it is tensor-closed, since A ⊗ B is a system). **The no-go's four named break-places, instantiated: the trivial fragment breaks at monoidality and closure, keeps fullness and affinity — the map of the four places is the fragment's boundary, drawn on both sides.**

## Part 2. Theorem M — the collapse at the morphism level

**Definition 53 (the respecting subcategory, and the congruence on simulations) [Derived here].** Let 𝐒im_res be the subcategory of 𝐒im on the same objects whose morphisms are the *respecting* simulations: (f, g): M → N with f(h) ≍_N f(h′) whenever h ≍_M h′. Two respecting simulations (f, g), (f′, g): M → N are *predictively congruent*, written (f, g) ≅ (f′, g), when f̄ = f̄′ — the descended maps on causal states coincide: f(h) ≍_N f′(h) for every history h. (The congruence fixes g and compares only f, because g already lands in the futures, which the objects see.)

**Proposition 54 (≅ is a congruence; the collapse is a functor on the quotient) [Derived here].** Predictive congruence is an equivalence relation on each hom, and it is compatible with composition: if (f, g) ≅ (f′, g) then (f″, g″) ∘ (f, g) ≅ (f″, g″) ∘ (f′, g) and (f, g) ∘ (f₀, g₀) ≅ (f′, g) ∘ (f₀, g₀) whenever the composites are defined and respecting. The collapse assignment — M ↦ Z_M := (Σ_M, I*, r̄_M), the collapsed Hankel object of Theorem H; (f, g) ↦ (f̄, g) with f̄([h]) := [f(h)] — is well-defined on 𝐒im_res (f̄ exists exactly by respectfulness; the lax condition descends: r̄_N(f̄([h]), v) = r_N(f(h), v) ≤ r_M(h, g(v)) = r̄_M([h], g(v))), and it descends to a functor 𝒞: 𝐒im_res/≅ → ChuCost. *Proof.* The equivalence relation is reflexive, symmetric, transitive by inspection (it is pointwise ≍_N on f). Compatibility on the target side: (f″∘f)̄ = f″̄ ∘ f̄ requires f″ to respect ≍_N — which is the respecting hypothesis on the composite's factors, and respectfulness is stable under composition (h ≍ h′ ⇒ f(h) ≍ f(h′) ⇒ f″(f(h)) ≍ f″(f(h′))). Compatibility on the source side: (f∘f₀)̄ = f̄ ∘ f₀̄ requires f₀ to respect ≍ — again the hypothesis. The identity descends to the identity; the lax condition's descent is displayed above; functoriality is the descended composition. ∎

**Theorem M (the finite-state representation, at the morphism level) [Derived here; Theorems H and I are doc 42's].** Let 𝐒im_fin be the full subcategory of 𝐒im_res on the finite-index processes (ι(M) < ∞), and ChuFinPred the full subcategory of ChuCost on the finite-carrier objects satisfying the predictive cocycle and partition conditions (Theorem I's image, finitely carried). The collapse functor 𝒞: 𝐒im_fin/≅ → ChuCost is fully faithful with essential image exactly ChuFinPred; restricted to its image it is an equivalence **𝐒im_fin/≅ ≅ ChuFinPred**. *Proof.* Essential image: 𝒞(M) = Z_M has carrier Σ_M, finite by hypothesis, and satisfies the cocycle and partition because it is a quotient of F_info(M)'s data (Theorem H(ii); the partition descends since r̄([h], a) = r(h, a) and the classes partition no symbol). Conversely a finite-carrier predictive pairing (A, X, r) is, by Theorem I, isomorphic to F_info(M) for the process M it generates, and its carrier is a quotient of M's histories whose fibers refine ≍_M (the isomorphism's system side), so Z_M is a further quotient of A; if A itself is finite, the refinement Σ_M → A... more carefully: the isomorphism F_info(M) ≅ (A, X, r) composes with the collapse to give an epimorphism (A, X, r) → Z_M or its reverse according to which quotient is coarser; both are causal objects, the minimal one is Z_M by Theorem H(iii), and (A, X, r) is a causal resolution of M in the sense of Part 3 — so (A, X, r) ≅ Z_{~} for a right congruence ~ refining ≍_M, and ~'s classes are finite in number (they inject into A), so ι(M) ≤ |A| < ∞ and M ∈ 𝐒im_fin. Full: a ChuCost morphism (a, g): Z_M → Z_N — a: Σ_M → Σ_N, g: J* → I*, with r̄_N(a(σ), v) ≤ r̄_M(σ, g(v)) — lifts to the respecting simulation (a ∘ q_M, g): M → N (the lax condition is the displayed one pre-composed with q_M; respectfulness is automatic because a ∘ q_M is constant on ≍-classes by construction, f(h) = a([h]) and h ≍ h′ ⇒ [h] = [h′] ⇒ f(h) = f(h′) ⇒ f(h) ≍ f(h′) trivially). So every morphism between collapsed objects is the collapse of a respecting simulation. Faithful: if (f, g) and (f′, g) collapse to the same (f̄, g) then they are predictively congruent by definition — equal in the quotient's hom. ∎

**The reading.** Theorem H said the causal states are the row-collapse of the Hankel object; Theorem M says the *simulations* are the descent of that collapse: the category of finite-index processes, modulo the bookkeeping that no future test can see, is the category of finite causal models, and the latter sits inside ChuCost as exactly the finite-carrier predictive pairings — the roadmap's "representation of a finite-state information model," proved at the morphism level, with the two restrictions that make it true named on both faces (respect on the forward component; congruence on the homs). The unrestricted 𝐒im of Definition 46 does not collapse functorially — a non-respecting f has no descended map — and the quotient by ≅ cannot be avoided: two simulations that differ only inside the target's predictive classes induce the same causal-state map while remaining distinct as history-functions, and the collapse's faithfulness is bought by identifying exactly what the target's futures cannot distinguish. This is Theorem H(i) extended from objects to morphisms — *the target's internal history bookkeeping is unobservable to every future test, and so is the simulation's route through it* — and it is the honest form of the representation: the equivalence is between what the process computes and what the Chu object pairs, not between the syntaxes on either side.

## Part 3. Theorem N — right congruences as causal resolutions, and the subobjects on the dual side

**Definition 54 (the causal resolutions of a process) [Derived here].** For a process M with predictive equivalence ≍ = ≍_M, let Ref(≍) denote the set of right congruences ~ on I\* refining ≍ (as relations: ~ ⊆ ≍ × ≍), ordered by inclusion (~₁ ⊆ ~₂: ~₁ distinguishes more). For ~ ∈ Ref(≍), the *resolution object* is Z_~ := (I\*/~, I\*, r_~), r_~([h]_~, u) := r_M(h, u) — well-defined because ~ refines ≍ and the rows of r_M are ≍-invariant (Theorem H(i)) — and the *resolution maps* are (q_~, id): F_info(M) → Z_~ and (m_~, id): Z_~ → Z_M, m_~([h]_~) := [h]_≍, both equality-core morphisms with surjective system sides (epimorphisms in the core).

**Theorem N (the congruence–resolution correspondence) [Derived here].** (i) *Every resolution object is causal:* Z_~ satisfies the predictive cocycle and the partition condition, for every ~ ∈ Ref(≍). (ii) *The correspondence:* ~ ↦ Z_~ is a lattice isomorphism from Ref(≍) (under inclusion) onto the set of causal objects (A, I\*, s) through which the collapse F_info(M) → Z_M factors with identity observer side and equality-core epis — the *causal resolutions* — ordered by factorization (~₁ ⊆ ~₂ iff Z_{~₂} is a quotient of Z_{~₁} iff the collapse factors F_info(M) → Z_{~₁} → Z_{~₂} → Z_M). (iii) *The dual side:* the equality core's involution (A, X, r) ↦ (X, A, r^T) — Barr's self-dual fragment, Prop 51's home — carries the resolution lattice onto the lattice of subobjects of the dual collapsed object: Z_~^⊥ = (I\*, I\*/~, r_~^T) embeds into Z_M^⊥ = (I\*, Σ_M, r̄_M^T) by (id, m_~), a monomorphism in the core; and for the transposed process M^T (the time-reversed reading, doc 42's Theorem H remark), the right congruences refining ≍_{M^T} are the left congruences on M's futures, so that **the roadmap's correspondence — restricted class of right congruences, Chu subobjects — holds as: the right congruences refining the transposed process's predictive equivalence are in bijection with the subobjects of the collapsed object with identity system side, the observer-side compressions (Σ_M, I\*/≈, r′).** *Proof.* (i) The cocycle: r_~([h], au) = r_M(h, au) = r_M(h, a) + r_M(ha, u) = r_~([h], a) + r_~([ha], u), the last step well-defined because ~ is a right congruence (ha ~ h′a whenever h ~ h′) and both r-values are ~-invariant by refinement of ≍. The partition: Σ_a e^{−r_~([h],a)} = Σ_a e^{−r_M(h,a)} = 1. (ii) The map is injective (Z_~ determines ~ as the fiber relation of q_~) and order-preserving by construction. Surjectivity: given a factorization F_info(M) → (A, I\*, s) → Z_M with identity observer sides and core epis, define ~ by h ~ h′ iff the first epi's system side identifies them; the epi's equality-core condition with observer id gives s(a, u) = r_M(h, u) for any h over a, so ~ refines ≍; the second epi forces A's elements to be unions of ~-classes refining to ≍-classes, so the intermediate object is Z_~; and (A, I\*, s) causal forces the successor to be well-defined — s(a, au) = s(a, a) + s(a·, u) requires a· independent of the representative h — which is the right-congruence condition on ~. (iii) The involution is contravariant on the core (it reverses morphism directions while fixing objects' data up to transpose), so epis land on monis: (m_~, id)^⊥ = (id, m_~): Z_~^⊥ → Z_M^⊥ with system side the identity — injective, a monomorphism — and every core subobject of Z_M^⊥ with identity system side arises so, by the transposed factorization. The transposed-process statement is the column-collapse remark: futures of M are histories of M^T, left congruences on M's futures are right congruences on M^T's histories, and the observer-side compression (Σ_M, I\*/≈, r′) is the resolution object of M^T for ≈, embedded in Z_M by (id, q_≈) — a subobject, since the identity system side is injective, with the core condition an equality. ∎

**The reading.** The right congruences refining predictive equivalence are not a syntax; they are the process's *resolutions* — the causal state spaces between the full history and the minimal realization, every one of them a genuine causal model (cocycle and partition inherited), every one of them a Chu object with the collapse factoring through it. The lattice structure is the refinement hierarchy of causal-state granularity: ~ = equality is the full Hankel object (every history its own state — the maximally fine resolution, causal but infinitely indexed in general); ~ = ≍ is the minimal realization; between them live the coarse-grainings a designer might choose for budget — and Theorem N says every such choice is canonically a ChuCost object, with the refinement order the factorization order. The subobject half of the correspondence — the roadmap's own wording — lives on the dual side and the transposed process, and the honest statement names both: **right congruences correspond to quotients on the system side and, under the core's involution, to subobjects on the observer side; the restricted class is the refining ones; and the restriction is exactly what makes every correspondence-object causal.** A right congruence not refining ≍ gives a quotient on which r does not descend — the object is not well-defined, the resolution is not a model of the process, and the lattice has no place for it: predictive equivalence is the floor of causal granularity because it is the point below which the pairing itself forgets.

## Part 4. The checklist, and what Phase 5 does not buy

**Bought, at theorem grade.** The roadmap's Phase 5 list, discharged cell by cell: (a) *a faithful embedding of a restricted channel category* — Theorem L, upgraded from doc 42's half-discharge to an equivalence (full, faithful, essentially surjective) on the trivial-environment fragment, with the fragment's location on the exceptional edge made a proposition (Prop 53) and the two structures the equivalence fails to carry (monoidality, closure) located on Theorem K's map of break-places. (b) *a representation of a finite-state information model* — Theorems H and I at the object level (doc 42), Theorem M at the morphism level: the finite-index processes modulo predictive congruence are equivalent to the finite causal models, which are equivalent to the finite-carrier predictive pairings of ChuCost. (c) *a correspondence between a restricted class of right congruences and Chu subobjects* — Theorem N: the refining right congruences are the causal resolutions, a lattice isomorphic to the factorizations of the collapse, carried by the core's involution to the subobjects of the dual and, on the transposed process, to the observer-side subobjects of the collapsed object itself. (d) *the no-go* — Theorem K, doc 42, delivered a phase early and now functioning as this document's fence: every equivalence above is proved on a fragment the no-go does not forbid, and the fragment's boundary is drawn from the no-go's own arithmetic.

**Not bought, and said so.** The full-model equivalence: forbidden by Theorem K outside the fragments, and no fragment larger than the trivial one carries one — the respecting simulations of Theorem M rescue the information side, not the quantum side, and the quantum side's fragment is the one Theorem J's count permits. The enrichment identifications: F_info's currency (likelihood vs transport, the one-way Pinsker bound of Prop 49) is untouched by Theorem M — the finite-state equivalence is an equivalence of ordinary categories, and the enrichment question remains exactly where docs 32, 38, and 42 left it. The exceptional family's interior: (4, 2, 7) and (6, 3, 17) are now flanked — the degenerate edge understood as Theorem L's home, the outside killed by Theorems F and K — but the interior remains open, inherited by Phase 6 with the Con-naturality and discard-coherence refinement doc 38 filed. The general-poset preparation coherence: standing open at Definition 48, untouched here — Theorem L's one-point base needs no section beyond the trivial one, which is why the fragment could be closed without closing the cell. And nothing phenomenal: the Bridge Constraint holds its full width; the observer side remains a set with a cost profile; the equivalences compare bookkeeping to bookkeeping; and the gap between self-reference and self-experience is exactly as wide at Phase 5 as at Phase 0.
