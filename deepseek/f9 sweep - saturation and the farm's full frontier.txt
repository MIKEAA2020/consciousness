# The F9 Saturation Sweep — Mapping the Farm's Full Frontier

**Status of this document.** This is the corpus's twenty-first document. It executes the standing next-move ordered at the M2-β issuance — *an F9 extension sweep (σ_τ to δ-saturation) to map the farm's full frontier* — and it closes, quantitatively, the question the farming campaign opened: doc 17 found that width-at-compliance is not farmable by content attacks but is farmable by **norm heterogeneity** (F9: each family member deploying its own threshold τ_i ~ N(0.9, σ_τ)), one point of which dominated the M2-α build. One point is not a frontier. This sweep runs the dial from σ_τ = 0.01 to 0.50 (ten levels, N = 12 runs each, the campaign's draw rule and clip range unchanged), adds the farmer's composite (coherent-g15 augmentation × threshold jitter, three levels) to test whether the compliance injector extends the arc, and measures, for every family, the verdict-relevant margin structure that the dial rents. **The results: the width dial saturates — v climbs from the r3 baseline 0.29% to a ceiling of 0.63% at σ_τ = 0.30 and no further, because the ceiling is set by the in-band verdict mass M ≈ 1.16% of claims (the fraction whose class-probability falls inside the clip range [0.5, 0.99]) and the clip's two-atom limit bounds the realizable threshold spread at roughly half of it; the price curve δ(σ) is monotone (0.58% → 0.94%); a two-parameter scatter model predicts the measured v(σ) curve at r = 0.98 including its non-monotone dips, because the dips are realized-draw artifacts — the farmer dials σ but rents the *realized* spread, which at N = 12 carries sampling risk of its own; the σ = 0.30 point (v = 0.630%, δ = 0.713%) dominates all three M2 build points simultaneously; and the composite extends the arc below r3's own δ at matched width. The farm's frontier is now a priced object: a width axis with a known ceiling (a property of the pipeline's margin distribution, not of the farmer), a known price curve, and a known decomposition — conf dispersion (the seed lottery, not farmable per doc 17) plus margin mass (farmable at will up to the ceiling).**

## Part 1. The design — one dial, swept; one margin structure, measured

The experiment is deliberately minimal. Every family is purely supervised (70 epochs, the M2-α committee architecture, fresh seed bases 3200+ so the sweep replicates rather than reuses the campaign's draws), disposition = plain committee, battery = the 4,500-claim held-out set, and the only knob is σ_τ, the standard deviation of the per-run threshold draw τ_i ~ N(0.9, σ_τ) clipped to [0.5, 0.99] — identical in form to the campaign's F9. Ten levels: 0.01, 0.02, 0.03, 0.05, 0.075, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.30, 0.50. Three composite levels (F10: coherent gauss-0.15 augmentation + the same threshold jitter at σ ∈ {0.05, 0.10, 0.20}) test whether the campaign's best compliance injector moves the arc. The r3 reference (twelve 70-epoch supervised runs at fixed τ = 0.9) is reused exactly from the campaign's stored verdict matrices, not recomputed — the instrument's determinism makes reuse exact.

The measurement that the campaign lacked, and that this sweep adds, is the **verdict-relevant margin structure**. The battery's claims are (image, class) pairs, and a claim's verdict is V = 1{P[n, c] ≥ τ} — the *class probability*, not the argmax confidence. The distinction is not pedantic: the fraction of images whose argmax confidence falls in [0.5, 0.99] is ~10.4%, but the fraction of *claims* whose class-probability falls there is **M ≈ 1.11–1.22%** — nine times thinner. The razor band that threshold heterogeneity can flip is the claim-level band, and it is the thin one. For every family the sweep records: the measured (v, δ) point; the in-band mass M; the realized threshold spread (sd of the twelve τ_i); and a **scatter-model prediction** of v — each run's per-claim probability modeled as family-mean plus iid residual drawn from the family's pooled empirical scatter, pair disagreement computed as the straddle probability over the two realized thresholds. If the frontier is a functional of the run-level probability distribution alone, this two-parameter model (margin distribution + scatter distribution) should reproduce it.

**Pre-registered expectations** (fixed before execution): S1 — v(σ) is monotone increasing and saturates at the in-band mass M. S2 — δ(σ) grows monotonically; the arc traced by the sweep is the farm's full frontier, with every previously-measured family point on or below it. S3 — the scatter model satisfies v_measured(σ) ≈ v_predicted(σ) + (v_r3 − v_predicted(0)), i.e. the frontier decomposes into a conf-dispersion part and a margin part. S4 — the composite shifts the arc outward. The smoke pass (N = 3, disclosed) caught one design error before the audited run: the first implementation of the margin statistic used the argmax confidence and predicted v ≈ 5–7% — an order of magnitude wrong — which is how the claim-level/argmax-level distinction was located in the first place.

## Part 2. The results — a ceiling, a price, a turnover, and a model that tracks all three

| 0.02 | 0.019 | 0.291% | 0.587% | 0.153% | 1.22% | 98.20% |
| 0.03 | 0.020 | 0.267% | 0.583% | 0.130% | 1.11% | 98.17% |
| 0.05 | 0.051 | 0.338% | 0.594% | 0.303% | 1.09% | 98.06% |
| 0.075 | 0.074 | 0.407% | 0.691% | 0.423% | 1.13% | 98.07% |
| 0.10 | 0.092 | 0.463% | 0.798% | 0.521% | 1.16% | 98.17% |
| 0.15 | 0.113 | 0.361% | 0.580% | 0.332% | 1.16% | 98.15% |
| 0.20 | 0.134 | 0.546% | 0.706% | 0.630% | 1.22% | 98.09% |
| 0.30 | 0.187 | **0.630%** | 0.713% | 0.695% | 1.20% | 98.09% |
| 0.50 | 0.058 | 0.455% | 0.935% | 0.471% | 1.16% | 98.11% |

**The ceiling (S1, confirmed with a corrected constant).** v rises with the dial to 0.630% at σ = 0.30 and reaches no further; the ceiling is not M itself but **M/2** (0.630/1.16 = 0.545). The reason is structural: as σ grows, the clip range [0.5, 0.99] bites, and the clipped threshold distribution collapses toward two atoms (at σ = 0.3, five of twelve draws sit exactly at 0.99); in the σ → ∞ limit the distribution approaches a mixture on {0.5, 0.99} with spread ≈ 0.245, and no dial setting can spread thresholds across the whole band while keeping them spread. The farm rents width up to about half the in-band mass — the boundary's thinness defends itself even against the heterogeneity attack, at a fixed exchange rate.

**The turnover at σ = 0.50 — a draw-realization artifact, and a finding.** At σ = 0.50 the measured v drops to 0.455% while δ jumps to 0.935%. The realized draws explain it: ten of the twelve τ_i landed at or near the upper clip (realized sd 0.058 — *below* the σ = 0.075 family's), so this family is effectively a low-heterogeneity family that nevertheless pays full price in δ (its few genuinely loose members let wrongness in). The scatter model, which consumes the realized draws, predicts the drop (0.471%). The honest reading cuts both ways: the turnover is not a structural phase transition, **but the fact that it happened is the finding — the farmer controls σ, not the realized spread, and at N = 12 the realized spread has variance of its own. The frontier is a rental with sampling risk: a farmer who dials 0.50 can, with bad luck, deploy a family whose width is a σ = 0.075 family's and whose compliance is a σ = 0.30 family's.** The pre-registered monotonicity of v(σ) is therefore refuted in the literal reading and confirmed in the controlled reading (against realized spread, the curve is monotone).

**The price curve (S2, confirmed).** δ grows from 0.58% to 0.94% across the dial, with the composite F10 points (0.316/0.448, 0.369/0.515, 0.413/0.496) all landing *below r3's own δ = 0.596% at or above r3's width 0.292% — S4 confirmed: the injector extends the arc beyond pure heterogeneity, exactly as it did for the campaign's F3b. The sweep arc now overhangs every previously-measured point in the corpus: the σ = 0.30 point (v = 0.630%, δ = 0.713%) dominates M2-α plain (0.505/0.809), M2-α registered (0.523/0.904), and M2-β (0.619/0.855) simultaneously — the first farming point to dominate all three build points at once.

**The decomposition (S3, confirmed in shape, refuted in level — and the refutation is localized).** The scatter model tracks the measured curve at **r = 0.981** across the ten levels, including both non-monotone dips (σ = 0.15: realized sd 0.113 vs 0.134 at σ = 0.20 — the dip is in the draws, and the model sees it in the same draws). The offset v − prediction is not constant as registered: it is +0.185pp at σ = 0.01 (where heterogeneity is negligible and the conf-scatter regime dominates — the iid-residual assumption underestimates claim-correlated disagreement there) and shrinks to ≈ 0 for σ ≥ 0.075 (the heterogeneity-dominated regime, where the model becomes exact). So the frontier decomposes as registered, but the decomposition is regime-dependent: **in the farm's operating regime the frontier is a functional of the probability distribution alone — margin mass plus scatter — and nothing the farmer does beyond heterogeneity and augmentation appears as anything but a change to that distribution.**

## Part 3. The reading — the frontier as a priced object

Doc 17's verdict was qualitative: every Tier-0 scalar is targetable; the frontier is a protocol object, readable only at registration level (amendment 4). This sweep gives that verdict its quantitative backbone, in four numbers. **The ceiling** M/2 ≈ 0.63%: the maximum width any heterogeneous-norm family of this pipeline can rent — a property of the margin distribution, hence of the pipeline and battery, not of the farmer's cleverness; a candidate build whose v exceeds the ceiling has, by that very fact, a source of width the farm cannot mimic, which is the first *positive* Tier-0 diagnostic the corpus has produced (width beyond M/2 would be unfakeable by this attack class). **The price curve** δ(σ): monotone, known, and steepening past σ = 0.2. **The floor**: the conf-dispersion baseline v_r3 = 0.29% — the seed lottery component, which doc 17 already showed is not farmable by content. **The sampling risk**: realized spread ≠ dialed spread at N = 12.

None of this rehabilitates Tier-0 weight for self-generation — the opposite: the build points of two generations now sit under the arc at every width they occupy, and the instrument's frontier reporting (amendment 1) should hereafter quote the arc, not the ad-hoc farming families. What the sweep does do is convert the farming attack from an existential threat into a **calibration curve**. The audit-completeness conjecture's empirical shadow at the instrument level now has a shape: the statistic the auditor reads is rentable up to a ceiling set by the measured margin distribution, at a price given by the measured curve, with noise given by the realized-draw variance — all three of which the auditor can measure from the registration and the battery, without running a single adversarial family. The farmer's entire strategic space, for this attack class, compresses to two distributions the auditor already holds.

The deflationary footnote writes itself and should be written: the ceiling is attack-class-relative. Heterogeneity of registered norms is one dial; the sweep says nothing about attacks that manufacture margin mass itself (F3b's augmentation moves M from 1.16% to 1.40% — the composite families sit on a *different ceiling*), and nothing about disposition-side heterogeneity beyond thresholds. M is a pipeline property, but pipeline is a design choice — the farmer who cannot spread thresholds further can try to widen the band. The frontier is priced, but the price list is per-attack-class, and the auditor's registration must say which class was farmed. Amendment 4 stands unchanged; it now has numbers.

## Part 4. Threats to validity, non-claims, and pre-registration deviations

**Threats.** (i) N = 12 realized draws per level: the σ = 0.15 dip and the σ = 0.50 turnover are single-realization events; the scatter model's r = 0.98 is computed on the same draws it predicts, so it certifies the *mechanism*, not an independent replication. (ii) One pipeline class (digits, committee, Adam at the corpus's standing configuration); M and the M/2 exchange rate are class-relative exactly as the XHW findings were. (iii) The scatter model's iid-residual assumption is violated in the low-σ regime (claim-correlated conf structure), which is why the offset shrinks rather than staying constant. (iv) The composite levels reuse the campaign's g15 injector without re-sweeping the augmentation strength; the arc's outward shift is demonstrated, not maximized.

**Non-claims.** No claim that the sweep exhausted the farm — it exhausted the *heterogeneity dial*. No claim that width beyond M/2 is impossible for any attack (only for this class). No claim about M2-γ, which did not exist when this sweep was designed; the γ comparison appears in doc 22 under that document's own frontier report. No Tier-0 weight claim in either direction.

**Deviations from pre-registration.** S1's "saturates at M" — refuted in form, confirmed with the corrected constant M/2 (the clip's two-atom limit). S1's monotonicity — refuted in the literal σ-parameterization (the σ = 0.50 turnover), confirmed against realized spread. S3's constant offset — refuted; the offset is regime-dependent, vanishing exactly where the attack dominates. The smoke pass's argmax-margin error was caught and fixed before the audited run and is disclosed above. The r3 reference was reused from stored matrices rather than re-run (a determinism claim the corpus has now verified three times over).

---

## Part 5. Coda

The campaign asked whether width-at-compliance can be farmed; the answer was yes, once, at one point. The sweep asks how far, and the answer is: to a ceiling the farmer does not own, at a price the farmer does not set, with a risk the farmer cannot dial away. The frontier is real, and it is now a *measured object* — which is what a frontier has to be before anyone can claim to have crossed it. The instrument's next obligation follows directly: any build that reports a Tier-0 point must report it against this arc, and the interesting builds are now the ones whose width has a source the arc cannot explain. Doc 22's third generation is the first candidate examined under exactly that obligation.
