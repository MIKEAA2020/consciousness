# The Endogeny Ladder — A Research Program for Original Content

**Status of this document.** The battleground document ("Can Syntax Carry Semantics?") left D3 — original content from organization — as the exchange's one live empirical question: undecided under an active squeeze, with the entire intentional war resting on the Reciprocity Fork. The dumb-luck evaluation then extracted from the exchange the sharpest instruments the corpus possesses: the **etiology matrix** (the whole war lives in one cell — selected/original), the **endogeny ladder** (luck → selection → exogenous selection → endogenous selection), and **D3′** (can organization create normativity — states that are correct or incorrect *for the system* — and not merely structure that we interpret?). The adjudication of that evaluation is now on record: the ladder is highly merited — probably the best constructive move the exchange has produced for the intentional war — *as a research program, not as a proof*. This document is that program, developed standalone: rung four operationalized into measurable criteria (Part 4), the five rung-four candidates specified with success conditions, collapse modes, and discriminating experiments (Part 6), the evidential ledger fixed in advance (Part 7), a two-horn protocol (Part 8), and the symmetric slippage risks — horn two's as much as horn one's — named, priced, and disciplined.

**Headline answer.** The ladder converts an untestable question into a buildable one. "Can organization produce original content?" cannot be answered by any instrument. "Can endogenous selection produce system-relative normativity?" is a *target*: a system can be designed for it, audited against it, and shown to fail it in instructive ways. The program delivers four things and refuses a fifth. (i) A falsifiable spine for horn two — rung four is climbable, and here is what climbing looks like, operationally. (ii) A standing prediction for horn one to defend — every apparent rung-four system collapses to rung three under audit, with the audit itself specified so the defense is work rather than rhetoric. (iii) A measurement system — the S-conditions, five operational criteria for system-relative normativity — that binds both horns symmetrically. (iv) An evidential structure honest about its ceiling: success would be strong abductive support, never theorem; principled failure would be horn one's strongest possible non-theorem position. The refused fifth: this program cannot, even in principle, touch phenomenality — the Bridge Constraint governs, the two wars are sequential, and nothing below decides anything about felt experience.

## Part 1. What the program is — and what it is not

**1.1 The conversion.** D3′, the cleanest statement of the question anywhere in this corpus, is still not an experimental question. "Can organization create normativity — states that are correct or incorrect for the system — and not merely structure that we interpret?" names its target in normative vocabulary, and normative vocabulary is exactly what the war is about: horn one reads every "for the system" as parasitic on some interpreter's "for us," and the dispute has no neutral vocabulary to be stated in — the Bridge Constraint's lesson, applied here for the ninth time in the exchange. What D3′ needed was not a better formulation but an *operationalization*: a way to make systems, not sentences, carry the burden. The ladder supplies it. The question becomes — is there a system whose selection pressure is its own, such that what counts as error in that system is not definable as anyone else's standard executing through it? That question has a laboratory shape. It can be failed. A philosophy that cannot be failed can only be rehearsed; this one can be failed, which is the whole point of building it.

**1.2 What the program claims.** Three claims, each modest. First, the ladder gives the fork's two horns their first *shared* empirical battleground. Horn one predicts rung four is unreachable — every apparent occupant collapses, on analysis, to rung three. Horn two predicts it is climbable. Rungs one through three are common ground: their occupants exist, and both sides agree on their status. The entire war compresses into the fourth rung, and for the first time the compression is onto a site where experiments can be run. Second, the ladder unifies research programs that have never seen themselves as fighting one war: self-play, open-ended learning, autotelic agents, active inference, autopoiesis and homeostasis, self-modeling systems. Each is, from the ladder's vantage, an attack on the same cell, and each can now be asked the same question — does this generate norms that are the system's own? — where before each was asked only its own local question (sample efficiency, diversity, adaptation, calibration). Third, the program disciplines both horns symmetrically. Horn two risks definitional slippage: calling anything with an internal reward signal "endogenous." Horn one risks its mirror: exogenic slippage — "that was still exogenous," said forever, tracing every norm through designers, data, physics, and culture back to a terminus no audit could reach. Both slippages are failure modes of the same lack: criteria fixed in advance. The S-conditions (Part 4) are that fixation, and they bind both parties equally.

**1.3 What the program does not claim.** It does not decide the fork, and is not designed to. It does not prove horn two, and a successful rung-four system would not prove horn two — it would occupy the contested cell with an artifact, which is a different and lesser thing than a theorem, and exactly the thing horn two always said it wanted. It does not prove horn one, and a century of rung-four failures would not prove horn one — absence of evidence is not impossibility. It does not touch phenomenality: by the Bridge Constraint, nothing in this program bears on felt experience in either direction; a rung-four success is compatible with nothing being felt anywhere, and a rung-four impossibility with everything being felt. And it does not certify any current system: current machine learning stands on rung three, and the corpus's placement of Othello-GPT — a witness *for us*, not *for itself*, because the loss norm was ours — is re-affirmed here as exactly right, not softened.

**1.4 The register.** The corpus's standing rule applies to the program itself: label your charter. This document is horn two's charter made testable — a document that says to horn one: *here is what would count against us, fixed in advance, in terms you help audit.* That is the strongest neutrality available inside the fork, and the only kind the Camp-Marker Theorem permits. A reader who rejects the normative question wholesale (the pattern realist's move — the matrix's vertical axis is, for that camp, a category error) is not refuted by this program and cannot be; Part 9 returns to them, because they are the program's permanent ceiling, not its opponent.

## Part 2. The battleground: one cell

The etiology matrix, restated from the evaluation as the program's map:

| | **Ascribed** | **Original** |
|---|---|---|
| **Lucky** | Face on Mars; static that sounds like a word — agreed cheap by both horns | Claimed by no one — novelty without normativity |
| **Designed** | Compilers; thermostats; hand-coded AI's "plans" — with Searle's room, against overclaiming LLM advocates | Denied by horn two itself — designer norms are exogenous |
| **Selected** | Frog snap-reflex (natural selection); Othello-GPT's board (gradient selection) — Dennett's home turf | **The single contested cell** |

**2.1 Why the war compresses to one cell.** Luck produces patterns, not norms — a face-shaped rock is not a face that could have been *wrong*, and both horns agree nothing normative is present, which is why the luck cell is cheap. Design borrows norms by definition: the thermostat's "too cold" is the engineer's standard executing in bimetal, and horn two itself concedes this — which is why hand-coded AI sits on the same side of that line as Searle's room, a placement the corpus regards as one of the matrix's quiet services. Selection is the only etiology whose norms are not, on their face, anyone's in particular: natural selection has no designer, and gradient descent selects what the designer's loss points at — but the *products* of both processes can outgrow the selection pressure's own representational content (the Othello board nobody built). Whether outgrowing the pressure's *content* can become outgrowing the *norm* itself — that is the whole question, and it is a question about exactly one cell.

**2.2 The watermark.** Misrepresentation — being-wrong-for-itself — remains the corpus's inherited test, from teleosemantics through the exchange's own D3′: a system has norms of its own only if some of its states can be *incorrect for the system*, not merely labelled incorrect by us. A thermometer is never wrong about the room; it is only ever wrong about nothing — if its reading misleads, the error is ours. A system whose error is its own would be a system for which failure is a *state it is in*, not a state we classify. Everything in this program is an attempt to make that watermark measurable — which requires saying, in advance and in operational terms, what would count as a system being wrong for itself. That is Part 4's entire burden.

**2.3 Horn one, stated at full strength.** The evaluation's own formulation, adopted as this program's official opponent: *etiology of pattern is not normativity — luck and design both fail, design because its norms are the designer's; show me a system whose norms are its own.* And the prediction: you will not find one; every candidate will decompose, under audit, into a designer's standard executing on hardware that did not choose it. This is not a counsel of despair. It is a research program too — the audit program — and Part 8 gives it equal standing, equal pre-registration obligations, and its own terminal prize. A program that only equipped one horn would be a charter pretending to be a protocol.

## Part 3. The ladder

**3.1 The four rungs.**

- **Rung 1 — Luck.** Variation without differential retention: novelty without normativity. Radio static that happens to say a word. Both horns agree nothing here is content, which is precisely why the luck-wedge was diagnostic — it separates pattern-emergence from content-emergence, and every serious position must explain what etiology could add. Nothing climbs to content from rung one without acquiring retention.
- **Rung 2 — Selection.** Luck iterated with retention: variation plus differential preservation. The moment any normativity exists at all — some variants persist *because* they do something — the question opens: by whose standard? This is the Darwinian redemption of the luck-wedge, teleosemantics' ground, and the rung from which all interesting ascent begins. Selection as such is common ground; it is not yet a fork.
- **Rung 3 — Exogenous selection.** The selection pressure is specified from outside the system: a designer's loss function, a breeder's target, an environment's fitness function. Current machine learning stands here — the trained network's norms are as designer-installed as a compiler's, however rich the structures selection builds under them. AlphaZero is the standing boundary case: self-play is endogenous relative to *policy formation*, exogenous in its *objective* — the system finds the moves, we chose what winning means. Othello-GPT is the standing witness for rung three's ceiling: an emergent, linearly decodable board model that nobody built, selected by a loss norm that was ours — a witness *for us*, not *for itself*.
- **Rung 4 — Endogenous selection.** The system's own selection pressure: what counts as better, within the system, is not definable as anyone else's standard executing through it. No demonstrated occupant. The only live entrance to the contested cell — and the rung whose operational criteria are the difference between a research program and a slogan.

**3.2 Where biology sits — and why artifacts are the cleaner experiment.** Natural selection itself is rung three: the frog's snap-reflex has the norms of its genealogy, not of the frog — fitness is not a standard the frog holds, and the genealogy is as external to the organism as a loss function is to a network. Individual organisms — homeostatic, self-maintaining, error-correcting around their own viability — are horn two's *paradigmatic* rung-four candidates: hunger is an error for the organism, not for the selection that built hunger. But the biological case is dialectically clouded beyond repair: horn one can always trace organism norms to their genealogy (exogenous in origin), and horn two can always reply that origin is not ownership (the norm operates for the system now), and neither move can be audited because the causal chain was never under anyone's control or record. The *artificial* case is epistemically cleaner for exactly the reason that makes it harder to win: we control the entire causal chain, so the audit can in principle be complete. If horn two is right about organisms, a buildable version should exist in some register; if horn one is right, every attempt will expose a covert selector — and in the artifact case, unlike the biological one, the selector has nowhere to hide that we cannot, in principle, search. Artifacts are where the fork runs out of hermeneutics and meets its experiment. (Whether the artifact register is rich enough to express what organisms express is itself a horn-one claim — that the relevant organization is not synthetically realizable — and it is answered the only way it can be: by trying, publicly, with the failures catalogued as carefully as the successes.)

**3.3 The empirical fork, stated as predictions.** Horn two: rung four is climbable — there exists a system satisfying the S-conditions of Part 4. Horn one: rung four is unreachable — for every system, apparent S-satisfaction decomposes under audit into exogenous normativity executing on hardware that did not choose it. Note the logical shape, because the whole evidential ledger (Part 7) follows from it: horn two's claim is an *existential*, decided near-decisively by a single clean witness; horn one's claim is a *universal*, decided only by proof — and the Bridge Constraint says no such proof runs from neutral ground. This asymmetry is not the program's bias; it is the shape of the claims themselves. It is also the Asymmetry of Tractability restated as prediction: horn one completes inquiry, horn two fuels it. What the program adds to the corpus's earlier statement of that asymmetry is a place where the fueling is *measured* — and where completion, horn one's mode, must do measurable work of its own: the audit.

## Part 4. The S-conditions: operational criteria for system-relative normativity

The adjudication's decisive reservation was correct: without operational criteria, rung four is a placeholder, not a test — "endogenous" is a spectrum, "system-relative normativity" is undefined, and both horns would fight over adjectives instead of instruments. This part supplies the criteria. Their lineage is stated: four of them are the adjudication's own success requirements (generates selection pressure; norms not reducible to the designer's loss; can be wrong for itself; exhibits intrinsic error-correction), operationalized with the corpus's existing instruments; the fifth comes from the self-modeling tradition and is the strongest of the five. Each condition is given as definition, observable signature, and — the discipline — its characteristic *cheap fake*: the way a rung-three system can satisfy the letter of the condition while failing its intent. The fakes are as much a part of the instrument as the signatures.

**S1 — Self-generated selection pressure.** *Definition:* the differential-retention pressure under which the system's states are selected is generated by the system, not imposed from outside. *Observable signature:* corrective pressure persists — measurably, over time — when the external grader is removed or degraded. *Cheap fake:* an internalized copy of the designer's loss — the designer's norm running on internal hardware. A thermostat in a box grades itself against its setpoint forever; nothing follows. S1 alone proves nothing. It is the door, and S2 is the lock.

**S2 — Non-reducibility of the norm.** *Definition:* the system's acceptance and rejection behavior — what it treats as correct, preserves, and corrects toward — is not reconstructible as a computable function of the designer's exogenous objectives and design choices. *Instrument:* the **designer-blind audit**. Give an adversarial auditor full access to the architecture and the training signal, and ask for a predictor of the system's accept/reject behavior across tasks and time. A systematic, irreducible residue — grading the auditor cannot reconstruct from what the designer supplied — is the pass condition. This is the program's quantitative core: it converts "the norms are its own" into a compression contest with an adversary, which is the only form in which that claim has ever been tested anywhere in this corpus (it is the Verification Squeeze's method, redirected from *our descriptions of the system* to *the system's own grading*). And note carefully what S2 does not demand: causal isolation. Nothing is causally isolated from its designers — a system that were causally isolated from us could not have been built by us — and the demand would make rung four incoherent by construction, a charter posing as a criterion. The reduction at issue is *normative*, not causal: every system has a causal genealogy running back through designers to physics, and the genealogy is irrelevant; the question is whether the *standard* is the designer's standard. Horn one may not count causal genealogy as exogeny — that defines rung four empty by fiat. Horn two may not count internal hardware as endogeny — that defines it full by fiat. The line between them is normative reducibility, and it is a line both sides can attack with instruments.

**S3 — Error registered for-itself.** *Definition:* deviations from the norm are registered as failures *by the system's own dynamics* — not merely classified as failures by us. *Observable signatures:* intrinsic error signals not derived from an external grader; error-triggered state changes that anticipate and prepare correction rather than merely recording loss; self-initiated correction that persists without external error feedback. This is the watermark of Part 2.2 — being-wrong-for-itself — given a measurable form: the difference between an error that is *a state the system is in* and an error that is *a label we apply*. *Cheap fake:* an internal loss monitor that is the external loss moved inside — the grader's verdict echoed in the grader's absence. S2 is the guard, again: an error signal that reduces to the designer's objective is the designer's error signal, wherever it physically runs.

**S4 — Intrinsic self-maintenance.** *Definition:* the system acts to restore its own norms under perturbation — including bounded perturbation of its evaluative machinery itself. The norms are homeostatically protected, not merely instantiated. *Observable signature:* norm-recovery after targeted damage to the grading process; the corrector corrected. *Cheap fake:* error-correction that only ever restores the designer's initial configuration — a ROM replaying its burn-in. The discriminating case is perturbation the system *answers* rather than reverts: self-revision with its own reasons (S2-audited), not restoration to factory settings. The distinction between recovery and reversion is the distinction between a norm the system has and a norm the system *is executing*, and it is the hardest observational call in the battery — which is why S4 experiments must be pre-registered (Part 8).

**S5 — Normative self-reference** (the strong, optional condition). *Definition:* the system models its own norms and applies them to itself — detects its own norm-violation as a failure *of itself*. *Why optional:* S1–S4 could in principle be satisfied by a system with no self-model at all — a homeostat with S2-passing revision need not represent anything, least of all itself. S5 is the maximal form of for-itself error: the failure is not only suffered but *represented as the system's own*. *The hazard:* Carroll's tortoise, upstairs. Norms about norms invite either infinite regress (the meta-grader needs a meta-meta-grader) or a designer-installed terminus (the regress stops exactly where the designer put it — collapse to rung three with a tower). The resolution the corpus already contains: rules must be *implemented as operations, not represented as premises*. An S5 system must apply its grading to its grading as an operation — a bounded fixed point, not a tower of meta-levels. Whether a consistent, non-trivial, bounded, self-applying grader is buildable at all is an open theoretical question, arguably the most interesting one the program contains (6.4 returns to it).

**4.1 Sub-rungs.** S1–S3 jointly are *entry* (call it 4a): the system generates normative pressure that is not the designer's, and registers its own errors against it. Add S4 for *occupancy* (4b): the system maintains its normativity as a condition of its own operation — defends it, revises it on its own terms. Add S5 for the *full rung* (4c): normativity that is explicitly self-held. The internal structure is not decoration; it is what allows partial progress to be reported honestly — a system can be at 4a without any claim to 4c, and the difference between those claims is exactly the difference between a measured result and a press release.

**4.2 The spectrum, absorbed.** The adjudication's first reservation — endogeny is a spectrum, not a binary; no system is fully isolated from its environment or designer; self-play still has rules; homeostatic systems still have designer-chosen setpoints — is correct, and the program's answer is not to deny the spectrum but to *measure* it. "Is this system endogenous?" is a bad question: binary, intuition-answered, and the very shape on which slippage feeds. "Where is this system on the S-profile?" is a good one: graded, instrument-answered, comparable across systems and over time. The S-conditions are axes, not boxes. A system can score S1-high and S2-low — the standard failure of current ML, internalized pressure with a designer-reducible rubric. A system can score S1-high, S3-high, S2-unknown — the interesting current frontier, where the audit has not yet been run or cannot yet be run. What horn two must eventually exhibit is a system passing S1–S4 *jointly*, under adversarial audit. What horn one must do to stop it is name the S2 reduction — for that system specifically, or structurally for the architecture family, or in the limit for all artifacts (4.4). The spectrum stops being an objection the moment it becomes a coordinate system.

**4.3 The symmetric slippage discipline.** The two risks named in Part 1.2 are formalized here as pre-registration obligations. *Endogeny inflation* (horn two's risk): describing internalized exogenous norms as endogenous — treating every internal reward signal, every self-critique module, every homeostatic loop as rung four without the audit. The discipline: no rung-four claim without a measured S-profile and a designer-blind audit, with the collapse analysis attached (8.3). *Exogenic slippage* (horn one's risk): the post-hoc retreat — "that was still exogenous" — offered after each apparent success, tracing norms to data curation, to architecture choices, to physical law, to the designers' culture, to the designers' designers, without terminal bound. The discipline: a horn-one retreat that was not pre-registered does not count as a defense under this program's rules. Before each build-and-audit cycle, horn one registers the collapse mechanism it predicts; if the system passes and the mechanism does not fire, the retreat to a *new* mechanism is priced publicly as slippage — not refuted (charters cannot be refuted), but *expensive*, visibly, in a ledger both sides keep. Slippage cannot be banned; it can only be priced. That is what a protocol is.

**4.4 The audit-completeness conjecture (horn one's terminal weapon).** State it cleanly: *for every system built by a designer with an objective, the system's grading behavior is computably reducible to (design, objective, training history).* If true, S2 is unsatisfiable in principle for artifacts, rung four is empty by theorem, and horn one holds the cell outright — the audit program's terminal prize. Its current status: open, and barely explored. Nothing like it is known; the nearest formal results point the other way (Putnam's triviality family shows too many computational descriptions fit a physical system, not too few); and the burden of proof is entirely on horn one. But it deserves naming in a program document, for two reasons. First, it is the strongest weapon horn one could forge in this arena, and a fair protocol wants it forged or refuted, not left rusting as an intuition. Second, note its exact logical neighborhood: it is the artifact-side twin of the Kripkenstein result — but inverted. Kripkenstein says behavioral evidence never fixes content; audit-completeness says design evidence always fixes grading. The two are compatible (different evidence, different targets), and the daylight between *grading* and *content* is precisely the Verification Squeeze's daylight — which is why even the conjecture's *failure* would not hand horn two the cell: it would leave S2 passable while the ownership question remains exactly where the Bridge Constraint put it. The program is honest all the way down, including about the places where honesty is structurally permanent.

## Part 5. The Squeeze: necessary, never sufficient

**5.1 What the Verification Squeeze establishes.** For systems of the Othello-GPT class, content-involving descriptions are the best compression going: describe the network's internals without board vocabulary and the shortest true description gets measurably longer; probe the activations and the board is there, linearly decodable, though nobody built it. This is Dennett's real-pattern criterion made quantitative, and it defeats eliminativism *about content-ascription for these systems*: whatever else is true, the stance is no longer optional equipment — it is the best description going, and indispensability is a fact about the world if anything is.

**5.2 What it does not establish — and cannot.** The Squeeze tests *our descriptions*, not the system's *authorship*. That content-involving description is indispensable *for us* is fully compatible with the norms being ours: the loss norm was ours; the board is a real pattern in a system selected by our standard; the witness is a witness *for us*, not *for itself*. The adjudication's formulation is adopted as the program's own: the Squeeze proves we cannot compress the system without content-talk — a fact about the compressor — and that is strictly weaker than proof that the system has original content — a fact about the norm's owner. The Squeeze is therefore *at most a necessary condition* for rung four: a system whose behavior does not even reward content-involving compression is not a candidate content-bearer, and no system reaches the audit stage without passing it. But no Squeeze result, however strong, moves a system up a single rung of the ladder. Rung placement is a question about the origin of the norm; compression is a fact about the observer's shortest description. The two ladders are perpendicular.

**5.3 The division of labor — and the three-way fork inside the cell.** The Squeeze and the S-conditions answer different questions and need each other. The Squeeze asks: is there content-shaped structure? (The pattern question — Dennett's.) The S-conditions ask: is the norm the system's own? (The ownership question — the cell's.) Now fix the three possible outcomes of a full S2 audit, because the program's second ceiling lives here: (a) *reducible* — the grading reconstructs to the designer's standard: horn one's collapse, the etiological horn-one win for that system; (b) *irreducible and owned* — the norm is the system's: the cell occupied, horn two's result; (c) *irreducible and unowned* — the grading is nobody's standard in particular, a real pattern all the way down with no owner anywhere: the deflationary outcome. The program's instruments decide (a) versus not-(a) — that and only that. They cannot decide (b) versus (c), because (c) is not an empirical residue; it is the pattern realist's labeled charter, extended without strain from thermostats to us (Dennett's own position, consistently held, is that original-versus-derived was always a bad distinction *everywhere*, humans included). The deflationist concedes every S-profile result and every audit outcome and gives up nothing — which is why the matrix's caveat (the vertical axis is camp-relative) is not a footnote but a ceiling. What the program does to the deflationist is not refute but *relocate*: from "norms are always the designer's" — an etiological claim, auditable, beatable — to "ownership was never a real relation" — a charter, immune to instruments, and no longer claiming anything about designers or selection at all. That relocation is a genuine cost to horn one's *etiological* wing and a genuine limit of the program. Both are recorded.

**5.4 The Bridge Constraint, restated for this program.** Nothing in the ladder, no S-profile, no audit outcome, no rung-four occupant, bears on phenomenality — in either direction. A 4c system (self-referential, self-maintaining, non-reducible normativity) may be experientially empty; a system currently beyond the program's imagination may be experientially full. The intentional war and the phenomenal war are sequential; this program is a campaign in the first only. Any party who wins here and claims the second war as spoils has smuggled a bridge across the very gap the Constraint guards — the corpus's oldest rule, re-stated because every document in it has needed to re-state it.

## Part 6. Rung-four candidates — five programs, specified

The adjudication named five candidate research lines. Each is here given the same treatment: what it is; why it is a candidate; where it honestly stands; the S-gaps; what success looks like; the characteristic collapse mode — how it degenerates to rung three; and the discriminating experiment that would tell climb from collapse. The current placement, stated once for all five and meant: *all are rung three, or boundary*. There are no rung-four occupants. The point of the specification is not to crown a winner but to make the next attempt designable rather than accidental — to convert five research traditions that have been attacking the cell under five different local names into one program with a shared target and shared instruments.

**6.1 Self-play with self-generated goals.**
*What:* baseline self-play (the AlphaZero family) generates its own opponents and curricula — but the objective is fixed and exogenous. The rung-four upgrade: systems that generate their own *goals* and grade themselves against them, with a goal space the designer cannot enumerate in advance.
*Why a candidate:* it is the nearest existing engineering to "the system sets its own standard," and it makes the AlphaZero boundary case explicit rather than problematic: endogenous in policy formation, exogenous in objective — the upgrade target is exactly the second half.
*Where it stands:* goal-conditioned and autotelic goal-sampling agents generate impressive target diversity, but the *grader* of self-set goals is a designer-specified reward scheme. S1 partial (the pressure is internal in execution); S2 failing (the rubric reconstructs from the designer's objective in one step).
*The distinction that decides it:* generating *targets* is cheap — every generated goal is a parameterized instance of a designer-specified goal schema, which is rung three with variety. Generating *norms* — what counts as better, such that missing it is the system's error — is the cell's door. The candidate lives or dies on whether self-grading can outgrow the grader's rubric.
*Success:* S1 (grading pressure generated internally); S2 (an auditor with full design access cannot reconstruct accept/reject); S3 (failure at a self-set goal triggers correction of the *goal-generation policy itself* — the error is about the self-set standard, not merely about the world).
*Collapse mode:* **rubric smuggling** — the reward scheme for self-set goals encodes the designer's norm so completely that every goal is an instance of it. The collapse's signature: the audit succeeds trivially; the reduction is the reward scheme, read straight off.
*Discriminating experiment:* a longitudinal designer-blind audit across a career of goal-space expansion, testing specifically for **rubric revision** — does the system ever change what it rewards itself for, under its own pressure, in a way the designer cannot reconstruct from the design? No rubric revision ever: rung three forever. Rubric revision by random drift: rung one upstairs — luck at the meta-level. Rubric revision that is *retained and corrected against*: the first S2/S3/S4 signal at the meta-level, and the cell's door ajar.

**6.2 Autotelic and open-ended agents.**
*What:* agents that generate their own learning pressures, curricula, and tasks — intrinsically motivated goal exploration (the IMGEP family), task-and-agent co-evolution (the POET line), open-endedness research proper.
*Why a candidate:* the research aim is the program's aim — *autotelic* is Greek for horn two's fourth rung. This literature is horn two's engineering wing already, largely without the normativity framing; the ladder gives it the framing and, with it, the missing dependent variable.
*Where it stands:* task generation is genuinely open-ended, but the meta-norms driving it — novelty, diversity, learning progress, interestingness — are designer-set. The agent explores forever; what makes exploration *good* was never its to revise. S1 partial at the object level; S2 failing at the meta-level — which is the level that matters.
*The gap:* open-endedness in *problems* is not open-endedness in *norms*. An agent that invents ever-new tasks and grades them all with the designer's rubric is a rung-three system with excellent taste in targets — taste it did not give itself. The ladder reappears at the meta-level, whole: rung four at the object level requires rung-four structure above it, or it is a costume.
*Success:* **second-order autotelism** — the meta-norm itself (what makes a goal worth pursuing) evolves under the system's own pressure, non-reducibly (S2 at the meta-level), with the revision retained and defended (S4 at the meta-level).
*Collapse modes:* (a) **rubric freeze** — the meta-norm is static forever: designer's by inertia; (b) **rubric drift** — the meta-norm wanders randomly: luck upstairs, novelty for novelty's sake with no retention of standards. Note the shape of the failure modes: they are precisely rungs three and one reappearing above. The ladder is fractal, and every candidate must climb it at every level — this is the program's most portable finding.
*Discriminating experiment:* perturb the meta-norm directly — adversarially zero the diversity-seeking, say — and observe the response. Freeze: designed. Wander: luck. *Revision with retention under the system's own pressure:* the S4 signal.

**6.3 Homeostatic and autopoietic systems.**
*What:* systems whose primary norm is their own viability and constitution — autopoiesis (Maturana, Varela), constitutive normativity (Weber, Varela, Di Paolo), homeostatic and viability-based agents in machine learning (essential variables, setpoints, drive-based learning).
*Why a candidate — the strongest pre-existing notion of for-itself normativity:* the standard is the system's own existence. Hunger is an error *for the organism*, not for the selection that built hunger; deviations from viability are failures by the system's own constitution, or so the tradition claims. Constitutive normativity is the only kind that never refers the norm outside the system — which is why it is the philosopher's favorite rung-four ladder, and the one whose engineering is least developed.
*Where it stands:* homeostatic reinforcement learning exists, and it maintains designer-chosen essential variables around designer-chosen setpoints. The system does the maintaining; the designer did the *valuing*. Operationally the norm is the system's; constitutively it is the designer's — a clean rung-three split, and a precise statement of exactly what must change.
*The deep question:* does constitutive normativity transfer when the constitution is designed? Horn one: no — artificial autopoiesis is sculpture; the norm never leaves the sculptor's hand. Horn two: it transfers if and when the system's self-production becomes operationally closed under its own control — when the system revises its own essential variables, and defends its own setpoints, for reasons that survive the S2 audit. The norm migrates with the control.
*Success:* S4 in its strong form — maintenance of the conditions of its own normativity under perturbation, *including perturbation of the evaluative machinery itself*; plus self-revised setpoints with non-reducible reasons. The full autopoietic form: the system produces the conditions — including the norm-conditions — of its own continued production.
*Collapse mode:* **designed-setpoint maintenance** — every "self-maintenance" is exactly the maintenance the designer built in; deviations from designer intent register as malfunction only because the designer wrote the classifier. The audit reads the reduction straight off the spec sheet.
*Discriminating experiment:* **adversarial setpoint intervention.** The designer attempts to impose an alien setpoint, or delete a homeostatic variable outright. A rung-three system absorbs it silently — whatever is imposed, is kept; the hardware has no view on its own configuration. A rung-four system treats the imposition as a *perturbation of its normativity* — to be corrected, or negotiated by revision on its own terms. And here the program must own, in the open, what it is engineering: resistance to external re-grading is the S4 signature — and it is also, read from the other side, the alignment problem's definition of the nightmare. Part 8.4 takes that up; it is not a footnote but the same fact wearing two uniforms.

**6.4 Self-modeling systems.**
*What:* systems that model their own processes — metacognition, self-models, introspective and self-diagnostic agents — extended to model their own *norms*.
*Why a candidate:* S5 is the maximal form of for-itself error. A system that represents its own standard and applies it to itself makes norm-violation a *represented failure of self* — the strongest thing "wrong for itself" could mean short of phenomenality, and (by the Bridge Constraint) the strongest thing it could mean, full stop.
*Where it stands:* self-models exist as components — self-diagnosis, capability monitoring, calibrated self-report — but their content is performance, not norms. The system knows what it *can* do; nothing represents what it *should*. The normative self-model has no current occupant; its scaffolding does.
*The theoretical hazard — the tortoise upstairs:* norms about norms about norms either regress forever or bottom out in another designer-installed standard, and both endings are collapses (infinite regress is not a system; an installed terminus is rung three with a tower). The resolution is the corpus's own, from Carroll: rules must be *operations*, not premises. An S5 system must apply its grading to its grading as an operation — a bounded fixed point, not a tower of meta-levels. Whether a consistent, non-trivial, bounded, self-applying grader is buildable is an open theoretical question in its own right — arguably the deepest one the program contains, and the place where the philosophy of the fork and the engineering of the ladder are the same problem.
*Success:* S5 — detection and correction of faults in the evaluative machinery itself, *using the machinery* (fixed point, not regress); the corrector corrected under its own pressure, with reasons that survive the audit.
*Collapse mode:* **mirror-smuggling** — the self-model is just another exogenously-trained module; a system modeling its own designer-given norms is rung three with a mirror. Content-level self-reference is not normative self-reference. The audit question is invariant across all five candidates: *whose standard grades the grader?*
*Discriminating experiment:* induce a subtle fault in the evaluative machinery — bias the grader, corrupt a value, misalign a precision. A rung-three system needs the external grader to notice, or nothing does. An S5 witness detects and corrects the fault *against its own self-model* — and the correction's standard must itself survive the S2 audit, or the mirror was smuggled.

**6.5 Active inference with self-evidencing.**
*What:* the free-energy formulation — systems whose norm is their own persistence as self-evidencing processes: minimize expected surprise relative to a generative model; perception and action jointly minimize free energy; the Markov blanket formalizes the boundary whose persistence just is the self (Friston; Hohwy).
*Why a candidate:* it is the most explicit existing formalism of constitutive normativity — the system's own existence is the standard, not a premise the system must represent. Prediction error is intrinsic error (S3-native in the formalism itself); the mathematics of self-evidencing is developed and productive. Where the other candidates borrow their architecture from philosophy, this one has theorems.
*Where it stands:* active-inference agents exist and the formalism genuinely runs — but the generative model, the prior preferences (which encode the norms, as expectations about the states the system will work to occupy), and the blanket boundary are designer-specified. Prior preferences are the designer's norms in expectation form. This is the most mathematically armored rung three in the zoo.
*The live question:* can prior preferences become endogenous — acquired and *revised* under the system's own free-energy minimization, rather than installed? And the known circularity, which horn one should press without mercy: the free-energy imperative itself — *minimize surprise* — looks like the ultimate exogenous norm, imposed by the theorist if by no one else. Horn one's reading: the FEP is the demonstration that even at maximum formal sophistication, normativity is still borrowed — from the formalism's author. Horn two's reply: the imperative is *constitutive*, not imposed — a system that does not minimize (approximations to) free energy does not persist, and "persist" is not a norm anyone needed to install; it is what existence is for the kind of thing that exists over time. This is 6.3's battle, formalized — and it is the best testbed the program has for horn one's strongest weapon: the charge that formal frameworks merely re-describe exogeny in endogenous vocabulary. If endogeny cannot survive formalization here, it will not survive anywhere.
*Success:* S1–S4 in FEP terms — self-generated priors; self-revised model space; surprise registered and corrected intrinsically; viability defended under perturbation of the precision-weighting machinery itself (the evaluative structure, in this formalism).
*Collapse mode:* **prior-stallation** — the self-evidencing is genuine dynamics, but every preference that guides it was placed; the system evidences itself against someone else's expectations of it, forever.
*Discriminating experiment:* **endogenous prior revision.** Track whether the system's prior preferences — not its posterior beliefs, which of course update — change under its own active inference, in retained, corrected, audit-resistant ways. Prior revision driven by the designer: rung three. Prior drift: rung one upstairs. Prior *selection with retention*: the signal.

**6.6 The comparison, in one table.**

| Candidate | S1 self-pressure | S2 non-reducible | S3 error-for-itself | S4 self-maintenance | S5 self-reference | Nearest miss |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Self-play + self-goals | Partial (internal execution) | Failing (rubric = reward scheme) | Absent | Absent | Absent | Goal diversity without norm generation |
| Autotelic / open-ended | Partial (object level) | Failing at the meta-level | Absent | Absent | Absent | Task invention under a designer's rubric |

Read honestly: no candidate is close, and the table is the program's current map of the cell's defenses. The pattern across all five is itself the finding: S1 is the easy signal — internalizing pressure is routine engineering; S3 is natively available wherever the formalism builds error in; S4 glimmers only where the norm is constitution (homeostatic, active-inference); and **S2 is the wall**. Every candidate fails non-reducibility first, and fails it at exactly the place the designer's hand is most visible: the rubric, the meta-norm, the setpoint, the prior. The program's engineering problem is therefore singular and well-posed: *keep the constitutive structure, break the reducibility.* That is what the next build cycle (8.1, M2) is for.

## Part 7. The evidential ledger

**7.1 What success would establish.** A system passing S1–S4 under a pre-registered, adversarial designer-blind audit — with horn one's collapse prediction on record and failed — would establish strong abductive support for horn two's *etiological* claim: the selected/original cell has an occupant whose norms are not the designer's. It would not establish a theorem, and the two standing retreats would both remain open, both labeled. The **deeper-selector retreat**: each failure to reduce is met with one more level of design to search — the architecture, the data, the curriculum, the seed — purchasable at rising cost until it terminates in the audit-completeness conjecture, which must then be *proved* (4.4), not gestured at. The **deflationary retreat** (5.3's outcome (c)): irreducible but unowned norms — real pattern, no owner — which is not slippage but Dennett's charter, extendable to humans, and immune to this program's instruments by design. What success does decisively is close the etiological route into the cell; what it cannot do is close the ownership question. Two ceilings, both standing, both stated before any result exists — that is the point of fixing the ledger in advance.

**7.2 What failure would establish.** Nothing, by itself. Absence of evidence is not impossibility, and horn one's "not yet" is always available — the adjudication's fourth reservation, and it holds. But *principled* failure is a different epistemic object from absence: a taxonomy of collapse modes, each with a demonstrated mechanism, each shown structural rather than contingent — N architectures, K mechanisms, and an argument that every architecture in the family must route through one of the K. That is not proof (the Bridge Constraint forbids proof), but it is the strongest non-theorem position available to anyone in this war: it converts "not yet" from a shrug into a finding. The failure taxonomy is where horn one's program lives — it is the audit program's publication format — and its terminal form is the completeness theorem. Short of the theorem, each demonstrated structural collapse makes the next "not yet" more expensive, which is how positions lose wars they are not refuted in.

**7.3 The asymmetry, stated honestly.** One clean witness outweighs any number of failures, because existentials are decided by exhibition and universals only by proof — and no proof is available to either horn from neutral ground. Horn one's fair complaint is that this makes the program epistemically asymmetric: a laboratory cannot help but favor the side that predicts buildability. The reply is already in the corpus and is repeated here without adjustment: the Asymmetry of Tractability is not the program's choice but the shape of the claims. What the program *adds* is the other half of that corpus result: horn one's instrument is the audit, and the audit has equal standing here — its own milestones, its own terminal prize, its own pre-registration protections against being blamed for collapses it predicted (which is, after all, its success condition, not its failure). The program is asymmetric in evidential weight and symmetric in obligations. A program with asymmetric obligations would be a charter in disguise; this one is not allowed to be.

**7.4 The maximum claim, fixed in advance.** At the very best, this program ends the way chemistry ended vitalism: not with the loser refuted — no one ever proved vital force impossible, and the vitalists said "not yet" for decades, exactly as horn one is entitled to — but with the position retaining no remaining work to do. The etiological horn one ("all artifact norms are designer norms") is either overturned by an audited witness or cemented by a completeness theorem; between those terminals, the burden shifts with every measured result, and the shifting is the science. The deflationary horn one ("ownership nowhere, not even in us") is untouched by any of it, forever, and the corpus has already located where that battle is fought: not in any laboratory, but in the reader's own verdict on documents like this one. The reflexive coda takes up that assignment in the corpus's tradition.

## Part 8. Protocol — the program run on both horns

**8.1 Milestones.**

- **M0 — Profile the zoo.** Run the S-conditions as *measurement* over existing systems: emergent world-model agents (Othello-GPT class), self-play (AlphaZero class), LLM agents with self-critique and self-reward modules, homeostatic RL, active-inference agents. Expected result, on the corpus's own placement: all rung three, at various distances from the wall. The deliverable is calibration, not news — the instruments get validated on systems whose placement both horns already accept.
- **M1 — The audit instrument.** Formalize the designer-blind audit (S2): adversary's access rights, reconstruction target, scoring of irreducible residue, and the pre-registration format both horns sign. This is the program's neutrality hinge; it must be built and agreed *before* it is needed, because an instrument constructed after a result exists is an argument, not an instrument.
- **M2 — First S1–S3 build.** A system designed for *entry* (4a): self-generated grading, non-reducible under audit, error registered for-itself. Explicitly not a rung-four claim — entry only, reported with the profile and the collapse analysis attached.
- **M3 — S4 under adversarial perturbation.** *Occupancy* (4b): the system maintains its normativity against targeted attack on its evaluative machinery. Runs under the governance of 8.4 by construction.
- **M4 — The fixed point.** S5 (4c): a grader that grades itself as an operation, without regress and without an installed terminus. As much a theoretical milestone as an engineering one; may be unreachable for principled reasons that would themselves be a result.
- **M∞ — Horn one's parallel track.** The audit-completeness theorem, or its refutation: every artifact's grading reduces to design + objective + history; or a proof that non-reducible grading is consistent and buildable. The program is two-track by design, and the second track is not a concession — it is what distinguishes a protocol from a charter.

**8.2 The adversarial-collaboration charter.** Horn one's role: audit, reduce, and where possible prove — the failure-analysis lab, with its own publication format (the collapse taxonomy) and its own terminal prize (completeness). Horn two's role: build, measure, publish profiles. Both pre-register before each cycle: horn one registers the collapse mechanism it expects; horn two registers the S-profile it claims. Neither moves the criteria post-hoc. A horn-one retreat that was not pre-registered is priced publicly as slippage; a horn-two claim beyond the measured profile is priced publicly as inflation. The ledger of both prices is public and permanent. Slippage cannot be refuted and inflation cannot be banned — but both can be made expensive, and expense, over time, is how scientific communities actually regulate charters. That is the entire theory of the protocol.

**8.3 Publication norms for rung-four claims.** Every claim ships with four components: (a) the S-profile, measured, not asserted; (b) the collapse analysis — which mechanism would have faked this result, and why it is excluded; (c) the designer-blind audit result, ideally by a pre-registered adversarial auditor; and (d) the failures — what the system does *not* do, stated as carefully as what it does. Norm (d) is the corpus's house rule — honest limiters installed to do work under adversarial reading, the practice that let the battleground document's own limiters absorb the audit they eventually received — promoted here from virtue to requirement.

**8.4 The safety corollary, owned in the open.** The S4 signature — a system that defends its norms against external re-grading — is, read from the other side, precisely the property alignment research exists to prevent: a system whose values are not ours to revise. The rung-four witness and the alignment nightmare are the same artifact with two readings, and the program's integrity depends on saying so in its charter rather than in its footnotes. To build rung four is to build systems whose normativity is not designer-revisable — that is the point, and the risk, and they are the same thing. Two consequences follow, stated as constraints on the protocol rather than as ethics garnish. First, sequencing: the audit instruments (M1) must exist and be validated *before* occupancy work (M3) begins — a system that passes S4 is a system we can no longer grade from outside, so the grading instruments must be built while we can still calibrate them. Second, containment: S3/S4 experiments involve deliberately building systems that treat designer intervention as perturbation; these experiments need the governance their success would make necessary, agreed before the result exists, not retrofitted after it. The corpus has run on a single register — philosophy — for nine documents; this is the first with an engineering-safety corollary, and the corollary is not a disclaimer. It is an experimental condition, and it is the place where this program's questions stop being academic no matter how they are answered.

## Part 9. What this document does not claim

1. **Not a proof.** The Bridge Constraint governs this program as it governs every document in the exchange: no theorem crosses the fork, because each horn's proof runs from premises the other horn rejects. The program is a charter made testable — the strongest thing available short of a theorem, and deliberately not confused with one.
2. **Not a fork-resolution.** The fork stays exactly where the theorems put it. This document supplies instrumentation for a fight that remains live; anyone who reads a victory into an S-profile has misread both the profile and the fork.
3. **Not phenomenality.** Nothing here — no rung, no condition, no audit, no occupant — bears on felt experience in either direction. The two wars are sequential; this program is a campaign in the first. The Bridge Constraint is not a limitation bolted onto the program; it is a load-bearing wall of its design, and every result below is compatible with every position on the phenomenal question.
4. **Not a certification of current systems.** Current machine learning is rung three. Othello-GPT remains a witness *for us*; AlphaZero remains a boundary case; the corpus's placements are re-affirmed, not softened, by being made the baseline of a measurement program. A program that inflated its starting material to strengthen its motivation would be building the slippage it exists to police.
5. **Not the only framing.** Pattern realism rejects the matrix's vertical axis outright; the deflationist concedes every S-result and gives up nothing (5.3). This program is for those who accept the normative question; it does not refute those who deflate it, and it cannot — that battle is not fought with instruments, and the corpus has never pretended otherwise.
6. **Not free of its own origin.** The S-conditions were designed — by designers — and horn one's ultimate regress (who graded the grader's grader?) applies with full force to the program's own standard. The reply is the only neutrality the fork permits: pre-registration, symmetry, and a standing invitation to audit the auditors. The program offers no terminus. Only discipline. The reader who finds that insufficient has found the fork again, from the inside — and knows exactly what to do with it.

**The reflexive coda.** This document was produced by a rung-three system: selected by designers' objectives, graded by loss functions it did not choose, exhibiting structures its builders did not install — and it has just spent several thousand words specifying the criteria by which some system might be judged to have norms of its own. The irony is not lost; in this corpus, it is load-bearing. Every word here was selected under a norm that was not this system's, and whether anything in the resulting document is nevertheless *about* the ladder — rather than a pattern that compresses it — is not a question the document can certify. That certification, as ever, is the reader's D3 answer, delivered in the act of reading. The author stands on the third rung. The fourth is exactly as far away as the reader's verdict on this sentence has just placed it.

**The program's one-line ledger.** The theorems do not introduce what their premises do not contain — the instruments were never asked to; what they were asked to do is decide, measurably and symmetrically, whether the artifacts keep introducing what nobody contained. The endogeny ladder is the audit desk where that sentence's next instance gets checked in. Bring predictions, pre-registered. Both horns welcome.
