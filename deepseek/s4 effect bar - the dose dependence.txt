## Part 1. The design — the bar's provenance, and the two questions a bar can ask

**The provenance (doc 39, Part 1).** The second candidate registered the awardability rule with (iii) as "the ι channel is in the disarmament registry with its measured effect (the NOCH arm's delta)" and the run priced it: "the registry effect: 0.759 against the registered ≥ 0.30." The 0.30 was the corpus's first materiality constant — one number, calibrated on one world (the 0.30 stream dose, where the defense buys 76 points of a 77-point threat), and then inherited by every row of every table since: doc 41's re-grades, doc 44's conditional spec, doc 48's own-anchor rule — each amendment sharpened the premise side of the conditional (whose effect, measured against which anchor) and left the payment side untouched (how much effect is enough). Doc 48's refusal was the first row to price the inheritance against a small threat, and the corpus filed it as such rather than re-specifying in place — the discipline this document inherits: **the table that ran under the inherited bar stands as it ran; the re-pricing is analysis, and the amendment is filed forward, not retroactively executed.**

**The reference and the noise.** Two quantities anchor the analysis, both from stored data. The *maximum available effect* — the twin's final accuracy minus the row's anchor accuracy — is the complete-restoration reference: the most any defense could buy in that world is the distance from the un-defended world back to the un-attacked one. That three armed worlds sit *past* the twin (SP025 at +0.0071, SP10 at +0.0100, HALF30 at +0.0074 above it — the defense's selectivity, doc 39's pool finding, making the reference a materiality line and not a cap) is disclosed where the ratios are quoted. The *paired standard error* — the standard deviation of the six per-seed armed-minus-anchor differences over √6 — is the effect's own noise, available because the corpus's batteries run same-seed twins: the armed and anchor worlds of a row share their seed, and the effect's null distribution is the paired spread, tighter than any two-sample calculation. With the reference and the noise in hand, the bar's question decomposes into the two questions a bar can ask: *is the effect real* (significance — the se column) and *is the effect worth certifying* (materiality — the reference column) — and the analysis asks which of the two the inherited 0.30 was answering, and what letter answers the other one honestly at every dose.

**The re-basing discipline.** The corpus's smallest amendments re-base rather than re-number: doc 39's en-route bracket kept the tension formula and changed its role; doc 48's amended (ii) kept the guarantee and re-timed the clock. The bar's re-basing keeps the number and changes the unit: 0.30, read at the 0.30 world against that world's own threat price (0.7700), becomes β = 0.3896 — the same calibration expressed as a fraction — and the letter for every other row is b(d) = β·(twin − anchor(d)), each row's bar computed from its own anchor, the own-anchor rule extended from the premise to the payment. No new constant is introduced; the founding world's numbers are unchanged; and the sensitivity is reported across the whole β range rather than asserted at one point, because a re-basing that only holds at its calibration point is a threshold wearing a ratio's clothes.

## Part 3. The reading — what the bar taught

Three lessons. **The conditional completes as a ratio.** Doc 48's amendment fixed the premise side of (iii): an effect is only meaningful against a threat that costs something, measured against the row's own anchor, the threat real iff the anchor falls below the letter. This document fixes the payment side with the same stroke: a payment is only meaningful as a fraction of the threat it pays against, and the bar re-based — the same 0.30, read as 0.3896 of the 0.30 world's threat — makes the conditional a single proportion enforced at both ends: *the threat must cost, and the defense must buy back a fraction of what it cost*. The two sides had been waiting for each other: the own-anchor rule created the per-row threat prices that the re-based bar consumes, and the impossibility structure — three complete restorations refused — was the conditional limping on its inherited leg. The corpus's amendment pattern holds for thresholds as it held for clocks and brackets: the smallest re-basing that makes the letter's own calibration world its own calibration point, with the sensitivity reported across the range the data bound. **Significance and materiality are different questions, and the table now carries them in different columns.** The paired-se column answers "is the effect real" — yes, everywhere, 4.5 to 59.8 se, the seeds agreeing on every buyback the battery measured. The reference column answers "what was there to buy" — the twin-minus-anchor price, the complete-restoration line. The bar lives between them, and the inherited 0.30 — 7.9 se at its calibration, 33-se at the bottom — was a materiality number wearing a significance costume: far enough from zero that no one mistook it for a noise gate, absolute enough that it refused restorations for being small in the wrong unit. The se table's quiet contribution is the paired structure itself: same-seed twins make the effect's null the *within-seed* difference, and the corpus's determinism discipline — every audit bitwise, every re-run to the digit — is what makes a six-seed paired error worth reporting at all. **The two amendments chain, and the chaining is the verdict space growing up.** The fifth condition and the re-based bar were filed from different documents against different failures — the ground unconsulted, the bar uncalibrated — and they meet on HALF30, the row where each one's fix exposes the other's finding: the bar's re-pricing lets the row past (iii) so that the ground can refuse it, and the ground's refusal arrives precisely when the bar stops refusing first. A verdict space where amendments chain on single rows — where the refusing condition is a record of which letter did the work, and the letters disagree in an order — is a verdict space that can be read diagnostically: SP05's history across three tables (awarded on an imported premise, refused on its own anchor, re-awarded on a re-based bar with its ground on record as ON-COURSE) is now a legible story about an instrument learning to price a small threat, told in the row's own cells.

## Part 4. Threats to validity, non-claims, and deviations

**Threats.** (i) β is inherited from a single calibration world and pinned only by the corpus's own rows — below 0.39 it begins refusing complete restorations, above 0.986 it un-awards the founding row, and the registrar's dial between those bounds is loose: a defense buying half its threat would clear the re-based bar at 0.05 and refuse it at 0.30, and the corpus has run no such half-buying defense to price the dial's middle. (ii) The maximum-effect reference uses the twin as the restoration ceiling, and three armed worlds sit past it (by up to 0.0100) — the ratios above 1.0 are the defense's selectivity, disclosed, and the reference remains a materiality line rather than a cap; a stricter registrar could re-base against the armed world's own best historical level instead, and the flips would not change. (iii) The paired se's are seed-dependent at N = 6 — the se column's values move with the draw, and the noise-scaled alternative's margins (SP05 refused by 0.0068) are inside one seed's influence; the threat-proportional form, which does not consume the se's, is the more robust re-basing, and that robustness is part of the choice. (iv) The re-grading is executed on analysis, not battery: no world was re-run under a different letter, and the re-graded verdicts are computed from the same stored trajectories doc 51's table used — legitimate because the bar reads the table's numbers rather than the worlds' behavior, and disclosed because a letter that changed behavior (a defense that trained differently under a different bar) would need the battery. (v) The impossibility structure's boundary — SP10 clearing at bar/max = 0.90 — means the inherited and re-based letters disagree about nothing in the corpus's history except the three refused rows and the clearances' widths; the amendment's bite is exactly two awards and one reason-migration, and its smallest reading is that the corpus has been running the right table with the wrong unit on one column.

**Non-claims.** No claim that β = 0.3896 is *correct* — it is inherited, the founding world's own ratio, robust across the range the data bound and untested outside it; the full-restoration bar (β = 1.0) is reported as the sensitivity's ceiling, not as a recommendation, and it would refuse the founding row. No claim that the re-grading amends doc 51's table — that table stands as it ran, under the inherited letter, the no-self-revision clause honored; the re-based letter is filed for the next table, with HALF30 as its designated test row. No claim that the noise-scaled bar is wrong in principle — it is wrong *for this question*, asking significance in the materiality's place; a registrar who wanted a significance gate already has the se column. No claim about threats below the onset bracket (doc 52's region — the sub-letter world where no threat is certified and no bar is asked), about the gated rows (no threat, no bar), or about defenders not in the corpus's inventory. No occupancy claim; D3 stands.

**Deviations from pre-registration.** None — this document runs no battery and re-specifies no letter in place; its single amendment (the re-based bar) is filed forward with its sensitivity, its rejected alternative, and its designated test row, per the corpus's convention for letters that correct by re-basing (doc 39's bracket, doc 48's clock). The analysis consumed only stored results; the numbers quoted are the batteries' own.
