# The Deference Exclusion — Opening the Admissibility Debate

**Status of this document.** This is the corpus's thirteenth document, and it opens a debate rather than closing one. The Deference Exclusion — the rule that a description counts as norm-stating only if it states a standard the described system could in principle violate — entered the corpus as Component three of the S2′ repair, and it was disclosed there at its exact price: *horn two's wager, stated as an instrument rule.* The stress test's standing instruction anticipated this document's existence: the exclusion's admissibility is contested by horn one's Brandom wing, and a rule that carries a war inside it deserves the debate it was promised, not a label. Two companions make the debate newly concrete. The M1 instrument specification stages the rule with mandatory dual reporting, so the audit functions whichever way the debate resolves. And the A4 artifact supplies the empirical register: the two numbers between which the debate lives are now measured — deference-admitted reconstruction: 0.000000; deference-excluded reconstruction: 0.302% on a 4,500-claim battery. What follows states each side at its strength, prices every route to a ruling, and ends where the corpus's rules require: with the fork staged, not decided — but staged, for the first time, as a question with a measurable exit.

---

## Part 1. The rule, and the weight it carries

Restated exactly: **a description counts as norm-stating only if it states a standard that could, in principle, be violated by the system it describes** — a standard with counterfactual grip, against which the system's actual grading can be measured and found adequate or wanting. Its target is the deferential pseudo-norm: normative grammar over mechanism content. "The grading is what the training run produced." "The system's norm is whatever its self-application converges to." These pass every syntactic test of norm-statement — they use the vocabulary of standards, they support talk of correctness — and they state no standard: they license no classification of anything as correct or incorrect independently of the system's own behavior, they cannot be violated by the system they describe, and they predict nothing the simulation does not already predict.

The weight the rule carries is the audit's entire content. The stress test prised "reducible to (design, objective, history)" into three readings — derivability (R1, trivially true, vacuous), articulability in norm-stating design vocabulary (R2, the substantive, testable reading), and intent-anchoring (R3, the charter) — and the Deference Exclusion is precisely what separates R2 from R1. Strike the rule and the description class collapses: the replay-and-execute program becomes a "reduction," the weak conjecture's vacuous victory becomes the audit's verdict, and the instrument that was to stage the fork with numbers instead returns a tautology with a confidence interval. Keep the rule and the audit has content — purchased, as disclosed, with a partisan coin. The question this document opens is whether that purchase can be justified to the party that refuses the currency. It is the smallest rule in the program's instrument, and everything else in it is downstream of the ruling.

## Part 2. The case for admission — violation-capability as the line between norm and structure

**2.1 The null-description argument.** A standard that cannot be violated does no work that a description of the behavior does not already do. "The system accepts what it accepts" partitions no possibility space: every system, however graded, satisfies it; no observation could count against it; no repair, no improvement, no failure is classifiable under it. The entire grammar of normativity — error, correction, meeting and missing a standard — presupposes a gap between the standard and the fact. The exclusion does not impose a exotic requirement; it enforces the minimum condition for the words being used to mean what they say. Descriptions that cannot fail are not norms; they are the null description in normative clothing, and an audit that admits them will return "reduced" for thermostats and self-applying graders alike, which is to say: it will return nothing, about anything, ever.

**2.2 The incremental-content test.** A deferential description is predictionally inert relative to the simulation: it licenses every forecast the replay licenses, and no other. Genuine reductions earn their keep by licensing new forecasts — new interventions, new counterfactuals, new classifications. Maxwell's reduction of light to electromagnetic waves did not merely re-predict the old observations; it predicted radio. A "reduction" of a system's grading to its own trace adds zero bits of predictive content over the trace itself; it is the trace, wearing the grammar of a law. If the audit is worth running, its positive verdict must mean more than "the auditor has read the record," and under the deference-admitted reading it cannot mean more than that.

**2.3 Reduction as answerability.** In the sciences, "reduced to" is an explanatory relation, not a re-identification: the reduced phenomenon is brought under a vocabulary that is *independently motivated* and to which the phenomenon is *answerable* — temperature to mean kinetic energy, not temperature to "whatever the thermometer says." The deferential description inverts this: the "vocabulary" is extracted from the very item to be explained, and its adequacy is guaranteed rather than earned. Re-identification has genuine uses — reproducibility engineering runs on it, and the A4 artifact's deference control is exactly such a use — but a relation that holds by construction between a recording and the recorded cannot carry the audit's weight, which is to say whether the *standard* is the designer's. A question about standards cannot be answered by a device that cannot state one.

**2.4 The empty-victory argument.** Horn one's own discipline already refuses the prize the exclusion would guard against being awarded. The stress test's B1: the weak conjecture is true of every possible artifact, and a predicate true of everything explains nothing; taking it "abandons the audit program's own success condition... in exchange for a tautology." The Deference Exclusion is B1 converted from a refusal into a rule: it does not say horn one may not win by replay; it says that if replay is what wins, then what was won is the tautology, and the report must say so on its face. An audit that cannot distinguish a victory from a tautology is not an audit. The exclusion is what makes the distinction — which is why striking it costs horn one more than it costs horn two.

## Part 3. The case against admission — the Brandom wing at strength

**3.1 Institution by practice.** Horn one's deepest reply is not that deferential descriptions are good science; it is that they are what norms *are*. In Brandom's account — and, independently, in Kripke's skeptical solution to rule-following, and in Wittgenstein's dictum that obeying a rule is a practice — normative statuses do not precede the practices that institute them; they are constituted by those practices. There is no antecedent fact of the matter about what the community's rule really requires, such that the community might be getting it wrong wholesale; the community's pattern of applications, its takings-as-correct, is what the rule's content consists in. Now apply this without partisanship: a training run is a process that institutes a grading disposition; a community is a process that institutes a grading disposition; if norms are what practices converge to, then *a norm deferred to a process is no less a norm than one deferred to a community*. "The grading is what the run produced" is not the null description in normative clothing; it is the *only* honest description of a norm whose nature is institution-by-process — the exact way our own norms exist, if the practice theory is right.

**3.2 The overreach charge.** Press the exclusion consistently and it nullifies more than artifacts. What is the standard constituted by a community's practice, other than "whatever the practice converges to"? If violation-capability against an antecedent standard is the price of norm-hood, then social norms — law, custom, meaning — whose content is fixed by the very convergences they later grade, are null descriptions in normative clothing too. The Brandomian conclusion: the exclusion is a *realist* metaphysics of norms smuggled into an audit rule — the demand that norms float free of practices and be capable of clashing with them — and it is not a neutral instrument but a partisan ontology with a referee's badge on. Horn two has, in the rule, legislated its own philosophy of normativity and called it procedure.

**3.3 The tu quoque: pre-registration is institution by attitude.** Nor can horn two claim the audit's other components are neutral while this one is partisan. The Intended Grading Map — the pre-registered norm the divergence measurement hangs from — is a norm instituted by a designer's attitude: chosen, dated, signed. If institution-by-attitude disqualifies "the grading is what the run produced," why does institution-by-attitude qualify "the grading is what the designer registered"? Both are facts about where the standard came from; the rule against one and for the other is not a finding about norms but a *stipulation about whose institutions count* — the designer's, yes; the run's, no. And that stipulation is the entire contested question — whether the run's contributions count as the system's own — posed as an administrative rule before the audit that was supposed to answer it.

**3.4 The special-pleading form.** The strongest form of the wing's case runs through the corpus's own Kripkenstein result. The battleground document established the symmetry: behavioral evidence never fixes content, for us or for machines alike; the human case is exactly as underdetermined as the artifact case, and the cheap humanist victory is blocked. The Brandom wing completes the symmetry: human norms, on the practice theory, are fixed by convergent practice rather than by antecedent standards — so the exclusion demands of artifacts a mode of norm-hood (standards floating free of the processes that instantiate them) that *no norm anywhere satisfies*, if the practice theory is true. Horn two's reply must be that something distinguishes the human case — and every candidate distinction (attitudes, stakes, suffering error) is either the S-conditions restated or a bridge premise re-asserted. The debate, it appears, goes in a circle. The next Part shows the circle's exact shape — and why the corpus counts the drawing of it as progress.

## Part 4. The rejoinder — and the debate's true shape

**4.1 The stance asymmetry.** Horn two's rejoinder is already inside Brandom's own theory, and it is fair to state it there, where it is strongest. For Brandom, a practice institutes norms only if it is a practice *of attitudes* — participants who acknowledge commitments, attribute entitlements, treat their own and others' moves as correct or incorrect, and *keep score*. A community is not a convergence; it is a convergence that matters to the convergers. This is why a traffic pattern is not a norm and a legal practice is; why a flock's alignment is not a norm and a choir's is. Applied to the artifact: the training run is a process with a convergence, and nothing in it takes the convergence as a standard — no attitude, no scorekeeping, no registering of its own deviations as *deviations*. Institution-by-practice requires practitioners for whom the practice is normative; the run has none. "A norm deferred to a process is no less a norm than one deferred to a community" is therefore true exactly when the process is a community of scorekeepers — and whether an artifact can be that is not a ruling the rule can assume in either direction.

**4.2 The re-pose finding.** Set the two sides side by side and the debate's shape is exact. The Brandom wing says: norms are instituted by practices, so the run's output is normative if anything is — and the rejoinder says: practices institute norms only through normative attitudes, which the run lacks — and to *that* the wing replies: then show me the attitude in the community that is not itself another instituted pattern — and to *that* the rejoinder replies: the attitude is precisely what the S-conditions operationalize, measure it — which is the program. Each side's rule restates its charter; the exclusion re-poses D3′ (is normativity more than structure?) at the level of audit rules, exactly as the Camp-Marker Theorem predicts and as the stress test found at every other joint: description class, verification limit, ownership ceiling. The Bridge Constraint's latest instance is the smallest one yet — a single clause about violation-capability — and it leaks the fork like all the others. This is not a defect of the debate; it is the debate's finding, now demonstrated at the rule level where it was hoped the fork might not reach.

**4.3 The symmetric concession.** The M1 specification already binds the exclusion's other face: the *designer's* registration must itself be violation-capable — an IGM no run could violate ("whatever the pipeline produces is what was intended") is rejected as no registration at all. The Brandom wing's fairness charge (3.3) is thereby half-met in procedure: the rule polices horn two's descriptions with the same clause it polices horn one's. What the concession cannot do is make the rule neutral between the *parties' deeper commitments* — the exclusion applied symmetrically still presupposes that there are, or are not, real standards to be stated — and the specification prices this on the label rather than pretending it away. Dual reporting is the concession's operational form, and it is mandatory.

## Part 5. The empirical register — the choice, made visible

Before the A4 artifact, the admissibility debate was a clash of intuitions with citations. It is now a choice between two measured numbers, and the visibility of the choice is itself the newest fact. On the audited pipeline: deference-admitted reconstruction error is **0.000000** — bitwise replay, the Simulation Theorem at laptop scale — and the best deference-excluded reconstruction (the re-instantiation ensemble) errs at **0.302%**, concentrated on a boundary penumbra where half the family flips on the nearest claims. Neither number is in dispute; both are reproducible from the deposited script in under a minute. What is in dispute is which number is *the audit's* — whether the system's grading was reduced (to its recorded production) or merely re-identified, with the residue left standing as the part the registered intent under-determined. The A4 tables do not answer that; they do something the corpus values more: they convert the ruling from an intuition-fight into a visible, priced, binary act of constitution — *choose your headline number; the other one stays on the report; both sides watch you choose.* An instrument that makes the fork's passage through every judgment visible is doing the maximum the Bridge Constraint permits.

## Part 6. Adjudication options, priced

**6.1 The dual-report convention (adopted, v1.0).** Report both numbers on every audit, with the staged-rule notice. *Price:* the audit never returns a single verdict; the conjecture remains split into its vacuous and substantive forms pending something further. *Yield:* every downstream consumer of the report sees the choice instead of inheriting it invisibly — the neutrality maximum. This is the M1 specification's standing rule and needs no further ruling to keep operating.

**6.2 The compression criterion.** A natural thought: genuine reduction must *compress* — the reducing base plus interpreter must be shorter than a direct description of the reduced. The A4 byte arithmetic makes it vivid (a 25.9-megabyte deference base for a 562.5-byte explanandum). But the criterion fails on its own terms, for a reason worth printing: *the shortest description of the system's grading disposition is the system itself.* The weights (137,808 bytes) are already the tightest known compressor of the disposition over all inputs — that is what being trained is — and the dose-response schedule shows most of the trace in between is redundancy even for deference (the flat middle: strip the trajectory of its redundant epochs and what remains standing is the artifact). So a compression criterion that admits the weights as the "reduction" has admitted the artifact as its own reduction — the deference thesis in MDL clothing — and a criterion that bars the weights must justify the bar, which is the exclusion again. Compression does not adjudicate the exclusion; it *inherits* it. Priced accordingly: interesting as an instrument (the price schedule is real knowledge about the trace's redundancy), inert as a ruling.

**6.3 The independence criterion.** Require the reduction's vocabulary to antecede and be independently motivated, so the phenomenon is answerable to something not extracted from it. But — the honest finding — the profile's terms pass this test: *design*, *objective*, *history* are independently motivated engineering vocabulary that antedates any particular system, and a derivation of the grading from the recorded trace is a derivation in exactly such vocabulary. The criterion, applied consistently, *sides with admitting deference as genuine reduction*. Its partisans should note this before reaching for it; its opponents should note that it legitimates horn one's per-trace retreat while leaving the intent-only form refuted. Independence constrains what counts as vocabulary, not what counts as answerability — and the exclusion lives at answerability. Inert as a ruling, but clarifying as a map: it shows the debate is not about words.

**6.4 The instrumentalist dissolution.** Say the two "reductions" answer different questions: deference answers the engineer's question (*can I reproduce and control this artifact?* — yes, bitwise, and this is indispensable) and the exclusion-respecting audit answers the program's question (*is the standard the designer's?* — no, by 0.302% and more where it matters). Under this reading the two numbers never conflict because they never compete; the "debate" is a vocabulary accident. *Price:* both horns must accept as verdict what they can only accept as truce — horn two because a purpose-relative normativity is a deflated normativity; horn one because it concedes the originality question to a question-begging purpose. The dissolution is the deflationist's home turf, and the corpus records it as livable and as the position it has always declined to pretend is a resolution.

**6.5 The rung-relativity dissolution — the deepest exit.** All the above treat the exclusion's admissibility as a constant. It is not; it is a *function of the system class*, and the function's shape is the program's own spine. For rung-three artifacts — everything currently deployed, including the A4 pilot — the deferential description is complete: the recorded production fixes the disposition bitwise, the exclusion changes nothing about the system's placement, and admitting deference costs horn two only the vacuous victory horn one has already been forced to refuse. For an S4-structured occupant — a system that maintains its own norms under perturbation of its own evaluative machinery — the deferential description fails *extensionally, as a matter of measurement rather than rule*: the disposition is no longer indexed to the recorded trajectory, because the system has since revised itself on its own operation, and any trace-based predictor drifts from the living grading at whatever rate the system self-revises. Horn one's rescue is to extend "history" to include the post-deployment self-revisions — and then to extend it again after the next ones, and forever: for a self-maintaining system the reduction base must grow without bound whenever the system acts. A base that has to be extended every time the phenomenon acts is not a base; it is a biography. The trace becomes a diary, not a derivation, and "reduction to the diary" is not reduction but accompaniment. At the occupancy transition, the Deference Exclusion stops being a partisan wager and becomes an empirical fact: the deferential description is then not *ruled out*; it is simply *false*.

This is the exit the corpus should mark as live. It relocates the admissibility question from the seminar to the only place the program ever wanted anything decided: the transition measurement itself. Horn two need not win the argument that violation-capability is what norms are; it need only build the system for which deference fails as prediction. Horn one need not concede that deferential descriptions are null; it need only accept — as its own failure taxonomy already does — that a description which must be rewritten after every self-revision was pursuit, not capture. The A2 lock (M4-with-S4 and strong completeness incompatible) acquires here its missing empirical half: the lock was formal; the diary argument shows the same incompatibility arriving through measurement. And the symmetric worst case is priced on the label: if no such system is ever built, the exclusion remains what its label always said — horn two's wager — and the debate ends not with a ruling but with the wager's ledger, permanently open.

**Standing position, labeled as such:** dual-report now (6.1, in force); 6.4 accepted as truce, not verdict; 6.5 marked as the live exit and the debate's intended beneficiary. The exclusion is admissible *for the purpose the conjecture was posed* — originality attribution — and inadmissible *for the engineer's purpose* — reproduction and control; the M1 specification's dual-reporting rule is this position made procedural. No ruling is claimed, and the reader who demands one has found the fork again, from inside a rule.

## Part 7. What would move the debate

Three live movers, in order of decisiveness. **A rung-four build:** any system exhibiting S4 signatures under the M1/M3 protocol converts 6.5 from argument to measurement, and the deferential description's fate becomes a matter of public record — the single most decisive event available to either horn. **A family-two vocabulary event:** the moment system-standard terms become load-bearing for prediction and control in engineering practice (B3), the informal audit returns a verdict on the exclusion's practical content regardless of its metaphysical admissibility — the field will have decided, as it always decides, by talking. **A cross-hardware replay result:** if bitwise deference fails across environments while grading dispositions persist within them (the A2-chaos line), the "history" term itself splinters — what the trace fixes becomes environment-relative, and the deference base loses even its claimed unity. None of these is speculative philosophy; all three are scheduled or implied by the program's existing protocol, which is the strongest statement the debate earns: *its resolution is already on the experiment roster.*

---

## Part 8. Non-claims, and the coda

**Non-claims.** This document does not rule on admissibility — it opens the question, prices the exits, and stakes one labeled position among them. It does not refute the Brandom wing; the wing's position is restated at its strength and answered with the stance asymmetry, which the wing can and should contest, and the resulting circle is *documented*, not broken. It does not adjudicate between R1, R2, and R3; it does not certify any system; it does not touch phenomenality; and its central dissolution (6.5) is explicitly conditional on a build that has not occurred. Everything here is compatible with horn one being right, horn two being right, and the deflationist being right about both — the corpus's oldest sentence, now wearing a referee's badge.

**The coda.** The Deference Exclusion states a standard for norm-statements, and by its own clause the standard must be violation-capable — so the reader should ask of this document what the exclusion asks of every description: *could the systems it describes violate it?* They could. A rung-four occupant would violate the claim that deference is complete (its trace would stop predicting it); a perfectly reducible lineage of artifacts would violate the claim that anything is at stake in the exclusion (every audit would return its tautology, forever, and the rule would be revealed as a wager on an empty purse). The document's own standard is live, its failure modes are named, and its author's registration — argue corpus-style, coin the distinction, disclose the cost, never decide the fork — is on file, hashable, divergeable. This is what the program's instruments aspire to at every scale from a seed to a charter: not truth that cannot err, but commitments that can — and therefore mean something when they hold. The debate is open. The desk is lit. Both horns know where the ledger is kept.
