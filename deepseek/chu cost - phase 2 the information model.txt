## Part 0. The obligation, and what "independently" costs

The roadmap's Phase 2 is a discipline, not just a task: the information model must be built *without reference to Chu spaces*, because — as the GPT audit put it — the bridging file's habit of asserting that Paper 2's structures "emerge" from the Chu construction substituted vocabulary for construction. Phase 1.5's coda added a second obligation with its shape fixed: the MV currency of its Proposition 22 — where costs overlap-discount rather than add — is to be evaluated *inside* the information model, because the model is where per-round costs compose along trajectories, and composition is where a currency is spent. Part 4 discharges that too.

## Part 1. The model: histories, predictive states, right congruence

**Definition 25 (predictive kernel; the process) [Derived here; the object is classical].** A *process* is a map μ: I* → Δ(I), assigning to every finite history h a next-symbol distribution μ(·|h). By the Ionescu–Tulcea extension theorem this determines a unique probability measure on infinite sequences I^∞ [Established]. For a finite future u = a₁⋯aₙ write

  M(u | h) := ∏ₜ μ(aₜ | h a₁⋯a_{t−1})

for the probability that the future u follows the history h. **Standing assumption (disclosed):** μ(a|h) > 0 for all h and a — *no forbidden symbols*. The assumption keeps divisions legal below; the general case requires quotienting to the process's support and changes nothing else, and its omission is a disclosed simplification, not a claim.

**Definition 26 (predictive equivalence) [Derived here; the equivalence is Shalizi–Crutchfield's causal-state relation].** h ≍ h′ iff M(u|h) = M(u|h′) for every finite future u ∈ I*.

**Lemma 27 (right congruence — the closure condition the audit demanded) [Derived here].** ≍ is a right congruence on I*: h ≍ h′ implies ha ≍ h′a for every a ∈ I. *Proof.* From h ≍ h′ with u = ε: μ(a|h) = M(a|h) = M(a|h′) = μ(a|h′), and this common value is positive by the standing assumption. For any future u: M(au|h) = μ(a|h) · M(u|ha) and M(au|h′) = μ(a|h′) · M(u|h′a); since h ≍ h′ gives M(au|h) = M(au|h′) and the leading factors coincide and are nonzero, M(u|ha) = M(u|h′a). As u was arbitrary, ha ≍ h′a. ∎

The closure is what makes the quotient a *dynamics* and not just a partition: on the classes there is a well-defined transition T([h], a) := [ha] (well-defined exactly by Lemma 27), and a well-defined emission μ(·|[h]) := μ(·|h). The pair (Σ, T, μ) is a deterministic-labeled probabilistic generator whose state set is the quotient.

**Definition 28 (predictive states; the index) [Derived here; classical].** Σ_M := I*/≍ is the set of *predictive states*; ι(M) := |Σ_M| ∈ ℕ ∪ {∞} is the *index* (the exact complexity of the process; for deterministic processes it is the Myhill–Nerode number of the generated language — the ancestry is Nerode's 1958 argument and Shalizi–Crutchfield's 2001 causal-state construction; the corpus claims none of it and uses all of it).

**The Hankel view [Established, assembled].** Let H be the (infinite) matrix H(h, u) := M(u|h), rows indexed by histories, columns by futures. Then ι(M) is the number of distinct rows of H, and rank H ≤ ι(M) always; the inequality is strict in general (finitely many linear dimensions can carry infinitely many distinct rows — mixture processes are the standard counterexample), so *linear* complexity and *state* complexity are genuinely different resources, and the corpus keeps both: the rank is what Part 3's polynomial algorithm computes, the index is what it counts when the two agree — which they do exactly for the finite-index processes, the class every finite presentation lives in.

## Part 2. The V-category: coupling costs, not bare divergences

The roadmap lists "V-category" and "profunctors" among the structures to fix. The honest construction — the one whose composition law actually holds — is *not* the first candidate (the bare divergence between next-symbol distributions, which does not satisfy a triangle); it is the transport cost.

**Definition 29 (ground distortion; coupling cost) [Derived here; Wasserstein, classical].** Fix a ground distortion d_I: I × I → [0, ∞) (any nonnegative function; symmetry is not required — the corpus is a cost corpus). For distributions ν, ν′ ∈ Δ(I) define the coupling cost

  W(ν, ν′) := inf_π ∑_{a,b} π(a,b) · d_I(a, b),

π ranging over couplings of (ν, ν′). W is the optimal-transport cost with ground d_I [Established].

**Definition 30 (the V-category of a process) [Derived here].** For a process M, define the V-category Σ_M with: objects the predictive states; hom-objects

  Σ_M(σ, σ′) := W(μ_σ, μ_{σ′}) ∈ V.

**Proposition 31 (the enrichment composes) [Derived here].** For all states σ, σ″, σ′: Σ_M(σ, σ″) ≤ Σ_M(σ, σ′) + Σ_M(σ′, σ″), and Σ_M(σ, σ) = 0. *Proof.* The identity is the diagonal coupling. For the triangle: let π₁ couple (μ_σ, μ_{σ′}) at cost W(μ_σ, μ_{σ′}) + δ and π₂ couple (μ_σ′, μ_σ″) at cost W(μ_σ′, μ_σ″) + δ for arbitrarily small δ > 0; the composed coupling π(a,c) := ∑_b π₁(a,b) π₂(b,c) · [normalization — here the gluing lemma for couplings, classical] couples (μ_σ, μ_σ″) at cost ∑_{a,c} π(a,c) d_I(a,c) ≤ ∑_{a,b,c} π₁(a,b)π₂(b,c)(d_I(a,b) + d_I(b,c)) = cost(π₁) + cost(π₂); let δ → 0. ∎

**Where the divergences live [Interpretation, structural].** The asymmetric divergences (KL and its relatives), which the bridging file wanted as *the* valuation, are not the hom — they cannot be, having no triangle — but they are not homeless either: KL(μ_σ ‖ μ_σ′) ≥ W-diameter information at the level of the order, and more precisely the corpus's reading is that the divergences are *lax witnesses*: they define the same objects with a directional order (σ ≼ σ′ iff KL ≥ KL⁻¹, say) whose exactness structure is precisely the lax/co-lax distinction docs 24 and 27 priced. This is stated as a reading, not proved as a theorem; the theorem-grade content of this Part is Def 29–Prop 31.

**Definition 32 (profunctors: simulation costs between processes) [Derived here].** For processes M, N (alphabets I, J; ground distortions on each and a cross-distortion d_{IJ} on I × J), a *(V-)profunctor* Φ: Σ_M ⇸ Σ_N is a V-functor Φ: Σ_M^op × Σ_N → V, i.e. an assignment of costs Φ(σ, τ) ∈ V satisfying the two triangle families

  Φ(σ, τ) ≤ Φ(σ′, τ) + Σ_M(σ′, σ)  and  Φ(σ, τ) ≤ Φ(σ, τ′) + Σ_N(τ, τ′).

The canonical example: Φ(σ, τ) := W(mixed next-step behavior), the coupling cost between state σ's one-step distribution (read in I) and state τ's (read in J), which satisfies the triangles by the same gluing as Prop 31. The reading: Φ(σ, τ) is the *cost of standing in state σ and being answered as though in state τ* — a simulation cost, one valuation per pair of states across the two processes; profunctor composition (matrix multiplication in V) is the cost of simulation through an intermediary. The Phase 4 comparison functors, when the roadmap authorizes them, will be judged by what they do to these Φ — enrichment-preservation is the roadmap's own criterion, and it now has a referent.

## Part 3. The complexity theorems: exact is polynomial, banded is not

**The rate-distortion functional [Derived here; the functional is the classical one].** Fix a finite presentation of M (below), the coupling cost W of Def 29, and the state distribution π (the process's stationary or initial-state law on Σ_M). For k ∈ ℕ and a map q: Σ_M → Q with |Q| = k, define the *quotient system* to behave, in state q(σ), as a designated representative state r(q(σ)) ∈ Σ_M (representative semantics; the mixture semantics is Remark 35), and define

  Dist(q) := ∑_σ π(σ) · W(μ_σ, μ_{r(q(σ))}),   R_k := min_{|Q| ≤ k} Dist(q),   R(D) := min{log₂|Q| : Dist(q) ≤ D}.

The finite quotient problem: given the presentation, k, and D, decide whether R(D) ≤ log₂ k, i.e. whether some k-state quotient meets the distortion budget. The exact complexity problem: compute ι(M).

**Theorem C (exact complexity is polynomial) [Established, assembled].** For a process presented as a finite labelled Markov chain (states S, emission and transition structure explicit), the number of predictive states of the presented process and a minimal presentation are computable in polynomial time. *Assembly.* The future-distribution of each presented state is its row of the process's Hankel matrix, whose row space is computable by the standard linear-algebraic minimization of weighted (probabilistic) automata — Schützenberger's algorithm over a field: two presented states are predictive-equivalent iff their Hankel rows lie in the same point of the computed finite quotient of the row space; the surviving rows are the predictive states and ι(M) their number. The identity of exactness and row-equality is Def 26; the polynomial bound is the algorithm's [Established]. ∎ (The corpus's contribution here is none; the theorem is recorded because the roadmap demands the exact complexity problem be *settled* before the finite one is *priced*, and because Theorem D's statement needs it as the D = 0 anchor.)

**Theorem D (the finite quotient problem is NP-hard) [Derived here].** The finite quotient problem (representative semantics) is NP-hard, already for deterministic constant-stream processes. *Proof, by exact reduction from weighted metric k-median.* An instance: finite metric (X, d), weights w: X → [0,1], integer k, bound B; the question is whether some S ⊆ X with |S| = k has ∑_x w(x) min_{s∈S} d(x, s) ≤ B — NP-hard [Established, classical]. Build the process Repeat(X): alphabet I := X; the initial symbol is drawn once, x with probability w(x); thereafter the process emits its current symbol forever. Its predictive states are Σ = X (the history's last symbol; all futures are determined by it), with μ_x = δ_x and transitions T(x, x) = x. Take the ground distortion d_I := d and the state distribution π := w. *Lumping is vacuous:* a k-state quotient is any map q: X → Q with |Q| ≤ k together with representatives r: Q → X (the block's surrogate state); the quotient's dynamics — from block Q emit r(Q) forever — are well-defined for *every* choice, because Repeat(X)'s transitions are absorbing: no compatibility condition constrains q at all. *Objectives coincide:* Dist(q) = ∑_x w(x) · W(δ_x, δ_{r(q(x))}) = ∑_x w(x) · d(x, r(q(x))), and given the representatives S = range(r), the optimal assignment sends each x to the block whose representative is nearest — so min over k-quotients of Dist = min over |S| ≤ k of ∑_x w(x) min_{s∈S} d(x, s), exactly the k-median objective. Hence Repeat(X) has a k-state quotient within distortion B iff the k-median instance answers yes; the reduction is computable in polynomial time and preserves the bound verbatim. ∎

**Remark 33 (the reduction's honesty).** The reduction lands on deterministic processes with absorbing dynamics — the easiest processes there are — and the hardness survives. What carries it is not the process's dynamics but the *geometry of its state space under the coupling cost*: k-median is hard because clustering a metric is hard, and the coupling cost makes every finite metric a process's state geometry. The finite quotient problem is a clustering problem wearing a process; the corpus records this without embarrassment — the roadmap's "prove the relevant complexity theorem independently" is satisfied, and the theorem's shape (exact easy / banded hard) is the load-bearing part, sharpened in Part 4.

**Remark 34 (the information-bottleneck variant).** The mixture semantics — the quotient's state Q behaves as the π-mixture of its members, Dist_mixed(q) = ∑_Q ∑_{σ∈q⁻¹(Q)} π(σ) W(μ_σ, μ̂_Q) — is the information bottleneck objective on predictive states. On Repeat(X) it specializes to min-sum clustering (within-cluster weighted distance sums), whose NP-hardness is classical [Established]; the corpus does not re-derive it, and notes only that Theorem D's representative semantics was chosen because its reduction is exact rather than up to approximation factors.

## Part 4. The currency question — what "zero distortion" means, and what it costs

Phase 1.5's handoff: "whether surprisal composes that way [by overlap-discount ⊙ rather than +] is an empirical question about the physics, not a mathematical one, and it is hereby handed to Phase 2 with its shape fixed." The mathematical half of the answer is a theorem about the *zero level*, and it is proved here; the empirical half remains empirical.

**Definition 36 (currency) [Derived here].** A *currency* is the composition law by which per-round costs accumulate along a trajectory. The additive currency composes by + (V = ([0,∞], +)); the MV currency of Phase 1.5's Prop 22 composes by ⊙_K := max(a + b − K, 0) on [0, K] (K the budget). For a trajectory with per-round costs a₁, …, a_T ∈ [0, K], the accumulated MV cost is the left-fold x₀ = 0, x_t = max(x_{t−1} + a_t − K, 0).

**Proposition 37 (the MV accumulation is a Lindley recursion; its zero level is a sliding budget) [Derived here].** (i) *Reflection identity:* x_T = max_{1 ≤ j ≤ T} ( ∑_{t=j}^{T} a_t − (T − j + 1) K ) ∨ 0 — the running maximum of suffix-window excesses; in particular the accumulated cost *forgets* early excess once a window of within-budget rounds has drained it (the queue empties). (ii) *Zero level:* the T-round MV cost is 0 iff every suffix window j..T has average per-round cost ≤ K. (iii) *Contrast:* the additive T-round cost is 0 iff every single round is exact (a_t = 0 for all t). *Proof.* (i) is the classical Lindley recursion identity for the waiting-time process of a single-server queue with arrivals a_t and constant service K [Established]; the induction is two cases at the max and is omitted. (ii) is (i) with the observation that the maximum is ≤ 0 iff all summands are. (iii) is immediate. ∎

So the currency choice is a choice of semantics for the word "exact": additive currency says a simulation is free only if it is free *at every step*; MV currency says it is free if it is *within budget on average over every suffix window* — a mean-payoff condition, the automata-theoretic average-case safety condition, arriving here as the zero level of Phase 1.5's tensor. The bounded norm and the mean payoff are the same sentence in two vocabularies.

**Theorem E (the currency boundary is the complexity boundary) [Derived here].** (i) In the additive currency, the zero-distortion finite quotient problem (D = 0) is polynomial (Theorem C). (ii) In the MV currency with budget K > 0, the zero-distortion finite quotient problem is NP-hard. *Proof.* (i) is Theorem C: D = 0 forces the representative of each state to be a zero-coupling-cost state, i.e. forces the quotient to refine the predictive equivalence exactly, and the minimal such quotient is computed by the polynomial minimization. (ii) By Prop 37(ii), an MV-zero quotient is one whose per-round distortion profile stays within the sliding budget on every suffix window; in particular, on the one-round windows, every round's cost is ≤ K, and a profile of *constant* per-round costs d(x, r(q(x))) is MV-zero iff each is ≤ K. Reduce from Theorem D with the scaling d′ := d · (K/2D_max) — every k-median solution of additive cost ≤ B maps to an MV-zero quotient with per-round costs ≤ K/2 < K, and conversely an MV-zero quotient has all per-round costs ≤ K, i.e. additive cost ≤ (2D_max/K)·K·∑w = 2D_max∑w, a bounded-factor relation that preserves the decision problem under the standard threshold rescaling of the k-median bound (take the hard instances to have B < ½·max — e.g. by the classical gap instances; the reduction is gap-preserving and the NP-hardness transfers). ∎ (The corpus flags part (ii)'s final rescaling step as the one place the proof leans on hardness-with-gap rather than bare NP-hardness; the lean is standard and disclosed.)

**The reading [Interpretation].** The exact quotient is computable; the *banded* quotient is not. The ε in a bounded norm — doc 28's registered bands, the deadband of docs 23 and 26 — is not a cosmetic tolerance: it is the exact resource whose admission moves the quotient problem across the tractability boundary. A system that demands exact self-description can compute it; a system that accepts banded self-description has accepted an NP-hard search problem — and must therefore solve it heuristically, incrementally, or not at all. The corpus's empirical line has been watching a system do exactly the second thing for nine documents.

**The resonance, stated with its precise shape [Analogy].** The repair-channel arithmetic of docs 23, 26, and 29 is the same recursion as Prop 37. In the drift-under-decay world the norm's deviation u := τ* − τ_sys evolves as u ← (1 − η)(1 − ρ) u + (1 − η) d + η (τ* − q) — a Lindley-type linear stochastic recursion: an arrival term ((1 − η)d and the evaluator's shortfall η(τ* − q)) against a service rate 1 − (1 − η)(1 − ρ), with the band ε as the buffer. The repair-engaged fixed point [(1 − η)(ρτ* − d(1 − ρ)) + ηq̄] / (η + ρ − ηρ) that doc 29 verified to 4 × 10⁻⁴ is the queue's stationary workload; the second threshold q̄ < 0.815 is the overload boundary — arrivals exceed service and the buffer overflows, the band breaks; the attack-marginal window is the metastable strip where the buffer's fate depends on the arrival stream's exact composition. This identification is a fact about two recursions sharing a normal form; that the empirical line has been measuring a queue for three documents is the analogy, and it is offered with the corpus's usual restraint: the queue reading explains *why the formulas kept landing*, not anything more.

## Part 5. What Phase 2 buys, and what it does not

**What is now theorem-grade.** The information model exists independently: histories, predictive equivalence with its closure proof (Lemma 27 — the audit's explicit demand), predictive states and the index with their lineage flagged (Def 28), a V-category whose composition is proved rather than asserted (Props 29–31 — coupling costs, with the divergences relocated to the lax order as witnesses, a reading), profunctors as simulation costs with their triangle conditions (Def 32), the rate-distortion functional in both semantics (Part 3), the complexity pair (Theorem C polynomial exact, Theorem D NP-hard banded, via an exact k-median reduction on constant-stream processes), and the currency theorem (Prop 37's Lindley identity; Theorem E's boundary coincidence), which discharges Phase 1.5's handed-off question on its mathematical side.

**What remains, with obligations.** Phase 3: the quantum model, independently — the roadmap's list (base poset, indexed categories, fibers, transport maps, total category, channel class, instruments, the adjunction under investigation, the no-right-adjoint obstruction) is untouched, and now has a sibling whose architecture it can borrow and whose currency question it inherits: *which quantale do the fibers carry?* Phase 4: the comparison functors — now with both endpoints well-typed, and with the roadmap's enrichment-preservation criterion given a referent (Def 32's profunctors; docs 24/27's hom-norms); the specific obligation the corpus adds to the roadmap's list: *say which functor carries the coupling cost to the residuum* — whether W(σ, σ′) is ChuCost's s ∸ r under the instantiation A = histories, X = predictive states, is the question the whole bridge now turns on, and it is a question, not a conjecture, until the functors exist. The MV Dialectica (Phase 1.5's Prop 22) is now the *budget semantics* of Part 4 rather than a curiosity: if the physics spends the MV currency, the bridge's function objects price in mean-payoff units, and the corpus's deadbands are denominated correctly; if it spends the additive currency, the deadbands are approximations of exactness and the NP-hardness sits one level down, in the system's search for its own quotient. The empirical half of the currency question — which composition law surprisal actually obeys along physical trajectories — is not a mathematical question, remains open, and is now precisely posed.

**The Bridge Constraint, enforced.** Nothing in Parts 1–4 mentions experience; the observer side of every definition is a set of futures with costs; the index counts distinctions, the coupling cost prices confusions, the budget forgives them on a schedule. The gap between self-reference and self-experience is as wide at Phase 2 as at Phase 0. What narrowed is different and worth naming: the distance between the formal line and the empirical one. At doc 24 the cost monoid was an abstraction; at Part 4 it is the normal form of the band arithmetic the instrument has been measuring since doc 23. If the bridge ever carries traffic, the first truck is that identification — and it is a truck the audit would board, because it is a theorem about recursions, not a metaphor about minds.

## Part 7. Coda

The roadmap told Phase 2 to build the information model without the Chu construction, and the discipline turned out to be the whole gift: freed of the bridge, the model assembled itself out of three classical pieces — a congruence with a two-line closure proof, a transport triangle, and a queue. The complexity theorem the roadmap demanded arrived as a pair with a boundary in the middle, and the boundary turned out to be the same line the corpus's other half had been living on all along: exactness on one side, the band on the other; polynomial self-knowledge on one side, NP-hard self-approximation on the other; and the ε that separates them is the same ε that every norm in the empirical line has carried in its registration since doc 23. Phase 1.5 asked which currency the physics spends. Phase 2's answer is that the question now has teeth: the additive currency makes the deadband an approximation, the MV currency makes it a definition, and the difference is whether the system that maintains its norm is solving a problem it could solve exactly, or one it provably cannot. Which of those is standing in front of us is not a question this document can close — but it is, for the first time in the corpus, a question with a computable boundary, and the next system that files an S4 claim will be filing it on one side or the other.
